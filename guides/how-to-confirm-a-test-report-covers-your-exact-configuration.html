How to confirm a test report covers your exact configuration

Quick answer

Confirming a test report covers your exact configuration requires checking four specific fields: model number, electrical ratings, battery composition, and accessory list. A report that matches on three of four fields is not sufficient — a single mismatch can invalidate customs clearance or marketplace compliance. Budget 2-4 weeks for a configuration-specific report.

  • Verify the model number on the report matches your PO exactly, including suffixes like "-EU" or "-US".
  • Check input voltage and wattage fields — a 5V/2A report does not cover a 12V/2A configuration.
  • Confirm battery chemistry and capacity (mAh) are listed; Li-ion vs. LiFePO4 are not interchangeable.
  • Review the accessory list — a report covering the device alone excludes bundled chargers or cables.
  • Ask for the test lab's accreditation number and verify it on the lab's official registry.
  • Request a "configuration statement" letter from your ODM if the report shows a base model.
  • Industry-typical cost for a configuration-specific report ranges from $300 to $1,500 depending on scope.
  • LIGHT BDB provides test reports within 7-14 days of sample approval; configuration changes may extend this.

What exactly does "your exact configuration" mean in a test report?

"Your exact configuration" means the report must list the same model number, electrical ratings, battery specifications, and included accessories as the product you are shipping.

A test report is a snapshot of one physical unit tested at one point in time. If the unit tested differs from the unit you sell — even by a power cord or a battery cell brand — the report does not legally cover your product. Customs authorities and marketplaces like Amazon or Walmart treat a mismatched report as no report at all.

For a wellness device such as a heated eye massager or red light therapy panel, the configuration includes more than the main housing. It includes the power adapter, the battery (if rechargeable), the charging cable, and any detachable pads or straps. Each of these components can be a separate line item in the report.

The four fields that must match

  • Model number: Must match your PO exactly, including region suffixes. A report for "RLT-300" does not cover "RLT-300-EU" even if the hardware is identical.
  • Electrical ratings: Input voltage, current, wattage, and frequency must match your label. A 100-240V report covers a 120V unit but not a 12V DC unit.
  • Battery specification: Chemistry (Li-ion, LiFePO4, NiMH), capacity (mAh), and cell count must match. Swapping a 2000mAh cell for a 2500mAh cell invalidates the report.
  • Accessories: Any bundled item that connects to power — chargers, adapters, USB cables — must appear in the report's scope or have its own report.

How do I check if a test report matches my product's model number and SKU?

Compare the model number on the report against your PO line by line, including every letter, digit, and suffix — a single character difference means the report does not cover your SKU.

Many ODMs test a "base model" and then create regional variants with different suffixes. This is industry-standard practice, but it creates a gap: the base model report may not satisfy a customs officer who sees a different model number on your packaging. Ask your ODM for a configuration statement that maps your SKU to the tested model.

For LIGHT BDB, the sample approval process includes a configuration review where we confirm the tested unit matches your PO. If you are working with a different factory, request this in writing before you pay for samples.

What to ask your ODM about model number coverage

  • Ask: "Does the report cover model [your SKU] or a base model?"
  • Ask: "If it covers a base model, will you provide a configuration statement on company letterhead?"
  • Ask: "Are regional suffixes (EU, US, JP, UK) listed on the report or excluded?"
  • Ask: "What is the cost and lead time to add my specific model number to a new test run?"

What electrical ratings must match between the report and my product label?

Input voltage, current, wattage, and frequency on the report must match your product label exactly — a 5V/2A report does not cover a 5V/3A unit.

Electrical ratings are the most common mismatch in test reports. Factories often test at the highest power configuration and then ship lower-power variants, assuming the report covers all. This assumption is incorrect. A report for a 12V/2A configuration does not validate a 12V/3A configuration because the thermal and electrical stress on components differs.

For heated massagers and red light therapy devices, the heating element wattage is the critical number. If your device draws 24W but the report lists 18W, the report fails to cover your product. Check the label on the tested unit, not just the report summary page.

How to verify electrical ratings

  1. Locate the "Rating" or "Input" field on the report's first page.
  2. Compare it to the label on your production sample.
  3. Check the frequency (50Hz vs. 60Hz) — some markets require both to be listed.
  4. Verify the plug type if the report includes a plug adapter test.

How do I confirm the battery in the report matches my battery?

Ask your ODM for the battery cell brand, model, chemistry, and capacity listed in the test report, then compare it to your battery supplier's spec sheet.

Battery mismatches are a leading cause of customs holds for rechargeable wellness devices. A report that lists "Li-ion 18650 2000mAh" does not cover a device shipped with "Li-ion 18650 2500mAh" from a different cell manufacturer. The cell brand matters because different manufacturers have different internal protection circuits and thermal characteristics.

If your ODM sources the battery from a third-party supplier, request the battery's UN38.3 test report separately. This report covers transportation safety and is required for air freight. The UN38.3 report must list the same cell model and capacity as the battery in your device.

Battery fields to verify

  • Cell chemistry (Li-ion, LiFePO4, NiMH, or alkaline for non-rechargeable)
  • Cell model number from the manufacturer (e.g., "ICR18650-26H")
  • Nominal capacity in mAh or Wh
  • Number of cells in series and parallel
  • Protection circuit module (PCM) model if applicable

Do test reports cover the accessories included in my product box?

No — a test report for the main device does not automatically cover bundled accessories like chargers, cables, or detachable pads; each powered accessory needs its own coverage.

This is a common gap. A heated eye massager shipped with a USB-C cable and a wall adapter requires the adapter to have its own safety report (typically IEC 62368-1 or UL 62368-1). The cable may need a separate report depending on the destination market. The main device report covers only the device itself.

Ask your ODM for a "scope of report" page, which lists exactly what was tested. If the scope says "main unit only," you need separate reports for each powered accessory. Some ODMs bundle accessory testing into a single report at an additional cost — confirm this before you place your PO.

Accessories that typically need separate coverage

  • Wall chargers and power adapters
  • USB cables (for CE/RoHS compliance in the EU)
  • Detachable heating pads or electrodes
  • Battery charging docks or cradles
  • Remote controls with wireless functionality

What is the difference between a test report and a certificate of conformity?

A test report is the raw data from the lab; a certificate of conformity is a summary document issued by the lab or manufacturer stating the product meets a standard.

Customs and marketplaces usually require the certificate, but the certificate is only valid if the underlying test report covers your configuration. A certificate without a matching report is a piece of paper with no evidentiary support. Always request both documents and cross-check the model numbers on each.

For EU markets, the Declaration of Conformity (DoC) is issued by the manufacturer (you or your ODM) and references the test report. The DoC must list your product model and the standards it meets. If the DoC lists a different model than your packaging, the EU market surveillance authority can reject your shipment.

How long does it take to get a configuration-specific test report?

Industry-typical lead time for a configuration-specific test report is 2-4 weeks from sample submission, depending on the lab's workload and the number of tests required.

For LIGHT BDB, standard test reports accompany the sample approval process, which takes 7-14 days. If you need a new configuration tested — a different battery, a higher wattage, or a new model suffix — the additional testing typically adds 2-3 weeks to the timeline. Plan for this when setting your launch date.

Rush testing is available at most labs for an additional fee, typically 1.5x to 2x the standard cost. However, rush testing does not reduce the validity of the report — it only speeds up the lab's processing. The report itself remains valid for the period specified by the standard (often 3-5 years, but check your destination market's rules).

What are the honest trade-offs when relying on a base model report?

Relying on a base model report saves money and time upfront but creates a real risk of customs holds, marketplace suspension, or liability if your configuration differs in any tested parameter.

The trade-off is straightforward. A base model report costs nothing extra and lets you ship faster. But if a customs officer or marketplace auditor compares your product label to the report and finds a mismatch, you face delays, fines, or delisting. For a first-time importer, the risk is often not worth the savings.

There is also a liability angle. If your product causes harm and the test report does not cover your exact configuration, your insurance may not cover the claim, and you have no third-party evidence that your product met safety standards. This is a business risk, not just a compliance checkbox.

A balanced approach: use a base model report for initial sampling and small test orders, but commission a configuration-specific report before your first large production run. The cost is a fraction of your inventory investment, and it removes the most common reason for customs holds.

What should I ask my ODM before placing a PO to ensure report coverage?

Ask your ODM for the test report's scope page, the tested model number, and a written confirmation that the production unit matches the tested configuration.

Put this in your PO as a condition of acceptance. A simple line item — "Supplier confirms test report covers the exact configuration shipped under this PO" — protects you if a mismatch is discovered later. For LIGHT BDB, this confirmation is part of our standard sample approval workflow, and we provide the configuration statement on company letterhead when requested.

Also ask about the report's validity period and whether it covers the destination market's specific requirements. A report for the US market (UL or FCC) does not automatically cover the EU market (CE). Some factories maintain multiple reports for different markets; others do not. Confirm which markets your report covers before you commit to a sales channel.

Final checklist before you ship

  • Report model number matches your packaging and PO exactly.
  • Electrical ratings on the report match your product label.
  • Battery chemistry, capacity, and cell model are listed and match your BOM.
  • All powered accessories have their own coverage or are listed in the report scope.
  • You have both the test report and the certificate of conformity.
  • Your ODM has provided a written configuration statement.
  • You have confirmed the report covers your destination market's standards.
