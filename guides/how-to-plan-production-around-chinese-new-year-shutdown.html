How to Plan Production Around the Chinese New Year Shutdown

Why does the Chinese New Year shutdown affect my production timeline?

The CNY shutdown affects your timeline because factories close completely for 2–4 weeks, and the weeks immediately before and after the holiday suffer from massive labor and logistics bottlenecks. This is not a single day off; it is an annual migration where a large portion of China's manufacturing workforce travels back to their hometowns.

In the two weeks leading up to CNY, worker attendance drops steadily as employees take extended leave. During the CNY week itself, almost all factory floors are silent. In the two weeks following the holiday, production ramps up slowly as workers return, new staff are trained, and supply chains spool back to full capacity. For a brand owner, a PO placed in late January can easily miss the holiday window and slip to a late March or early April shipment.

For a wellness device like an eye massager or a red light therapy panel, the impact is felt across three distinct phases:

  • Component bottleneck: PCBA, injection-molded shells, and motors are manufactured upstream. Their lead times extend first.
  • Assembly bottleneck: Final finished-product assembly lines require the most manual labor, making them the hardest hit.
  • Logistics bottleneck: Freight forwarders, customs brokers, and trucking companies all operate on reduced staffing and capacity.

LIGHT BDB structurally mitigates these risks better than most ODMs. With a 3,000 sqm finished-product assembly plant in Shenzhen and a 5,000+ pcs/day overflow line in Yangon, Myanmar, we can shift overflow production outside China during peak Chinese holiday periods. However, this overflow capacity is strictly limited, so it is a contingency buffer, not a blank check for last-minute orders.

What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect for heated eye massagers?

MOQs vary by complexity, but a first-time buyer should expect to commit to at least 100–500 units for a standard heated eye massager with private-label branding.

LIGHT BDB segments its MOQs into three specific tiers, each serving a different market-entry strategy. Understanding where your product fits in these tiers is critical when planning around CNY, as it determines how much leverage you have to secure a production slot before the factory shutdown.

MOQ Tier Unit Range Best For Recommended Pre-CNY Order Date
Lite 100–499 units Sampling, testing keywords, limited drops 8 weeks before CNY week
Slim 500–999 units Most popular tier for standard launches 10 weeks before CNY week
Pro 1,000+ units Best unit cost, full customization, Amazon FBA 12+ weeks before CNY week (or Q4 of prior year)

If you are a first-time importer, the Slim tier (500-999 units) is often the sweet spot. It gives you enough inventory to validate a retail listing without tying up excessive working capital. Critically, it also places you in a better position to negotiate a production slot before the CNY cutoff.

Lite tier orders are great for testing, but they are often queued behind larger Pro tier orders when the factory is rushing to clear its books before the holiday. If your goal is a specific launch date, aim for at least the Slim tier before CNY.

How far in advance should I place my PO before the shutdown?

Place your PO at least 8–10 weeks before the CNY week to ensure your goods clear FOB Shenzhen before the factory gates close.

LIGHT BDB's standard lead time is 4–8 weeks from the receipt of your deposit to FOB Shenzhen. This is the metric you must anchor to. If CNY falls in early February, and you add a 2–3 week safety buffer to that 4–8 week window, your ideal PO placement window is late November to early December of the preceding year.

Here is the minimum planning checklist for a pre-CNY shipment:

  • Deposit payment (T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight): Your 30% deposit must be wired and confirmed before the production queue freezes.
  • Final sample approval: LIGHT BDB's sample turnaround is 7 days. You must have your pre-production sample signed off before CNY, because design teams in Hangzhou and Hamburg will also be on holiday.
  • Packaging artwork sign-off: Folding cartons, foam inserts, and manuals are printed in advance. If your artwork isn't confirmed, your packaging supplier cannot pre-produce materials.
  • Nominated forwarder booking: Lock in your freight booking 3–4 weeks before CNY. Air freight and ocean freight prices spike dramatically in the final two weeks before the holiday.

LIGHT BDB works with logistics partners on an FOB Shenzhen basis with your nominated forwarder. That means once your goods are loaded at the Shenzhen port, the risk shifts to you. Planning your forwarder booking early is as critical as planning the factory production itself.

What is the typical lead time for a wellness device ODM, and how does CNY extend it?

The typical lead time for a wellness device ODM is 4–8 weeks, but a CNY shutdown can extend this to 10–14 weeks without proper planning.

This extension is not just about the 2–4 weeks the factory is closed. It is about the weeks of reduced output on either side. In the two weeks leading up to CNY, production efficiency drops by 30–40% as workers depart. In the first two weeks after CNY, efficiency is similarly low as new workers are onboarded and trained.

LIGHT BDB's vertical integration helps compress standard lead times. The Panyu plant (10,000+ sqm, 600+ staff) produces auto-grade inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils, and performs SMT on PCBA. The Shenzhen facility (3,000 sqm) handles final finished-product assembly. This means your product does not wait on third-party component suppliers as much as it would with a purely assembly-based ODM. The Yangon line serves as an overflow valve for scaling past 5,000 pieces per day when China is at full capacity.

Here is how a CNY shutdown realistically stretches a 6-week lead time SKU:

  • Week 1-2: Component preparation and PCBA manufacturing in Panyu.
  • Week 3-4: Final assembly in Shenzhen; production finishes just before CNY.
  • Week 5-6: Quality control, boxing, and loading at FOB Shenzhen.

If that 6-week window overlaps CNY, the timeline shifts dramatically. Component prep happens, the factory closes, assembly moves to Week 7-8, and FOB misses the pre-holiday sailing. The safe window pushes out to Week 10-12.

How can I use the shutdown period for sampling and certifications?

Use the CNY shutdown to finalize your industrial design, engineering validation, and certification paperwork so your deposit is ready the moment the factory reopens.

The physical factory floor might be silent during CNY, but the strategic work of bringing a wellness device to market should not stop. LIGHT BDB operates design centers in Hangzhou and Hamburg, Germany. These teams can continue refining 3D renderings, color schemes, and user interface concepts while the Chinese assembly lines are paused.

LIGHT BDB offers a 7-day sample turnaround. Strategically, you should request your engineering samples before the CNY shutdown, so your team in the US, EU, or Japan has the physical units to test, photograph, and review during the Chinese holiday. When the factory reopens, you are immediately ready to place the bulk PO and confirm the booking, rather than waiting another 7 days for a first shot.

From a certification perspective, the wellness device category is highly sensitive. It is crucial to understand what you can and cannot claim. LIGHT BDB is a wellness-device ODM, not a medical device manufacturer. We do not hold ISO 13485, and no product is FDA approved. The certifications we hold—ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory, and Sony Green Partner—are quality and manufacturing process standards.

Your brand must manage its own regulatory compliance for markets like the US or EU. Use the CNY downtime for legal counsel to review your product claims, marketing language, and packaging labels. Ensure you avoid any phrasing that suggests the devices treat, cure, or diagnose diseases, as they are not cleared medical devices.

What are the honest trade-offs of scheduling production right before or after CNY?

Scheduling right before CNY risks freight delays and workforce attrition, while scheduling right after CNY faces inflated component prices and a scramble for constrained production slots.

There is no "perfect" time to book production around CNY—only a set of trade-offs you must consciously accept.

The "Pre-CNY" Rush: Placing your order 8–10 weeks out is the safest way to secure stock, but you are competing with every other brand doing the same thing. Component prices, especially for plastics and PCBA, often rise slightly as suppliers prioritize high-volume orders. The final two weeks before CNY turn into a logistics gamble. Air freight rates can double, and ocean carriers often impose blank sailings. If your order slips by just a few days, your goods sit in a Shenzhen warehouse for a month.

The "Post-CNY" Scramble: Booking your production slot immediately after the holiday means you are at the back of a long queue. Factories typically return to full output capacity about 2 weeks after CNY, but they clear the backlog of pre-CNY orders first. The benefit here is that component prices often stabilize and drop to normal levels, and freight capacity is plentiful. The risk is purely to your go-to-market timeline.

LIGHT BDB's structural advantage: Our Yangon overflow line (5,000+ pcs/day) offers a genuine buffer for post-CNY volume. However, this line is best used for larger, standardized orders where the design is already locked. If you are launching a brand-new SKU with heavy customization (Pro tier), you are best served waiting for the main Shenzhen line to resume at full strength.

We also mitigate the financial risk of holiday planning with our 500-day limited warranty. This is one of the longest warranties in the wellness device category. After-sales risk falls on the factory, not your brand, which gives you more confidence to order with enough buffer time before CNY.
