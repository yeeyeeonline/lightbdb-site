What actually changes when you add a starry sky ambience finish?

A starry sky finish changes four things: the shell's colour or print layer, the light transmission of the housing, the packaging artwork, and the item record in your ERP.

Most brand owners assume a colourway is a paint job. On the factory side it is a small engineering project that touches the moulding shop, the print supplier, the photometric bench and the packaging line at the same time. The reason it matters commercially is that three of those four changes are visible to your customer, and two of them are visible to your retailer.

The shell: pigment, film or texture

There are three common routes to a starry sky, and each carries a different tooling and MOQ consequence.

  • Moulded-in pigment. A metallic or pearl masterbatch blended into the resin. Cheapest at volume and the most durable, but the "stars" read as a texture or sparkle rather than an image. Colour drift between resin lots is the main risk.
  • Film insert (IMD/IML). A printed gradient, constellation map or nebula sits inside the moulding under a clear layer. This gives the deepest visual effect and the most photographic depth, but requires a plate set — which is usually the single largest setup cost line in the project.
  • Coating and laser etching. Soft-touch paint plus a laser-programmed star field under a clear coat. Flexible for smaller runs and easy to revise, but wear and chemical resistance depend entirely on the coating stack you specify.

Finish development for these routes is where our Hangzhou and Hamburg design centres earn their keep: Hangzhou drives colour and surface work close to the tooling, Hamburg handles the EU-facing colour reference and packaging review so the sample you approve in Europe matches the sample the factory is building to.

The light path: a finish is a photometric decision

On any illuminated product — a projector, a mood panel, a heated massager with an ambient ring — the housing is part of the optical system. A translucent midnight-blue shell will shift the perceived colour temperature of the glow even if the LED bin never changes.

Most factories in this category verify this with a haze meter and an integrating sphere, reporting haze and total transmission per ASTM D1003 against a retained golden sample. Treat that measurement as part of the colourway approval, not an afterthought. It is also the reason a colourway change should trigger a fresh LED bin review: keep the bin identical across SKUs where you want the ambience to read the same in a retailer's photo set, and document it when you deliberately change it.

The paperwork: artwork, labels and barcodes

A starry sky finish almost always means a new colour box, a new master carton print, a revised manual cover, and a rating label that matches the visible finish name. Each of those is a print item with its own plate or die-line, and each one is a reason your operations team will want a distinct item number.

Regulatory marks that your market requires on the label are supplied by you or your compliance lab — LIGHT BDB builds and marks to the artwork you approve, we do not decide your market access strategy for you.

Does a new colourway have to become a new SKU, or can it be a variant?

It can be either — the decision is commercial, not technical, and it hinges on whether retailers, marketplaces and your 3PL need to tell the two items apart.

The finish itself is easy. Splitting your catalogue is where brands get hurt. Use the following as a decision list; if two or more apply, register a new SKU.

  • Packaging differs. A new box means a new barcode, which means a new sellable unit in almost every channel.
  • Marketplace listings. Major marketplaces require a unique GTIN per listing. Reusing a GTIN across two visible finishes is a listing-integrity problem waiting to happen.
  • Retailer EDI and planograms. Buyers slot products by item number; a "variant" that arrives in different packaging breaks their receiving process.
  • Reviews and returns. If the finish could plausibly drive different expectations of the glow or the mood, keep the feedback streams separate.
  • Warranty tracking. A 500-day limited warranty is easier to administer when the item number maps to one build configuration.

The counter-case is real too: if the only difference is an internal colour reference and the retail unit is byte-identical, a variant saves you a full SKU setup and a second set of listing assets. Just confirm your 3PL can pick it without error before you go that route.

What MOQ applies to a starry sky colourway run?

A bespoke starry sky finish falls into the ODM custom tier: 500–1,000 pcs on Light & Mood platforms and 2,000–3,000 pcs on body care platforms.

The tiers exist because a custom finish consumes the same setup whether you order 500 pieces or 5,000 — colour matching rounds, plate or tooling work, packaging artwork, and a sample loop. Choosing an existing platform colourway and putting your brand on it costs far less volume commitment than inventing a finish.

What does a colourway change cost to set up?

There is no rate card — LIGHT BDB quotes each colourway project individually — but the industry-typical setup budget for a finish-only change in this category lands in the low four figures in USD.

That number is not a LIGHT BDB price; it is what most factories in this category will need to recover across the setup. The buckets are consistent:

  1. Colour matching rounds. Usually two to three rounds against your Pantone, RAL or physical master plaque. Most factories in this category work to roughly ΔE ≤ 2.0 on solid moulded colours and ΔE ≤ 3.0 on textured or soft-touch surfaces.
  2. Print plates or film tooling. The dominant line item if you choose an IMD/IML star field or a gradient print.
  3. Shell tooling changes. Zero if the colour changes but the geometry does not. Real money if the finish requires a new insert, a different surface texture or a revised light window.
  4. Packaging. A new colour box die-line, a plate set and a fresh master carton print. This is often underestimated because it is not "product" spend.
  5. Sample rounds and verification. Build, ship, haze or transmission measurement, and a retained golden sample for the production file.

The financial point that matters more than the setup total: setup is a fixed cost, so it lands hardest on the smallest tier. A starry sky film at 500 pcs can carry three to four times the per-unit setup burden of the same project at 3,000 pcs. If the finish is central to your brand story, commit to volume rather than splitting the launch.

How long does a colourway change take from sample to FOB Shenzhen?

Plan on 7–14 days for samples plus 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen; the colourway itself rarely adds time, but new print plates and packaging artwork frequently do.

The sequence usually looks like this:

  • Colour brief. You send a Pantone/RAL reference, a physical plaque, or reference photography. Physical references beat photography every time — screens lie about dark blues and metallics.
  • Sample build (7–14 days). LIGHT BDB builds the finish sample and returns it with the measurement data. The sample fee is refundable against the purchase order.
  • Approval and artwork. You sign off the colour and the packaging artwork in parallel. This is where schedules usually slip, and it is entirely on the buyer side — barcode issuance, label copy and legal review are rarely as fast as everyone plans.

Which certifications carry over to a new colourway, and what has to be re-verified?

Factory certifications carry over unchanged — a colourway does not alter the factory system — but material declarations, light-path measurements and labelling must be re-verified for the new build.

What stays put:

  • ISO 9001 — quality management system, applies to the site, not the SKU.
  • ISO 14001 — environmental management system.
  • BSCI (amfori) — social compliance audit of the operation.
  • UL registered factory — the registration describes the manufacturing site.
  • Sony Green Partner — our environmental procurement standard as a supplier.

What has to be refreshed per colourway:

  • Material declarations. A new pigment, masterbatch, film or coating is a new material entry in your compliance file. Most markets in the EU and US require substance declarations for it; your compliance lab or your buyer's regulatory team signs that off.
  • Light-path data. Haze and transmission figures if the shell's optical behaviour changed.
  • Surface durability. Abrasion and chemical resistance checks for coatings, especially soft-touch.
  • Label and artwork accuracy. The label must describe the product actually shipping, including the finish name.

One point worth being blunt about: LIGHT BDB is a wellness-device ODM. Products are not medical devices, we do not hold a medical-device quality certification, and no product carries regulatory clearance. A starry sky finish does not change that, and nobody should tell you a colourway upgrade moves a mood or massage product into a regulated category.

What are the honest trade-offs of launching a starry sky finish as its own SKU?

The real trade-offs are inventory fragmentation, colour-match drift between production lots, and packaging cost you cannot amortise across a second SKU.

We would rather you hear these before the PO than after the first container.

  • Volume splits against the MOQ tier. A 500-piece custom tier split across three colourways is three separate setups, not one. Either consolidate into one launch finish or commit to volume per finish.
  • Colour drift between lots. Pigment batches and coating batches move. Keep a signed master plaque in your file, agree a ΔE tolerance in writing, and re-approve when the resin supplier changes.
  • Packaging does not scale down. A second box is a second die-line, a second print minimum and a second warehousing line. It is the most common reason a "cheap" variant SKU ends up costing more than the original.
  • Test and documentation burden repeats. Factory certificates carry over, but material declarations and light-path data do not — every colourway adds paperwork per market.
  • Reviews and listings fragment. Two SKUs means two review pools, two ranking histories and two sets of creative assets. Sometimes the marketing value of a distinct finish is worth it; sometimes it quietly splits your social proof.
  • Photography is a genuine risk. Starry sky finishes are notoriously hard to shoot. A finish that reads beautifully in a dark lifestyle image can look flat on a white pack shot, and that gap drives returns.
  • You own the inventory. LIGHT BDB manufactures; the SKU, the stock and the sell-through risk stay with you. We will tell you when a tier makes sense and when it does not.

The honest summary: if the starry sky finish is the product's identity, build it as a full SKU at the ODM custom tier and commit. If it is a seasonal accent, ship it as a white-label run on an existing finish and keep the catalogue simple.

Frequently asked questions about starry sky colourway SKUs

How many units do I need to order for a custom starry sky finish?

For Light & Mood platforms, expect 500–1,000 pcs in the ODM custom tier. For body care platforms, expect 2,000–3,000 pcs. Standard platform colourways start much lower — 100 pcs and 500 pcs respectively — and white-label runs sit at 300 pcs and 1,000 pcs.

Does a new colourway need its own barcode and SKU?

If the packaging changes, yes. A new colour box means a new sellable unit, and major marketplaces require a unique GTIN per listing. If the retail unit is genuinely identical and only an internal colour reference differs, a variant can work.

How fast can I get a starry sky colourway sample?

Sample turnaround at LIGHT BDB is 7–14 days. The sample fee is refundable against the purchase order, so the sample loop is not sunk cost once you commit to production.

What is the production lead time once I place the order?

Lead time is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs. It is confirmed per SKU at the purchase order because it depends on current line loading. New print plates or insert tooling are scheduled ahead of that window.

Can I change the finish without changing the electronics?

Usually yes. Pigment, film inserts and coatings are shell-level changes and leave the driver, battery and firmware untouched. If the finish changes how light leaves the housing, the diffuser or LED bin may also need review.

Will the starry sky finish wear off?

It depends on the route. Moulded-in pigment cannot wear off because it is part of the material. Film inserts sit under a clear layer and are durable. Coatings are the wear-sensitive option, so specify abrasion and chemical resistance testing for soft-touch surfaces.

How do I keep colour consistent across repeat orders?

Approve a physical master plaque and keep a signed copy in your production file. Agree a ΔE tolerance with the factory in writing, and re-approve when the pigment or coating supplier changes. Repeat runs also need the same LED bin if the ambience must look identical.
