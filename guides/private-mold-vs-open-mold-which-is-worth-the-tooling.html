  • Open molds require no tooling deposit; private molds add a one-time mold fee amortized into your unit cost.
  • LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers: Lite 100-499 units (samples/drops), Slim 500-999 (most popular), Pro 1,000+ (full customization).
  • Private mold at Pro tier gives you exclusive shell design, logo placement, and colorways — not available on open molds.
  • Lead time from deposit to FOB Shenzhen is 4-8 weeks for most SKUs at LIGHT BDB.
  • Sample turnaround is 7 days; the sample fee is refundable against your purchase order.

You are launching a heated massager, a red light panel, or a light-and-mood lamp. Your factory shows you two catalogs: open-mold shells you can rebrand, and private-mold designs that are yours exclusively. The decision is not about aesthetics — it is about cash flow, inventory risk, and how long you plan to stay in the market. This guide compares both routes with real numbers, so you can decide before you wire a deposit.

What is the difference between a private mold and an open mold?

An open mold is a shell design owned by the factory that any brand can use, while a private mold is a design owned by you — the factory builds it for your exclusive use. Open molds are sometimes called "public molds" or "shared tooling." The factory already paid for the tooling and amortized it across many clients, so you do not pay a mold fee. Your brand is limited to stickers, printing, and sometimes slight color changes. A private mold means you commission new tooling (injection-molding steel) sized and shaped to your product. You own that steel or you sign an exclusivity contract for it. That gives you unique shape, button layout, airflow paths, and structural ribs — but you pay the tooling cost, usually upfront, and you accept a higher MOQ to justify it.

Where the ownership lives

  • Open mold: tooling owned by the factory; you own no physical asset.
  • Private mold: tooling owned by your company (or contractually exclusive to you).
  • In both cases, the electronics, firmware, and packaging remain your IP under an NDA.

How much should a first-time buyer budget for private tooling?

A wellness-device private mold typically costs between $3,000 and $15,000 depending on part count and complexity — but treat that as an industry-typical range, not a LIGHT BDB quote, because exact pricing depends on your shell design. A heated eye massager shell with two plastic parts and a silicone cushion is at the low end. A red light panel with a large frame, mounting brackets, and lens holders is at the high end. Your actual mold fee is quoted after a DFM (design for manufacturability) review, and you should always ask what the mold fee covers: steel, texturing, first-article trials, and revisions.

What the mold fee includes

  • Steel cutting and cavity machining for the injection mold.
  • Surface texture (gloss, matte, soft-touch) on the mold faces.
  • First-article samples and trial shots to validate the shell.
  • Typically two or three design revision rounds before PPAP-style sign-off.

Do not confuse the mold fee with the unit price. LIGHT BDB quotes unit cost separately at each MOQ tier: Lite (100-499), Slim (500-999), Pro (1,000+). At Pro tier, your per-unit cost drops and your mold fee amortizes fastest.

What MOQ does private mold require at LIGHT BDB?

A private mold at LIGHT BDB requires the Pro tier of 1,000+ units to be economically justified; the Slim tier of 500-999 works for open molds, and the Lite tier of 100-499 is for sampling and limited drops. That is a direct answer: below 1,000 units, a private mold rarely pays back within your first production run. At Pro tier, you get full customization — that is the point where the tooling cost per unit becomes small enough to be worth it. Most private-label buyers at LIGHT BDB start with an open mold at Slim tier, sell through, then commit to private tooling once demand is proven.

How long does private mold tooling take from design to FOB?

Budget 8-12 weeks from final 3D file to FOB Shenzhen for a private mold — the tooling itself adds 4-6 weeks on top of LIGHT BDB's standard 4-8 week production lead time. The standard lead time applies to open molds and existing SKUs; a new mold requires steel cutting, texturing, trial shots, and your approval before mass production starts. Your sample turnaround is 7 days, and the sample fee is refundable against your PO — so order a sample from an open mold first to validate the product feel while your private mold is being cut.

Typical private-mold timeline

  1. Design freeze: you submit final 3D files and DFM review — week 1.
  2. Tooling construction: steel cutting, cavity machining, texturing — weeks 2-6.
  3. Trial shots and first articles: you approve color, texture, and fit — week 7.
  4. Mass production: 4-8 weeks after approval — weeks 8-15.
  5. FOB Shenzhen: goods ready for your nominated forwarder.

Plan your seasonality accordingly. If you need products for Q4, commit to tooling by May at the latest.

What certifications matter for a private-label wellness device?

For a wellness device, the certifications that matter are factory-level compliance and electrical safety — not medical approvals, because LIGHT BDB does not hold ISO 13485 and no product is FDA approved. LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 14001 (environmental), BSCI (amfori) (social compliance), UL registered factory, and Sony Green Partner. You should verify electrical certification for your target market — UL/ETL for the US, CE for the EU, PSE for Japan — via the component specs and final product testing. Do not ask your factory to claim medical device status; wellness products are not medical devices, and marketing them as such is a legal risk in every major market.

Certification checklist to ask your ODM

What are the honest trade-offs of committing to a private mold?

A private mold is worth it only if you plan multiple production runs, because the tooling fee is a sunk cost that your first run must absorb. If you sell only 1,000 units and stop, your effective per-unit cost is roughly 15-30% higher than buying the same product on an open mold. The exclusivity also carries risk: if your design fails in the market, you cannot resell the tooling easily — you own steel that only fits your product. On the other hand, open molds mean your competitor can sell the exact same shell at a lower price, forcing you to compete on brand, distribution, and support rather than design.

When to choose each route

  • Choose open mold: first launch, low budget, testing a niche, short seasonal drop.
  • Choose private mold: proven demand, multi-run plan, distinct brand position, patentable design.
  • Hybrid: start open at Slim tier (500-999), validate sell-through, then commit to Pro (1,000+) with private tooling.

The hybrid route is the most common path at LIGHT BDB — it limits upfront risk while keeping the door open to exclusivity. Your sample fee is refundable against the PO, and the 7-day sample turnaround lets you test feel and sizing before any tooling commitment.

How do I evaluate a factory before paying for private tooling?

Verify factory certifications directly from certificates or audit reports, not from website claims — LIGHT BDB publishes ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory, and Sony Green Partner as verifiable credentials. Ask for client references, request a 7-day sample first, and check the factory's overflow capacity: LIGHT BDB runs a Panyu component plant (10,000+ sqm, 600+ staff), a Shenzhen 3,000 sqm assembly line, and a Yangon overflow line at 5,000+ pcs/day — which tells you they can scale. For private mold, you want a factory with in-house tooling or a long-term tooling partner; if they outsource the mold, your DFM turnaround will be slower.

Red flags to avoid

  • Factory claims ISO 13485 or FDA approval — LIGHT BDB does not hold these, and any wellness ODM claiming them without proof is a risk.
  • Mold fee quoted without DFM review or itemization.
  • MOQ below 100 units for a private mold — this usually means the tool is low-quality single-cavity.
  • No sample policy or non-refundable sample fee without PO credit.
