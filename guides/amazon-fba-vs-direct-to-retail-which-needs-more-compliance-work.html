Amazon FBA vs. Direct to Retail: Which Needs More Compliance Work?

Quick answer

Direct to retail requires roughly 3–5 times more compliance work than Amazon FBA for a first-time wellness device importer. Amazon FBA demands basic electrical safety and documentation, while retail buyers typically require full liability insurance, vendor compliance portals, chargeback agreements, and retailer-specific packaging standards before they even issue a PO.

  • Amazon FBA compliance focuses on electrical safety (UL/ETL), FCC for electronics, and CPSIA for children's products.
  • Direct to retail adds vendor compliance portals, routing guides, and chargeback contracts that Amazon never requires.
  • Most US retailers require $1M–$5M in product liability insurance before accepting a new vendor.
  • Retailers often demand EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) setup, which costs $200–$500 per month plus integration fees.
  • Amazon FBA allows you to start with a Lite MOQ of 100–499 units; retail buyers usually expect 1,000+ units per SKU.
  • LIGHT BDB ships FOB Shenzhen in 4–8 weeks, but retail compliance documents can add 4–12 weeks before your first shipment.
  • Amazon handles returns and customer service; retail chargebacks can deduct 2–10% of your invoice for compliance failures.
  • A UL registered factory like LIGHT BDB shortens the certification process, but retailer-specific paperwork remains your responsibility.

What compliance documents does Amazon FBA actually require for wellness devices?

Amazon FBA requires a product compliance document, an electrical safety test report, and a Declaration of Conformity — but no vendor portal, no EDI, and no chargeback agreement.

For a heated massager, red light therapy device, or similar wellness product, the core Amazon FBA compliance stack is:

  • Electrical safety testing: UL 1642 for batteries, UL 60335 for household appliances, or IEC 60335 equivalent. Most factories in this category test to IEC 60335 and provide the report.
  • FCC compliance: Any product with electronics, Bluetooth, or wireless charging needs FCC Part 15 (intentional radiators) or Part 18 (ISM equipment).
  • Product compliance document: A technical file showing materials, wiring diagrams, and test results.
  • Battery transport documentation: UN 38.3 test summary and MSDS for lithium-ion batteries.
  • Prop 65 warning: If you sell into California, a clear warning label for any listed chemicals (lead, phthalates, etc.).

Amazon does not require you to have a registered business entity in the US, but you need a valid bank account, tax ID, and a return address in the US or use Amazon's return service.

What compliance work does direct to retail add on top of that?

Direct to retail adds vendor compliance portals, routing guides, EDI setup, liability insurance, and chargeback agreements — none of which exist on Amazon FBA.

When a buyer at Target, Walmart, Costco, or a regional chain shows interest, the compliance workload expands in five distinct areas:

1. Vendor compliance portal registration

Most large retailers require you to register in their supplier portal (e.g., Walmart Retail Link, Target Partners Online). These portals require:

  • Business registration documents (W-9, EIN, certificate of incorporation)
  • Bank verification and ACH setup
  • Product liability insurance certificates
  • Factory audit reports (BSCI, SMETA, or WCA)
  • Conflict minerals declaration and modern slavery statement

2. Routing guide compliance

Every retailer publishes a routing guide specifying carton size, pallet configuration, labeling, barcode placement, and shipping carrier. Failure to follow the routing guide results in chargebacks. Common requirements include:

  • GS1-128 barcodes on every carton
  • Specific pallet heights (usually 48 inches max)
  • Retailer-specific case labels with PO number and store code
  • No overhang, no mixed SKUs on one pallet

3. EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)

Retailers require EDI for purchase orders, invoices, advance ship notices (ASN), and inventory updates. You will need:

  • An EDI provider (e.g., SPS Commerce, TrueCommerce) costing $150–$500 per month
  • Testing and certification against the retailer's specific EDI maps
  • Ongoing transaction fees per document

4. Product liability insurance

Most US retailers require $1M–$5M in product liability coverage, naming the retailer as an additional insured. For a first-time importer, this typically costs $2,000–$8,000 per year depending on product category and sales volume.

5. Chargeback agreements

Retailers deduct penalties for compliance failures. Common chargebacks include:

  • $100–$500 per carton for incorrect labeling
  • $50–$250 per shipment for late ASN
  • 2–10% of invoice for routing guide violations
  • Full cost of return shipping for non-compliant goods

Which channel requires more ongoing compliance maintenance?

Direct to retail requires continuous compliance maintenance through chargebacks, audits, and annual renewals, while Amazon FBA compliance is largely a one-time setup with periodic updates.

Ongoing compliance work breaks down as follows:

Amazon FBA ongoing requirements

  • Renew FCC and safety test reports every 3–5 years (or when the product changes)
  • Update the Declaration of Conformity if you change factories or components
  • Monitor Amazon's category-specific policy updates (e.g., new battery regulations)
  • Respond to Amazon's random compliance audits — typically 1–2 per year

Direct to retail ongoing requirements

  • Renew liability insurance annually and provide updated certificates to each retailer
  • Maintain EDI connections and test annually or when retailers upgrade their systems
  • Pass retailer-requested factory audits (BSCI, SMETA) — LIGHT BDB holds BSCI (amfori) certification, which most retailers accept
  • Update routing guide compliance when retailers change their requirements
  • Track and dispute chargebacks — a weekly task for most vendors
  • Submit annual sustainability or ESG questionnaires (increasingly common at Walmart, Target, and Home Depot)

Most brands selling through both channels report that retail compliance consumes 5–10 hours per week of staff time, versus 1–2 hours per month for Amazon FBA.

What are the honest trade-offs between Amazon FBA and direct to retail?

The honest trade-off is that Amazon FBA trades compliance simplicity for thin margins and no brand control, while direct to retail trades heavy compliance work for better margins and long-term stability.

Here are the real trade-offs, without the sales pitch:

  • Margin: Amazon FBA fees (referral + fulfillment) typically consume 25–40% of your sale price. Retail margins are negotiated but often yield 40–50% gross margin before marketing costs.
  • Brand control: On Amazon, you compete on price and reviews. In retail, your packaging, shelf placement, and brand story matter more.
  • Cash flow: Amazon pays every 2 weeks. Retailers pay in 30–90 days, and some take 120 days. Your working capital needs are significantly higher for retail.
  • Risk concentration: Amazon can suspend your listing with one policy violation. Retail spreads risk across multiple buyers, but losing one retailer hurts more.
  • Data ownership: Amazon owns the customer relationship. In retail, you get point-of-sale data and can build direct relationships.
  • Time to revenue: Amazon FBA can generate revenue in 10–20 weeks. Retail takes 20–45 weeks before your first PO ships.

Many successful brands start on Amazon FBA to validate the product, gather sales data, and build a track record — then use that data to approach retail buyers. LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers support this staged approach, and our 500-day limited warranty gives you a differentiation point in both channels.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need UL certification for Amazon FBA?

Amazon FBA requires an electrical safety test report, and UL certification is the most widely accepted standard in the US. A UL registered factory like LIGHT BDB can provide the necessary test data and documentation. For most wellness devices, IEC 60335 testing is the baseline, and UL listing is recommended for higher trust and fewer listing issues.

What is a vendor compliance portal and do I need one for retail?

A vendor compliance portal is a retailer's online system for managing supplier documentation, purchase orders, and compliance requirements. Walmart, Target, and other major retailers require new vendors to register in their portal before issuing a PO. The portal typically asks for business registration, insurance certificates, factory audits, and product compliance documents.

How much does product liability insurance cost for selling to retailers?

Product liability insurance for a wellness device brand typically costs $2,000–$8,000 per year for $1M–$5M in coverage. The exact premium depends on your product category, sales volume, and claims history. Retailers usually require you to name them as an additional insured on your policy.

What is EDI and why do retailers require it?

EDI (Electronic Data Interchange) is the system retailers use to send purchase orders and receive invoices and shipping notices electronically. Most large retailers require EDI instead of email or fax. Setup costs $150–$500 per month through providers like SPS Commerce, plus testing fees to certify against each retailer's specific requirements.

Can I sell to both Amazon FBA and retail stores at the same time?

Yes, many brands sell through both channels simultaneously. Start with Amazon FBA to build sales data, then add retail accounts once you have proven demand. You will need separate inventory planning for each channel, and you must ensure your packaging meets both Amazon's and the retailer's requirements.

What are retail chargebacks and how do I avoid them?

Chargebacks are penalties retailers deduct from your invoice for compliance failures like incorrect labeling, late shipments, or routing guide violations. They typically range from $50 to $500 per incident, and can reach 2–10% of your invoice value. You avoid them by following the retailer's routing guide exactly and using a compliance checklist for every shipment.

How long does it take to get approved as a new retail vendor?

Retail vendor approval typically takes 4–12 weeks from first contact to a signed agreement. The timeline depends on the retailer's buying cycle, your product category, and how quickly you complete their compliance requirements. Having a BSCI-certified factory like LIGHT BDB and a UL registered facility speeds up the process.
