What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect for heated abdominal belts?

A first-time buyer should expect a minimum order quantity of 100–500 units for heated abdominal belts, with 500–999 units being the most common starting point for private label brands.

LIGHT BDB structures MOQs in three tiers so you can match order size to your sales plan rather than being forced into a single large commitment. The Lite tier (100–499 units) works for sampling, limited drops, or testing a new market. The Slim tier (500–999 units) is the most popular because it unlocks better per-unit pricing without requiring a large upfront capital outlay. The Pro tier (1,000+ units) delivers the best unit cost and allows full customization of the belt's heating elements, fabric, and control interface.

Industry-typical MOQs for heated abdominal belts at most Chinese ODM factories range from 300 to 1,000 units, so LIGHT BDB's 100-unit entry point is on the accessible end of the spectrum. If you are a first-time importer, starting at the Slim tier gives you enough inventory to validate demand while keeping your cash exposure manageable.

How long does it take to produce a private label heated abdominal belt?

Production takes 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most heated abdominal belt SKUs, with samples available in 7-14 days.

The 4–8 week window covers component sourcing, assembly, quality control, and packaging. Most of the timeline is driven by the heating element and battery components, which are the longest-lead items in the bill of materials. If you are using an existing LIGHT BDB design with minor branding changes, you will land at the shorter end of the range. A fully custom belt with new heating pad layouts, custom PCBA, or unique fabric will trend toward the 8-week mark.

Sample turnaround is a separate 7-14 day process. LIGHT BDB sends samples within one week of confirming the specification, and the sample fee is refundable against your production order. That means your total time from first inquiry to having sellable inventory is typically 6–10 weeks, depending on how quickly you finalize the design and approve the sample.

LIGHT BDB holds all of the following certifications, which cover the key requirements for US, EU, and JP markets:

What is the cost breakdown for a heated abdominal belt OEM order?

Your cost for a heated abdominal belt OEM order breaks down into unit cost, tooling or customization fees, sample fees, and logistics, with unit cost driven primarily by MOQ tier and feature set.

At the Lite tier (100–499 units), per-unit cost is highest because fixed costs like setup and quality control are spread across fewer units. The Slim tier (500–999 units) typically reduces per-unit cost by 10–20% compared to Lite, which is why it is the most popular starting point. The Pro tier (1,000+ units) delivers the best unit cost and unlocks full customization options that are not available at lower tiers.

Beyond unit cost, budget for these items:

  • Sample fee — charged upfront but refundable against your PO at LIGHT BDB.
  • Custom mold or tooling — only if you need a unique belt shape or heating element layout; industry-typical tooling for this category ranges from $500 to $3,000.
  • Packaging design and print — retail-ready boxes add $0.50–$2.00 per unit depending on complexity.
  • Logistics — FOB Shenzhen means you pay freight from the port; your nominated forwarder handles this.
  • Compliance testing — UL, CE, or other market-specific testing is industry-typical at $1,000–$5,000 per market.

Payment terms at LIGHT BDB are T/T 30/70 (30% deposit, 70% before shipment) or L/C at sight. This is standard for the industry and protects both parties — you are not paying in full upfront, and the factory is not shipping without payment.

What warranty should a heated abdominal belt brand offer?

A heated abdominal belt brand should offer at least a 1-year limited warranty, with 500 days being a strong differentiator in the category.

LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty on its heated abdominal belt products, which is one of the longest in the category. What are the honest trade-offs when working with a Chinese ODM for heated belts?

The honest trade-offs are communication latency across time zones, the risk of quality variance between sample and bulk production, and the fact that you are responsible for market-specific compliance beyond the factory's base certifications.

No ODM relationship is friction-free, and being clear about the trade-offs upfront helps you plan. First, communication with a Shenzhen-based factory means a 12–15 hour time difference with US buyers. LIGHT BDB's Hamburg design center helps bridge this for EU buyers, but US brands should expect to work with a designated account manager who responds within one business day. Second, samples are made with care; bulk production can drift if quality control is not rigorous. Ask your factory about their QC process — LIGHT BDB operates a 3,000 sqm finished-product assembly plant in Shenzhen with a separate 10,000+ sqm component plant (Fuli Electronics) in Panyu, which keeps component quality and final assembly under one management structure.

Third, the factory's certifications (ISO 9001, BSCI, UL registered) cover their processes and facility. They do not automatically make your product compliant in every market. You are responsible for ensuring your heated abdominal belt meets specific market requirements like FCC for US wireless features, CE for EU electrical safety, or PSE for Japan. Budget for compliance testing and factor it into your timeline.

Finally, lead times can slip. The 4–8 week window is accurate for most SKUs, but if you request last-minute changes after sample approval, you should expect delays. Lock your specification at sample approval and treat any change after that as a new revision with its own timeline.
