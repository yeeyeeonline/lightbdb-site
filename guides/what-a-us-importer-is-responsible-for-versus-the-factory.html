What a US Importer Is Responsible For Versus the Factory

Quick answer

The factory builds the product and documents the build; the importer of record owns the legal exposure. In practice you carry US customs entry, duty and consumer-safety compliance, while LIGHT BDB handles tooling, production QC and 4–8 week FOB Shenzhen delivery, backed by a 500-day limited warranty.

  • As importer of record you file the customs entry, post the bond and pay duty; the factory's role ends at FOB Shenzhen.
  • LIGHT BDB MOQ starts at 100 pcs for Light & Mood standard platforms and 500 pcs for body care standard platforms.
  • ODM custom production runs take 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs.
  • Factory certifications are ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory and Sony Green Partner.
  • Your brand owns label copy, warnings and marketing claims; the factory supplies test data and printed artwork.
  • Samples ship in 7–14 days and the sample fee is refundable against your purchase order.
  • Every unit carries a 500-day limited warranty, one of the longest in the wellness-device category.
  • Payment terms are T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight, FOB Shenzhen, with your nominated forwarder.

Who is legally the importer of record when I buy from a Chinese factory?

You are — the moment your company files a customs entry on the shipment, you become the importer of record and the party US agencies hold accountable.

That title is not a formality on a form. It means your company name sits on the entry, your bond secures the duties, and your file has to survive an audit. The factory's legal role in the physical goods ends when the pallet is handed to your forwarder at the named Incoterm point — for LIGHT BDB that is FOB Shenzhen.

Practically, the split looks like this:

  • Your side: customs bond, entry filing through a licensed broker, HTS classification decisions, duty and tariff payment, cargo insurance, inland transport, and recordkeeping (CBP expects import records retained for five years).
  • Factory side: commercial invoice, packing list, material and construction data your broker needs, origin marking on the carton, and loading the container on time.
  • Shared: documentation accuracy. The factory should give you honest weights, dimensions and material breakdowns; you should not accept a vague invoice to “help” the entry.

One nuance worth flagging early: if a supplier offers DDP (delivered duty paid) to your US warehouse, someone else is being named as importer of record on that entry. That can be convenient for small trial shipments, but it also means the compliance file is not in your name, and retailers increasingly want the brand to be the IOR. For a private-label wellness device you intend to scale, FOB plus your own broker is the cleaner structure.

LIGHT BDB works FOB Shenzhen with your nominated forwarder, so you keep control of the ocean leg, the entry and the delivery appointment at your 3PL. We will quote ex-works or other terms case by case, but we will not quietly become the importer on your behalf.

Which compliance obligations stay with the US importer?

Consumer-safety, labeling and claim substantiation stay with you — the factory can test and document, but your brand name sits on the retail box.

Electrical and RF compliance

Any heated massager, red light panel or mood-light platform that plugs in or emits radio frequency needs the right authorization before it can be sold. That means the applicable FCC equipment authorization route for wireless or Bluetooth-enabled SKUs, and the appropriate electrical safety listing for corded and heated products. Retailers in this category typically ask for a product-level listing from a recognized testing body — not just a factory certificate — so budget for that as a launch cost rather than assuming it comes bundled.

State-level chemical and warning rules

California Proposition 65 warnings, battery and e-waste rules in several states, and packaging-recycling obligations in a growing number of jurisdictions are all brand-level duties. None of them are things a Shenzhen assembly line can decide for you, because the warning depends on where you sell and how you market the product.

Supply-chain tracing

Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act due diligence, and the customs documentation that supports it, sits with the importer. Ask your factory for material origin statements at the component level — inductors, transformers and charging coils are exactly the parts where a paper trail matters.

Retailer-specific paperwork

Amazon, Target, Costco and most EU grocers each run their own supplier portal with its own form set. The factory can supply documents on request; submitting, monitoring and re-submitting them is your job. Build two to three weeks into the launch plan for portal approvals alone.

Where exactly does the responsibility line fall for testing and documentation?

The factory produces test reports and engineering files; you decide which tests your market needs and you hold the finished compliance file.

A common first-import mistake is treating a factory test report as a compliance certificate. They are different documents. A report says “this sample behaved this way under this method.” A compliance file says “this brand has demonstrated the product meets the rules for this market, and here is who is accountable.” You own the second one.

Who is responsible for labeling, packaging and marketing claims?

You are — for anything a consumer reads, the factory prints what you approve but it does not author your claims.

This is where first-time importers get burned most often, because the failure is invisible until a marketplace listing gets flagged or a state regulator sends a letter. Three separate things get confused: what the law requires on the label, what the retailer requires on the listing, and what your brand wants to say. Only the first two are checkable in advance.

  • Required label elements: country of origin, importer or responsible party name and address, electrical ratings, and any applicable warning statements. We print them; you confirm them.
  • Proposition 65: the decision to warn, and the wording, is yours. It depends on sale state and product chemistry — not on our assembly process.
  • Wellness claims: language around warmth, relaxation, light and mood is marketing, and substantiation is a brand duty. Keep claims to what the product demonstrably does and avoid implying therapeutic outcomes.
  • Warranty statement: our 500-day limited warranty is a factory-backed term you can pass through, but the consumer-facing wording is your document.

We do supply the raw material: dielines, artwork templates, barcode placement guidance, carton drop-test data from prior shipments, and the technical descriptions your copywriter needs. Send us your draft label and we will tell you, honestly, which parts we can print and which parts you need to verify with your own regulatory advisor.

What should the purchase order say so the split is unambiguous?

Put the split in writing: Incoterm, who commissions and pays each test, who owns tooling, and how warranty claims get handled.

A PO that only lists quantity, price and ship date leaves the expensive questions open. The clauses below are the ones that prevent disputes six months later.

  1. Incoterm and named place: FOB Shenzhen, stated explicitly, with the forwarder named.
  2. Payment structure: T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight, with the deposit date defined as day zero for lead time.
  3. Golden sample sign-off: photo-documented, signed, and stored — this is the reference for every inspection.
  4. AQL sampling plan: agree the inspection level and critical/major/minor defect definitions up front.
  5. Test responsibility annex: a short table listing each test, who commissions it, who pays, and who holds the report.
  6. Tooling ownership: who paid, who holds title, and what happens to the mould if the relationship ends.
  7. Change control: no component, firmware or supplier substitution without written notice and your approval.
  8. Warranty terms: 500-day limited warranty, spare-parts percentage per shipment, and the RMA approval path.
  9. IP and exclusivity: who owns the custom enclosure design, and whether the SKU can be sold to others.
  10. Packaging approval: artwork approval gate before print, with a physical pre-production sample if the packaging is complex.
  11. Documentation pack: invoice, packing list, material declarations and test reports delivered with the shipment, not after.
  12. Lead time: 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen, with the review point at week three.

What are the honest limits of relying on your factory?

A good factory reduces your compliance workload but never absorbs your legal liability — that stays with the importing brand.

It is worth being blunt about where a strong ODM partner stops helping.

  • Factory certificates are not product certificates. Our UL registered factory status covers the facility. It does not mean each SKU carries a product listing, and no supplier should imply otherwise. Product-level listing is a separate project with its own cost and timeline.
  • Our test data is a starting point, not your defence. We test what we build; we cannot know every state rule, retailer requirement or EU member-state nuance you sell into.
  • Social audits are not safety audits. BSCI (amfori) tells you about working conditions, not about whether a heated product is safe in a bathroom.
  • Low MOQs mean standard platforms. Light & Mood starts at 100 pcs and body care at 500 pcs, but white-label runs start at 300 pcs and 1,000 pcs respectively, and ODM custom runs start at 500–1,000 pcs and 2,000–3,000 pcs. A 100-piece order buys a proven platform with your logo, not a new industrial design.
  • Lead time excludes freight. The 4–8 weeks runs from deposit to FOB Shenzhen. Ocean transit, port congestion and customs exams sit on your side of the line.
  • Warranty support needs your logistics. A 500-day limited warranty is generous, but someone has to receive the return, triage it and decide whether a part or a unit ships. We supply parts; you own the customer experience.
  • Tariffs and trade policy are yours. Duty rates, Section 301 exposure and de minimis rules change without warning and are priced into your landed cost, not ours.

None of this is a reason to avoid an ODM partner — it is a reason to choose one that tells you where the line is. A supplier who promises to “handle everything” is describing a service it cannot legally deliver.

Below are the questions US, EU and JP buyers send us most often before a first purchase order.

Who is the importer of record when I buy from a Chinese factory?

The buyer is, unless another party is explicitly named on the entry. If you purchase FOB and your broker files the entry under your company name, you are the importer of record and you carry the legal responsibility for that shipment.

Does the factory handle customs clearance and duties?

No, not under FOB terms. LIGHT BDB delivers to your nominated forwarder at FOB Shenzhen; clearance, duty, tariffs and inland delivery are arranged and paid by you. We can discuss other Incoterms, but the default keeps the entry in your name.

Who is responsible for FCC testing on a Bluetooth massager?

The brand owner is responsible for making sure the authorization exists and for holding the documentation. We provide engineering samples and technical data to your chosen lab, but the filing and the file belong to you.

Do I need a customs bond to import from China?

Yes. A customs bond is required to clear a commercial shipment into the US, and it is posted by the importer, typically through their customs broker. Most first-time importers start with a single-entry bond and move to a continuous bond once volumes are predictable.

Who pays for product testing — me or the factory?

Usually the importer, because the importer chooses the market and the standard. LIGHT BDB covers internal QC, functional testing and pre-shipment inspection, and provides samples and data for third-party testing that you commission and pay for.

What certifications does LIGHT BDB hold?

ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory status, and Sony Green Partner. These cover our management systems and facility, not individual product authorizations for your market.

Who writes the warning labels and Proposition 65 notices?

The brand owner decides and approves the warning content. We print exactly the artwork you approve and can flag obvious omissions, but the decision about what to warn for, and where, depends on your sales states and product chemistry.

What is the MOQ for a first order?

Light & Mood platforms start at 100 pcs for standard, 300 pcs for white-label and 500–1,000 pcs for ODM custom. Body care platforms start at 500 pcs standard, 1,000 pcs white-label and 2,000–3,000 pcs ODM custom.

How long does production take after I pay the deposit?

Most SKUs run 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen. Custom tooling adds time before that clock starts, and ocean freight, port delays and customs exams are additional on your side.

What warranty does LIGHT BDB offer and who handles returns?

Every unit carries a 500-day limited warranty, one of the longest in the category. We supply replacement parts under that warranty; receiving returns from end consumers and triaging them is normally the brand's job.

Can LIGHT BDB ship to my US warehouse directly?

We ship FOB Shenzhen to your nominated forwarder, who handles the ocean leg and delivery to your warehouse or 3PL. Direct-to-warehouse arrangements are possible through your forwarder, with you remaining the importer of record.

Who owns the tooling for a custom design?

That is a contract term, not a default. Custom tooling is normally paid for by the brand, and ownership, transfer and end-of-relationship handling should all be written into the purchase order before the mould is cut.
