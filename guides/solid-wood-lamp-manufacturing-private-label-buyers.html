Most first-time buyers approach a wood lamp the way they approach a plastic lamp: get three quotes, compare the unit price, pick the middle one.

That approach fails here, and it fails in a specific way. Plastic housings are moulded to a tolerance. Two suppliers quoting the same ABS part are quoting nearly the same object, so the price difference is usually overhead and margin. Solid wood is not like that. Two suppliers can quote the same drawing and deliver visibly different products — because one kiln-dried to 10% moisture content and one to 16%, because one hand-finishes 40 minutes per unit and one finishes 90, because one grades out the knots and one ships them.

Which means the useful question is not "what is your best price at 100 units?" It is "what exactly am I buying at 100 units, and what am I giving up?" This article answers that, using our seven light platforms as the worked example, and it names the limits of ours as well as the strengths.

Why do wood lamps behave differently from plastic lamps in a sourcing conversation?

Three structural differences explain almost all of the price spread, and none of them is the size of the lamp.

1. Batch colour variation cannot be fully eliminated. Wood is a natural material. Two batches of the same species, from the same supplier, in the same quarter, will differ in tone and grain. A factory can grade for consistency and it can specify a target tone, but it cannot promise a colour match the way an injection moulder can promise a Pantone match. Buyers who need absolute uniformity across reorders should be looking at ABS or MDF instead — see solid wood vs ABS housing.

2. Moisture content drives cracking, and it is invisible in a sample. Wood absorbs and releases moisture until it reaches equilibrium with its environment. Ship a part machined at 15% moisture into a dry heated interior and it shrinks; ship it into a humid coastal market and it swells. Both outcomes show up as cracks, warped panels and loose joints in month two, not on the inspection table. This is why a spec that names only species and dimensions is not a spec.

3. Hand-finishing hours scale non-linearly with shape complexity. A branch-form sculpture with two perched birds is not "slightly more work" than a plain cylindrical base. It is a different labour category — sanding into curves, matching grain across a joint, finishing areas a machine cannot reach. That is the single largest reason two wood-lamp quotes for the same silhouette can be 40% apart.

Between them, these three drive the whole conversation: compare quotes on specification and finishing plan, not on unit price alone. For the material-level comparison against engineered wood, see solid wood vs MDF housing.

What should you ask about the wood itself?

Five questions, and the last one is the one that prevents the most disputes.

Species and grade. Ask for the species, the grade and — critically — whether the visible surfaces are solid lumber or veneer over a substrate. Both are legitimate; only one is what most buyers think they bought when they read "solid wood." Where origin is part of your story, ask whether the timber is FSC-certified or otherwise traceable.

Moisture content, and how it is measured. Ask for the target moisture content, the drying method, and whether the factory logs readings per batch. As an industry norm, furniture-grade components for indoor use are typically specified at roughly 8–12% moisture content, with oven-dry verification under methods such as ASTM D4442 or EN 13183-2 — that is a general industry reference, not a claim about our specification, and you should have your own QA confirm it against your destination climate.

Kiln schedule, not just kiln use. Drying wood is not the same as drying it slowly. A properly staged schedule takes a board down gradually and conditions it at the end to relieve internal stress. Wood that reads 9% at the surface but still holds 12% in the core will move weeks after it ships. A factory that can show batch moisture logs is a factory that has thought about this.

Surface finish. Open-pore oil, sealed lacquer and wax finishes behave differently in humidity and in the hand. Ask which, and how the finish is assessed against restricted-substance requirements for your market.

Acceptable variation, in writing. This is the one. Write into your spec what degree of tone variation and what size or frequency of knots is acceptable, with photographs of a pass and a fail. Wood-lamp disputes are almost never about defects; they are about two parties holding different pictures of "normal."

Three electronics questions worth asking, whichever factory you use:

  • Plug-in or battery? A rechargeable battery adds transport documentation — UN38.3 and MSDS — and a shipping conversation. See rechargeable battery vs plug-in for ambient lamps.
  • Multi-room linkage and audio. If your lamp streams audio, FCC marking for light and sound devices is the relevant read before you spec the feature.

Which market changes the plug, the voltage and the label?

The destination market decides the plug, the manual language, the label set and the certification combination — and it should be decided before the BOM, not after.

Four markets are already in scope for these platforms: the US, EU, Japan and the UAE. Each brings a different plug type and voltage, a different manual-language expectation, and a different combination of product marks. Getting this wrong is expensive in a specific way: it is not a redesign, it is a re-pack, a re-label and a re-print, plus an air-freight bill.

The detail is covered in US, EU and JP voltage and plug differences for ambient lighting, and the marking side in country of origin labeling rules. For the US rule itself, see CBP marking of country of origin. If you are shipping to more than one market from a single run, say so on the RFQ — it changes the packaging and the label plan, and it is easier to design in than to retrofit.

What are the honest trade-offs of a solid-wood program?

Four, and the first one disqualifies some brands outright.

Colour variation and knots cannot be eliminated — only bounded. If your brand promise is a perfectly repeatable finish across reorders, solid wood is the wrong material and we will tell you so. ABS or MDF will hold a tighter match, at the cost of the thing that makes a wood object worth buying.

Only some platforms accept deeper customization. App UI control is available on the Dance of Light platforms. The other five take colour, logo and packaging — no structure change, no new function module, no app. If your roadmap needs a custom sculpture with a custom control interface at 100 units, that is not a Lite project and it is not a two-month project either.

A 100-unit run is a channel test, not a price ladder. Lite tier gets you into retail and into a DTC test; it will not produce the unit cost of a 1,000-unit Pro order. Do not build a permanent retail price on a 100-unit quote — price it as the smallest tier it is, and re-quote at 500. A solid wood lamp manufacturer in China worth your deposit will say that before you ask.

Scented candle and aroma products sit inside the same Light & Mood family, but they are represented on the category page and the FAQ rather than on a dedicated landing page. Treat the category page as the record, and confirm samples and certificates per project rather than assuming what a sibling category carries.

FAQ

What is the minimum order for a private label wood lamp?

100 units, on a platform we already produce. That is the Lite tier, and it covers colourway, logo, hang tags and packaging artwork. Structure, light source and electronics stay as validated.

Can I customize the wood species and finish?

Species and finish changes move you to a higher tier because they change the material lot and the finishing plan, not just the print. At Lite you are choosing a colourway on a validated platform; finish and material upgrades typically start at the Slim tier.

Does a wood lamp need different certifications than a plastic lamp?

Not because of the wood — the housing material does not change the electrical certification path. The certification set changes when the electronics change: adding a radio, a battery or audio changes the CE / FCC / PSE combination per model and per market.

Can I add wireless charging to a wood lamp design?

Yes. Wireless-charging coils are made at the Panyu components plant operated by our exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics, so the coil's lead time tracks that plant's schedule rather than a purchased-component minimum order. Dance of Light and Glow of Sunrise · Gift already carry wireless charging as platforms.

How long does sampling take for an ambient light sculpture?

Sampling turns around in a week — seven days from the point your technical files and the sample fee are confirmed. That fee then comes back to you as a credit on your first production order. Most SKUs then take four to eight weeks from deposit to the FOB Shenzhen handover.

Do wood lamps ship with batteries, and does that affect shipping?

Only if you specify a rechargeable configuration. A battery adds UN38.3 and MSDS transport documentation, and lithium cells change how the shipment can move. Our default Incoterm is FOB Shenzhen; CIF and DDP can be arranged per order.

Can I mix several platforms in one 500-unit order?

You can, but the minimum is counted for each model-and-colourway combination rather than for the order as a whole. Splitting 500 units across three platforms means three separate setups, so it carries the economics of three smaller runs — not those of a single 500-unit run.

What causes colour variation in solid wood, and can it be eliminated?

Natural variation in tone and grain between boards and between batches. It can be graded and bounded but not eliminated. Specify an acceptable range with pass/fail photographs — that is the wording that prevents a dispute later.

Which markets do your light platforms already cover?

The platform cards list the US, EU, Japan and the UAE as target markets, with CE · RoHS · FCC · PSE product marks issued per model. Confirm the exact combination for your model and destination at RFQ.

Can you make a fully custom sculpture at 100 units?

No. At 100 units you are customizing a validated platform. A fully custom structure with new function modules is a Pro-tier project at 1,000+ units, and it starts with design work rather than with a quote.

Who owns the design and tooling if we develop a new sculpture together?

It is agreed per project and stated in the contract — who pays for tooling, who owns it, and whether it can be moved. For platform-based orders at Lite and Slim, no new tooling is created at all.

What is the warranty on a wood light platform?

A 500-day term that begins when the goods ship and covers manufacturing defects only — longer than the one-year norm most suppliers in this category offer. Consumables and cosmetic wear fall outside it, and the detailed terms sit in the sales contract.
