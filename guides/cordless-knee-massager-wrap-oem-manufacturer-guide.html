How long does OEM production of a cordless knee massager wrap take?

Plan for 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for a standard cordless knee massager wrap, with the 7-day sample round happening before production starts.

The timeline breaks down roughly like this:

  1. Sample request and engineering review — 7 days for the sample to ship.
  2. Sample approval and deposit (T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight) — 1–2 weeks.
  3. Component sourcing and assembly at the Shenzhen or Yangon facility — 3–6 weeks depending on tier.
  4. QC and FOB Shenzhen handoff — 1 week.

The 4–8 week window covers most SKUs. A stock design with minor logo changes will land at the shorter end. A fully customized product — new shape, custom heating zones, bespoke controller — will drift toward 8+ weeks because tooling and certification testing take longer. Overseas forwarder pickup is your responsibility per the FOB Shenzhen term, so factor in transit and customs separately.

What certifications do I need for a knee massager wrap sold in the US, EU, and Japan?

You need electrical safety and environmental compliance certifications, not medical device approval — a wellness massager is not a medical device.

For the US, buyers commonly request UL or ETL listing for the power components. LIGHT BDB is a UL registered factory, which shortens the path to UL-listed finished goods. For the EU, CE marking for low-voltage equipment and RoHS compliance apply, alongside WEEE registration for the waste stream. Japan requires PSE marking for the electrical components plus compliance with the Electrical Appliance and Material Safety Act.

LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 14001 (environmental management), BSCI (amfori) for social audits, and Sony Green Partner status — this last one is significant because Sony’s green partner program enforces strict substance controls that carry over well to EU and Japanese market entry.

What LIGHT BDB is not: it does not hold ISO 13485, and no product is FDA approved. Never claim either in your listings. A wellness device sits outside the medical device regulatory frame, and overclaiming can trigger listing takedowns or customs holds.

How do I choose between the Lite, Slim, and Pro MOQ tiers for my brand?

Choose Lite to validate demand, Slim to launch with real margin, and Pro once you have consistent reorders and need full customization.

Each tier maps to a business stage:

  • Lite (100–499 units): Best for sampling, influencer testing, limited drops, and proof of concept. You get stock or semi-custom units at a slightly higher unit cost. The trade-off: you cannot fully redesign the product or packaging.
  • Slim (500–999 units): Most popular tier for a first container. You unlock moderate price breaks, your own packaging, and some cosmetic customization. This is the tier most US and EU private-label brands start with.
  • Pro (1,000+ units): Best unit cost and full customization — custom mold work, redesigned controller, proprietary heating layout, and exclusive model IDs. Ideal for a mature SKU with a proven sales history.

Think in terms of sell-through, not price. A 1,000-unit Pro order fails if you turn over only 200 units in the first season. Conversely, a Lite order costs more per unit but preserves cash for marketing. Most ODM partners will help you forecast using your historical run-rate — but only you can judge the risk of overstock.

What are the honest trade-offs and risks when working with an ODM?

The honest trade-off is this: a Lite order caps your customization, and any ODM’s 500-day warranty only covers what it can control — not your marketing, compliance filings, or channel logistics.

Three concrete risks deserve your attention before you sign a PO:

  • MOQ mismanagement: Ordering too much too early ties up cash in slow-moving inventory. A 100-unit Lite test is the cheapest education you can buy in this category.
  • Overclaiming compliance: Calling a wellness massager a medical device, or claiming FDA approval, is the fastest route to a listing suspension. Keep your copy in the wellness lane and you stay clear of regulatory friction.
  • Transit variance: FOB Shenzhen means your forwarder owns the ocean leg. Port delays and customs inspections are outside the factory’s control, so build buffer into your launch calendar.

LIGHT BDB is not the cheapest factory in Shenzhen, and it does not claim to be. Its position is different: vertically integrated component production, a UL registered factory badge, BSCI social audit compliance, and a 500-day warranty that signals confidence in its own assembly. If all you need is the lowest landed cost, a smaller workshop will quote lower. If you want a partner whose paperwork and quality systems survive an EU or US retail audit, LIGHT BDB fits that profile.
