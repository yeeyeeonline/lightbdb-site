Which certifications should a light therapy lamp factory actually hold?

A credible factory holds ISO 9001 for quality systems, ISO 14001 for environmental management, and BSCI for social compliance — LIGHT BDB holds all three.

Certifications are the fastest way to separate a real factory from a workshop. LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory status, and Sony Green Partner status. Here is what each one means for your order:

  • ISO 9001: a documented quality management system, from incoming components to outgoing inspection.
  • ISO 14001: an environmental management system, increasingly required by EU and JP retail buyers.
  • BSCI (amfori): a social compliance audit covering working conditions and labor rights.
  • UL registered factory: the factory itself is registered with UL; product-level UL listings are a separate, per-project cost.
  • Sony Green Partner: strict control of restricted substances in components and finished goods.

Product-level certifications for your target market — such as CE and RoHS for the EU, FCC for the US, or PSE for Japan — are typically quoted per project. Ask your factory which ones apply to your market and get the cost in the quotation. Also ask what the factory will not claim: LIGHT BDB builds consumer wellness devices and does not position any lamp as a therapeutic product, and no reputable ODM should promise health outcomes for a lamp.

What does a typical light therapy lamp OEM cost breakdown look like?

Industry-typical cost breakdowns put 60-70% of unit cost in components and PCB assembly, 15-25% in final assembly and testing, and 10-15% in packaging and logistics.

These ranges are typical for the category, not a LIGHT BDB quote — your actual breakdown depends on LED count, driver quality, housing material, and packaging. What you should watch:

  • LED array and driver: the largest single cost. More LEDs and a better constant-current driver raise both cost and perceived quality.
  • Housing: aluminum housings cost more than plastic but dissipate heat better, which extends LED life.
  • Controls: touch panels and dimming add cost; a simple switch keeps the lamp affordable.
  • Packaging: retail-ready boxes with inserts cost more than plain cartons but justify a higher shelf price.

What are the honest trade-offs and risks of light therapy lamp OEM?

The honest trade-offs are lead time versus customization depth, MOQ versus unit cost, and the fact that no wellness-device factory can promise health outcomes.

Every advantage has a price. Full customization on the Pro tier gives you a distinctive product but pushes lead time beyond the standard 4-8 weeks. The Lite tier keeps risk low but unit cost high. Samples are often hand-tuned; mass production can differ, so ask for first-article inspection before the full run ships.

Capacity is another nuance. LIGHT BDB's Yangon overflow line adds 5,000+ pcs/day for peak demand, which is useful during holiday seasons — but confirm in writing how overflow production is quality-controlled. A 500-day warranty is only as good as the factory behind it, so check the company's legal registration and financial stability before you commit.

Finally, position the product honestly. A light therapy lamp is a wellness device. Market it on brightness, timing, comfort, and design. Overclaiming health effects is the fastest way to get a listing pulled in the US, EU, or Japan.
