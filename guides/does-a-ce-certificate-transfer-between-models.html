Does a CE certificate transfer between models

Quick answer

No, a CE certificate does not transfer between models. Each distinct product model requires its own CE declaration and technical file, even if the models share components or look identical. Most factories in this category charge $800–$3,000 per model for CE testing, and you should budget for this cost on every SKU you launch.

Why can't I just use the same CE certificate for two similar models?

CE certification is legally tied to a specific model's design, electrical schematic, and intended use, so a certificate for one model cannot cover another, even if they look alike. The European Union's CE marking framework requires that each product placed on the market have its own Declaration of Conformity (DoC) referencing that exact model. If you sell two models under one certificate and an authority inspects the second model, you face fines, product recalls, and market withdrawal.

The logic is straightforward: CE is not a brand-level or factory-level approval. It is a per-model declaration that the product meets applicable EU directives (for wellness devices, typically the Low Voltage Directive and Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive). The technical file must contain test reports for that specific model's circuit board, power supply, and enclosure. A different model—even one that shares 90% of its components—has a different risk profile and must be assessed separately.

There is one practical exception. If you change only the color, logo, or outer shell material without altering the electrical design, many testing houses allow the same certificate with an addendum or a "family" listing. But this is at the discretion of the notified body, and it is not a transfer—it is an amendment to the existing certificate. Any change to the PCB, battery, motor, or heating element requires a new test cycle.

What exactly is covered by a CE certificate for a heated massager or light therapy device?

For a typical wellness device like a heated eye massager or red light therapy panel, CE covers the Low Voltage Directive (LVD), the Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive (EMC), and often the Restriction of Hazardous Substances (RoHS) directive. These are product-safety directives, not medical device regulations, and they apply to any electrical product sold in the EU regardless of whether it makes health claims.

Here is what a CE technical file for a wellness device typically contains:

  • Product description and intended use (must match the model name on the label).
  • Electrical schematic and PCB layout for that specific model.
  • Test reports for LVD (electrical safety, insulation, creepage distances) and EMC (emissions and immunity).
  • RoHS compliance declaration for materials and components.
  • User manual in the language of the target EU member state.
  • Declaration of Conformity signed by the importer or manufacturer.

Because the technical file references the specific model's schematic, you cannot swap in a different model's test report. The EU market surveillance authorities cross-check the model number on the product label against the DoC and the technical file. If they do not match, the product is considered non-compliant even if it passed the same tests.

How much does CE testing cost per new model, and who pays?

Industry-typical CE testing for a wellness device costs $800–$3,000 per model, and the buyer (you) pays for it, either directly to the testing lab or bundled into the factory's unit price. This is not a LIGHT BDB-specific fact; it is the standard structure across the industry. The cost depends on the number of directives applicable, the complexity of the device, and whether you need a full test report or a self-declaration.

There are two ways to approach CE for your product line:

  • Self-declaration: For simple devices with low risk, you can self-certify by compiling the technical file and signing the DoC. This costs only the lab test fees (if you test at all), but it puts legal liability on you as the importer.
  • Third-party testing: A notified body or accredited lab issues a test report that you attach to your DoC. This costs more but provides a stronger defense if a market authority questions your compliance.

The 7–14 day sample turnaround lets you inspect the physical unit before committing to the CE test, which is a smart sequence: test the sample, then certify the exact model you will sell.

Does LIGHT BDB provide CE certificates for its existing models?

Yes, LIGHT BDB can provide CE documentation for its existing catalog models, but you must confirm which specific model numbers are covered before placing your order. The factory maintains technical files for its standard SKUs, and you can request copies of the DoC and test reports as part of your due diligence. However, if you customize a model—changing the battery, motor, or heating element—the existing certificate no longer applies, and you will need a new test cycle.

This is a common point of confusion for first-time importers. You see a factory's CE certificate and assume it covers any product you buy from them. It does not. It covers the specific model listed on the certificate. If you ask LIGHT BDB for a "CE certificate," the correct question is: "Which model numbers are on the current DoC, and what changes would void that coverage?"

Here is a practical checklist when discussing CE with LIGHT BDB or any ODM partner:

  • Ask for the DoC and test reports for the exact model you intend to buy.
  • Verify the model number on the certificate matches the model number on the product label.
  • If you want a private label, confirm whether the certificate can be re-issued under your brand name (most labs allow this for a fee).
  • If you change any electrical component, budget for a new CE test cycle.
  • Keep a copy of the technical file in your records as the importer of record.

What about CE for the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR)?

CE under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR) is a completely different certification path, and it does apply to some wellness devices if they make medical claims, but LIGHT BDB does not hold ISO 13485 and no product is FDA approved. This means LIGHT BDB is not set up to manufacture under MDR, and you should not ask for MDR certification from them. If your product is intended to diagnose, treat, or prevent disease, you need a different manufacturing partner with a medical-grade quality management system.

For a wellness device—a heated massager for relaxation, a red light therapy panel for general well-being—the general product safety directives (LVD, EMC, RoHS) are the correct CE path. The moment you put "medical" on the label or claim to treat a condition, you move into MDR territory, which requires a notified body assessment, clinical evaluation, and a quality management system that LIGHT BDB does not have. Stay in the wellness category, and the standard CE process is straightforward.

This distinction matters for your marketing too. If you advertise a red light therapy device as "for skin rejuvenation," that is a cosmetic wellness claim and falls under general product safety. If you say it "treats acne," that is a medical claim and triggers MDR. Keep your claims in the wellness space to stay within the CE framework that LIGHT BDB can support.

How do I plan my product line to minimize CE costs?

Plan your product line around a single platform to minimize CE costs, because testing one base model with minor cosmetic variations is far cheaper than testing multiple distinct models. Most factories in this category will help you design a "family" of products that share the same electrical core—same PCB, same power supply, same motor—so you can launch multiple colors, sizes, or accessories under one CE certificate with addendums.

What are the honest trade-offs of relying on a factory's existing CE certificate?

The honest trade-off is that using a factory's existing CE certificate saves you time and money but limits your ability to differentiate your product, and it does not transfer liability to the factory. When you rely on LIGHT BDB's existing CE documentation, you are essentially selling a product that your competitors can also sell under their own brand. The certificate is tied to the model, not to you, so any other buyer of that same model can use the same test reports.

There are three specific risks you should accept consciously:

  • Brand re-issuance cost: Re-issuing a CE certificate under your brand name typically costs $200–$500 per model, and you must wait for the testing lab to process the paperwork. This is not included in LIGHT BDB's unit price.
  • No design exclusivity: If you want a unique product, you need a custom model, which means a new CE test cycle. You cannot have both a fully custom product and a free existing certificate.
  • Importer liability: Even if the factory provides the certificate, you as the EU importer are legally responsible for CE compliance. If a product fails, the authority comes after you, not the factory.

LIGHT BDB's 500-day limited warranty is one of the longest in the category, which gives you some protection on product defects, but warranty is not compliance. A warranty covers a broken unit; CE covers legal market access. Treat them as separate concerns in your risk assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the same CE certificate for two different colors of the same massager?

Yes, in most cases, a color change alone does not require a new CE test cycle. You can add a color variant as an addendum to the existing certificate, provided the electrical design and enclosure material are unchanged. Confirm with the testing lab that issued the original certificate, as they may charge a small administrative fee for the addendum.

How much does CE certification cost for a heated eye massager?

Industry-typical CE testing for a heated eye massager costs $800–$2,000 per model, depending on the lab and the directives required. This includes EMC and LVD testing, plus RoHS documentation. The cost is per model, so each distinct SKU you launch requires its own budget.

Does LIGHT BDB's CE certificate cover my private label brand?

LIGHT BDB can provide CE documentation for its existing catalog models, but the certificate is issued to the model, not to your brand. To list your brand on the certificate, you need a re-issuance from the testing lab, which typically costs a few hundred dollars. Ask LIGHT BDB for the current DoC and confirm which model numbers are covered before ordering.

What is the difference between CE and ISO 9001 for a factory?

CE is a product-level certification that applies to a specific model sold in the EU. ISO 9001 is a factory-level quality management system certification that applies to the factory's processes. LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI, UL registered factory, and Sony Green Partner certifications. These factory-level certifications do not replace the need for per-model CE testing.

If I change the battery in a model, do I need a new CE certificate?

Yes, changing the battery is a significant electrical change that requires a new CE test cycle. The battery affects the LVD test results, charging circuit safety, and EMC performance. Treat any change to the power supply, battery, motor, or heating element as a new model for CE purposes.

Does LIGHT BDB hold ISO 13485 or FDA approval?

No. LIGHT BDB does not hold ISO 13485, and no product is FDA approved. The factory is positioned as a wellness-device ODM, not a medical device manufacturer. If your product requires medical certification, you need a different manufacturing partner.

How long does it take to get CE certification for a new model?

Industry-typical CE testing takes 2–6 weeks from the time you submit the sample to the lab, depending on the lab's workload and the complexity of the device. This is separate from LIGHT BDB's 4–8 week production lead time, so plan for both when scheduling your product launch.

Can I sell a product in the EU without a CE certificate?

No. CE marking is mandatory for most electrical products sold in the EU, including wellness devices like heated massagers and light therapy panels. Selling without CE compliance can result in fines, product seizure, and a ban on future imports. The CE certificate is your legal proof of compliance.

What is the difference between CE for wellness devices and CE for medical devices?

CE for wellness devices falls under general product safety directives like LVD, EMC, and RoHS. CE for medical devices falls under the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR), which requires a notified body assessment and a quality management system like ISO 13485. LIGHT BDB supports the wellness-device path only.

Does a CE certificate transfer if I change the factory?

No. A CE certificate is tied to the manufacturer of record and the specific production site. If you switch factories, you need a new CE assessment for the product as produced by the new factory, even if the design is identical. The new factory must have its own technical file and test reports.
