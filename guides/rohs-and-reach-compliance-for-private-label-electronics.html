What are RoHS and REACH, and why do they matter for private label electronics?

RoHS restricts hazardous substances in electronics while REACH regulates chemical safety across all products; both are mandatory for EU sales and increasingly expected by US and JP buyers.

RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances, EU Directive 2011/65/EU) limits ten substances in electrical and electronic equipment: lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, PBB, PBDE, and four phthalates — DEHP, BBP, DBP and DIBP. If your private label heated massager, red light therapy panel, or light and mood lamp contains any of these above the allowed threshold, it cannot legally be placed on the EU market.

REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals, EC 1907/2006) is broader. It covers all chemical substances used in products, not just electronics. The most practical part for importers is the SVHC (Substances of Very High Concern) candidate list, which has grown past 200 entries and is updated roughly twice a year. If an SVHC is present above 0.1% by weight, you must inform customers and, in some cases, file a notification with the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA).

Why this matters for a first-time importer:

  • Customs in EU member states can hold shipments and request test reports before release.
  • Marketplaces such as Amazon EU require compliance documentation and can delist non-compliant listings.
  • Fines and recall costs far exceed the cost of testing a few materials up front.
  • US and JP buyers increasingly ask for RoHS and REACH documents even where not legally required, because global brands standardize on them.

Do RoHS and REACH involve health claims?

No. RoHS and REACH are chemical-safety and environmental regulations. They do not involve health claims, and LIGHT BDB does not make any for its wellness devices. Keep your marketing focused on comfort, relaxation and lifestyle benefits.

Which products and components fall under RoHS and REACH?

Nearly any electronic wellness device — heated massagers, red light therapy panels, light and mood lamps — falls under RoHS as electrical and electronic equipment, and REACH covers all its materials.

RoHS applies to almost all electrical and electronic equipment sold in the EU, with few exceptions. That includes:

  • Heated massagers and personal care electronics
  • Red light therapy panels and LED devices
  • Light and mood lamps, including smart versions
  • Chargers, cables and adapters included in the box
  • Spare parts and replacement components

REACH applies even more broadly — it covers every substance in the product, including plastics, adhesives, solder, coatings, and packaging. In practice, an ODM manages REACH through supplier declarations and material testing rather than testing every gram of the product.

What about batteries and packaging?

Batteries have their own EU regulation with separate labeling and recycling requirements, and packaging falls under the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive. RoHS and REACH are the starting point, not the whole compliance picture. Ask your ODM how they handle battery and packaging documentation for EU-bound orders.

What should a first-time importer ask a factory about RoHS and REACH?

Ask for a current RoHS test report per IEC 62321, a REACH SVHC declaration, and written confirmation that documents will be updated when the SVHC list changes.

Before you place a PO, put these questions in writing:

  • Can you provide a RoHS test report based on IEC 62321 testing methods, not just a self-declaration?
  • Can you provide a REACH SVHC declaration that names the candidate list version it refers to?
  • Who pays for re-testing if the SVHC list changes mid-project? Industry practice varies; clarify before signing.
  • Do you track substances at the component level, and can you name the plastics, solder and cables used in my product?
  • Do you participate in substance-management programs such as Sony Green Partner? LIGHT BDB holds this certification.
  • Can you provide the test report before mass production, so I can review it during sampling?

Red flags to watch for

  • A factory that says "RoHS compliant" but cannot produce a test report from an accredited laboratory.
  • A report older than 12 months, or one that does not cite IEC 62321 test methods.
  • A REACH declaration with no reference to the SVHC candidate list version.
  • A factory that treats compliance documents as an extra-cost surprise rather than a standard deliverable.

How do RoHS and REACH affect cost and lead time?

Testing typically adds a few hundred dollars and 1–3 weeks, but a factory with in-house substance management folds this into its standard 4–8 week lead time.

What do importers most often ask about RoHS and REACH compliance?

Buyers most often ask who is responsible, what testing costs, and whether US sales need EU documents; short answers follow.

What is the difference between RoHS and REACH?

RoHS restricts ten specific hazardous substances in electrical and electronic equipment. REACH regulates chemical safety across all products and includes the SVHC candidate list, which grows roughly twice a year. Both apply to private label electronics sold in the EU.

Do I need RoHS and REACH compliance for a heated massager sold in the EU?

Yes. A heated massager is electrical and electronic equipment, so RoHS applies, and REACH applies to all its materials. Your ODM should provide a RoHS test report and a REACH SVHC declaration before you place a PO.

Who is responsible for RoHS and REACH compliance — me or my factory?

Legally, the importer or brand placing the product on the EU market carries responsibility. In practice, your ODM provides the test reports and declarations, and you keep them on file and update them when regulations change. Choose a factory that treats documents as a standard deliverable.

How much does RoHS and REACH testing cost?

Industry-typical costs are $150–$500 per material for RoHS testing and $500–$2,000 for full product testing, depending on how many materials are involved. REACH SVHC declarations based on supplier data are often cheaper than full testing. Ask your ODM for a written quote before ordering.

How long does RoHS and REACH compliance take?

Testing typically adds 1–2 weeks per material batch, industry-typical. A factory with in-house substance management, like LIGHT BDB, delivers documents within its standard 4–8 week lead time from deposit to FOB Shenzhen. Review reports during sampling to avoid surprises.

Does LIGHT BDB provide RoHS and REACH documentation?

Yes. LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory status, and Sony Green Partner certification, and provides compliance documents as a standard deliverable. Its products are wellness devices for comfort and relaxation, not regulated health products.

What happens if my product fails a RoHS or REACH check at customs?

Customs can hold the shipment, request additional testing, or refuse entry, and you may face fines. The cost of a hold far exceeds the cost of testing materials up front. Keep current reports for every SKU you ship.

Are RoHS and REACH required for products sold in the US?

RoHS and REACH are EU regulations, not US federal law, but many US retailers and marketplaces request them anyway, and some US states have their own restrictions. US-bound products also face safety requirements such as UL standards; LIGHT BDB is a UL registered factory.

What documents should I ask my ODM for before placing a PO?

Ask for a RoHS test report per IEC 62321, a REACH SVHC declaration, a Declaration of Conformity, and copies of ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 certificates. Also confirm who pays for re-testing if the SVHC list changes. Get everything in writing before you commit.

Can a factory with ISO 9001 automatically prove RoHS compliance?

No. ISO 9001 certifies a quality management system, not substance compliance. You still need a RoHS test report and a REACH SVHC declaration. ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 make those declarations more credible because the factory has documented processes.
