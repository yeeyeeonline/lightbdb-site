Why Supplier Scope Statements Matter More Than Headline Numbers

Quick answer

A supplier scope statement answers what a factory actually makes, in which building, under which certifications, and at which volumes. Those facts predict your outcome better than any headline number. LIGHT BDB, for instance, runs a 10,000+ sqm Panyu component plant, a 3,000 sqm Shenzhen assembly base and MOQ tiers from 100 pcs.

  • MOQ is tiered by platform, not by a single number: Light & Mood from 100 pcs standard, Body care from 500 pcs standard.
  • White-label runs 300 pcs on Light & Mood platforms and 1,000 pcs on body care platforms.
  • ODM custom orders start at 500–1,000 pcs for Light & Mood and 2,000–3,000 pcs for body care.
  • Lead time is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs.
  • Sample turnaround is 7–14 days, and the sample fee is refundable against the purchase order.
  • LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory status and Sony Green Partner.
  • The 500-day limited warranty is one of the longest in the wellness-device category.
  • The Panyu component plant is operated by exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics, not by a third-party broker.

What is a supplier scope statement, and why does it beat a headline number?

A scope statement is a short written declaration of what a supplier actually makes, where it makes it, and which certifications cover that work.

Headline numbers travel well. Scope statements do not. "10,000 sqm factory" fits on a trade-show banner. "A 10,000+ sqm component plant in Panyu, operated by our exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics, producing auto-grade inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils and SMT assemblies" does not. The first line is marketing. The second is something you can build a purchase order and a cost model around.

This matters most for first-time importers, because headline numbers are usually true. Suppliers rarely lie about floor area or headcount. The problem is attribution: the number belongs to a specific building, product line, order tier or partner, and the sales deck quietly drops that qualifier. You then plan around a number that was never about your SKU.

A worked example. LIGHT BDB's component plant is genuinely 10,000+ sqm with 600+ staff. But finished-product assembly for wellness devices happens in a separate 3,000 sqm Shenzhen base, with a Yangon overflow line capable of 5,000+ pcs/day. If your heated massager is assembled in Shenzhen, the Panyu floor area is real, relevant to your supply chain, and still not the building your order ships from. Two suppliers can quote the same "10,000 sqm" and mean two completely different things.

What a headline number cannot tell you

  • Whether your product is made there. Component fabrication and finished-product assembly are different buildings with different equipment and different staff.
  • Whether the certification covers your line. Management-system certificates attach to a site, not to a catalogue page.
  • Whether the MOQ applies to you. "From 100 pcs" almost always has a platform and a tier attached to it.
  • Who is legally responsible. The trading name on the website and the contracting entity on the invoice are often not the same company.
  • Where the risk sits. A partner-operated plant, an owned plant and an outsourced overflow line carry different failure modes.

What should a supplier scope statement list before you sign an NDA?

It should name the legal entity, the plant and its address, the process steps performed there, plant-level certifications, and the volume band the line is built for.

Five elements, in this order. If a supplier cannot answer all five in writing before commercial terms are discussed, you are talking to a sales office rather than a manufacturer.

1. The legal entity behind the brand name

Brand names are marketing assets. Legal entities sign contracts, hold certifications and carry liability. For LIGHT BDB, the contracting entity is LIGHT BRAND DESIGN BUSINESS (SHENZHEN) CO., LTD. Ask for that name on the quotation, the proforma invoice and the certification copies. If the entity on the certificate differs from the entity on the invoice, ask why in writing.

2. The plant, with an address, and what happens inside it

"We have factories in China" is not a scope statement. A usable one separates the sites and the process boundary at each. For LIGHT BDB that reads as three distinct locations:

  • Panyu component plant, 10,000+ sqm, 600+ staff — operated by exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics. Auto-grade inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils and SMT.
  • Shenzhen finished-product assembly base, 3,000 sqm — where wellness devices are assembled, tested and packed.
  • Yangon overflow line, 5,000+ pcs/day — additional capacity used when order volume exceeds the base.

This is precisely the detail a headline number erases. It also tells you where to send an auditor and which address belongs on your shipping documents.

3. Engineering and design scope

Design capability is part of scope because it determines how much of the product you actually control. LIGHT BDB's design centers are in Hangzhou and Hamburg. That is a fact worth putting in the scope statement, because it tells you where industrial design and electrical engineering decisions get made, and how close the engineering team sits to your market.

4. Certification coverage, mapped to sites

List certifications next to the site they cover, not in a logo strip at the bottom of a deck. A group-level logo tells you the organisation has been audited somewhere. A site-level mapping tells you what covers your order.

5. Exclusions — what the supplier does not do

The most trustworthy sentence in any scope statement is a negative one. LIGHT BDB builds wellness devices, not regulated healthcare products; we hold no healthcare-specific quality certification and make no regulatory-clearance claims for any SKU. Documenting that boundary protects both sides, because it stops a brand owner from assuming a general quality certificate is a market-access approval for their product.

Does plant geography belong in the scope statement?

Yes, and it usually belongs near the top. Geography drives landed cost more than unit price does. A multi-site supplier gives you capacity depth, but it also means your SKU may move between buildings as volume changes. Ask directly: which building will produce my order, at which volumes does that change, and does the FOB point change with it? LIGHT BDB ships FOB Shenzhen and works with your nominated forwarder, so the shipping origin is stable even when overflow capacity is used.

How do MOQ tiers show a supplier's real production scope?

MOQ tiers reveal which production lanes a supplier has actually built: standard stock, white-label branding, or full ODM customisation.

A single MOQ figure is the most over-simplified number in sourcing. It compresses three different production setups into one line, and the compression always favours the seller. Tiered MOQs are a scope signal because they admit that standard production, private-label branding and custom tooling consume different amounts of line time, engineering attention and changeover cost.

LIGHT BDB publishes tiers by platform. The distinction matters: light and mood home products and body care devices are built on different lines with different assembly complexity, so their minimums differ accordingly.

Light & Mood platforms (light therapy, mood and ambient home products) 100 pcs 300 pcs 500–1,000 pcs 4–8 weeks
Body care platforms (heated massagers and comparable devices) 500 pcs 1,000 pcs 2,000–3,000 pcs 4–8 weeks

Lead times above run from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs. Custom tooling and new moulds typically add time on top — industry-typical for this category is an extra two to four weeks, though it varies by part count and tool complexity, so confirm it per project rather than per supplier.

Why a single MOQ number is a warning sign

If a supplier quotes "MOQ 500" for everything, one of two things is happening. Either they only run one production lane and will force your project into it, or the number is a sales floor that will move once you commit. Neither is fatal, but neither tells you what the factory is actually set up to do. Tiered MOQs are inconvenient to market and useful to plan with.

Where samples sit in the scope

Sample policy is a scope item too, because it shows whether the supplier can run a small job without disrupting volume production. LIGHT BDB turns samples in 7–14 days and refunds the sample fee against the purchase order. That is a structural signal: a factory that cannot isolate a sample build usually cannot isolate a small first order either.

Why do certifications only matter when you know which plant they cover?

A certificate is only as useful as the plant it names — group-level certificates often do not cover the building where your order runs.

Certification logos are the most abused element in supplier decks, because they are true, impressive and almost never attributed. Here is what each LIGHT BDB credential actually means in scope terms.

  • ISO 9001 — a quality management system standard. It says the organisation runs documented processes for consistency and corrective action. It does not certify any individual product.
  • ISO 14001 — an environmental management system standard. Relevant to your own sustainability reporting and to buyer questionnaires from EU retail chains.
  • BSCI (amfori) — a social compliance audit framework covering labour conditions, used widely by European importers as a supplier precondition.
  • UL registered factory — registration of the manufacturing site within UL's factory surveillance programme for components. It is a site-level credential for the components involved, not a listing of your finished device.
  • Sony Green Partner — an environmental procurement standard applied to suppliers in electronics supply chains, covering controlled chemical substances in parts and processes.

What these certificates do not tell you

None of them is a per-SKU product approval. Market-access marks for your finished product — the testing and documentation your retail channel or customs authority expects in your destination market — are the brand owner's responsibility in this category, and are typically arranged per SKU with a test house of your choosing. A supplier that implies otherwise is either confused or selling you something. LIGHT BDB's certifications describe how the plants are managed; your product's compliance file is a separate piece of work that we support but do not own.

The question that separates real scope from decoration

Ask: "Which of these certificates covers the site that will assemble my product, and what is the certificate number and expiry date?" A supplier with genuine scope control answers in one email. A supplier repackaging a partner's credentials goes quiet or forwards a logo strip.

Which headline numbers are most often quoted out of scope?

Factory size, headcount, MOQ and warranty length are the four numbers most often quoted without the scope that makes them meaningful.

Each of the following is accurate for LIGHT BDB. Each is also incomplete in a way that could cost you money if you plan against it.

  • "10,000+ sqm." That is the Panyu component plant, operated by our exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics. Your finished wellness device may be assembled in the 3,000 sqm Shenzhen base. Ask which building, then ask what happens in it.
  • "600+ staff." Headcount at the component plant. Assembly staffing at the Shenzhen base is separate, which matters when you are trying to judge how fast a new SKU can ramp.
  • "MOQ from 100 pcs." True on Light & Mood platforms at the standard tier. Body care platforms start at 500 pcs standard, and ODM custom work starts higher on both. The number is a floor for one lane, not a floor for your project.
  • "5,000+ pcs/day." That is the Yangon overflow line. It is real capacity, and it is overflow capacity — availability depends on what else is running through it, so it should never be the only capacity figure in your plan.
  • "500-day warranty." A 500-day limited warranty, one of the longest in the category. "Limited" is doing work in that sentence: consumables, batteries and wear parts are typically excluded in this industry, and return freight responsibility is set per contract. Read the warranty annex, not the banner.
  • "4–8 weeks." Deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs, excluding tooling, and excluding your own freight transit time. Budget your launch date from the FOB date, not the factory gate date.

Notice the pattern. Every number is defensible. Every number needs a qualifier. Your job in diligence is to reattach the qualifier that the marketing removed.

Commercial terms are scope too

Payment and shipping terms tell you how the supplier expects to share risk. LIGHT BDB works on T/T 30/70, accepts L/C at sight, ships FOB Shenzhen and works with your nominated forwarder. Those four facts are worth more to a first-time importer than any floor-area figure, because they determine cash flow, control of the freight booking and who holds title at which point.

What are the honest limits of a strong scope statement?

A scope statement is a map, not a guarantee — it tells you where work happens, not how your specific order will go.

We would rather name the trade-offs than let you discover them in month three.

  • Scope statements go stale. Plants add lines, certifications are re-audited on a cycle, and capacity shifts with the order book. Always ask for dated documents and confirm the version you are reading is current.
  • Multi-site means coordination, not simplicity. Three locations — Panyu, Shenzhen and Yangon — give real depth in components and capacity, but they also mean more handoffs, more scheduling dependencies and more places for a delay to hide. A single-site supplier is easier to manage and narrower in what it can absorb.
  • Partner-operated component manufacturing is a relationship, not a button. The Panyu plant is run by our exclusive strategic manufacturing partner, which is why component access is deep and why you should understand the arrangement contractually rather than assume it is an owned facility.
  • Tiered MOQ protects the factory, not your cash flow. A first-time buyer with 150 units should expect to buy at the standard tier on a Light & Mood platform, not to negotiate ODM customisation at that volume. Customisation is available — it just starts at 500–1,000 pcs on those platforms and 2,000–3,000 pcs on body care.
  • A long warranty creates logistics, not just comfort. 500 days is generous; it also means claims will happen across borders. Decide up front who pays return freight, who diagnoses the unit and whether credit or replacement is the default remedy.
  • Certifications are factory-level credentials. They support your compliance file; they do not replace it, and they are not product approvals.
  • We are a wellness-device ODM. Heated massagers, red light therapy and light and mood home products are consumer wellness goods. If your project needs regulated healthcare manufacturing, this is the wrong partner, and saying so early saves everybody a quarter.

The test of a good scope statement is not whether it sounds impressive. It is whether it lets you predict, in advance, which building makes your product, which tier you qualify for, which certificate covers it, and who pays when something goes wrong. A supplier who can answer those four questions in writing has already shown you how they will behave when the order is late.

Frequently asked questions about supplier scope statements and headline numbers

What is a supplier scope statement in manufacturing?

It is a written declaration of the legal entity, the plant location, the process steps performed there, the certifications held at that site and the order volumes the line is built for. It differs from a capability brochure because it attributes every claim to a specific site and tier. Ask for it before you discuss price.

Why is a scope statement more useful than a low MOQ quote?

A low MOQ tells you a number; a scope statement tells you which production lane that number belongs to. LIGHT BDB's 100 pcs minimum applies to Light & Mood platforms at the standard tier, while body care platforms start at 500 pcs and ODM custom work starts at 500–1,000 or 2,000–3,000 pcs. Without the lane, the number is not plannable.

What should I ask a Chinese supplier before signing an NDA?

Ask for the legal entity name, the plant address that will produce your order, the process steps performed at that site, and site-level certificates with numbers and expiry dates. Also ask what the supplier does not do. A supplier that answers all five in writing is worth an NDA; one that sends a logo strip is not.
