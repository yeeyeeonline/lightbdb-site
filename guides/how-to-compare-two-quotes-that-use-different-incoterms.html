How to Compare Two Quotes That Use Different Incoterms

Quick answer

To compare quotes with different Incoterms, convert every quote to a single landed-cost basis by adding freight, insurance, and destination fees to each FOB, EXW, or CIF price. A typical difference between FOB Shenzhen and DDP to a US warehouse ranges from $1.50 to $4.00 per unit for wellness devices, depending on weight and volume.

  • FOB Shenzhen means the seller covers costs until goods are loaded on the vessel; buyer pays ocean freight, insurance, and destination charges.
  • EXW places all transport and export costs on the buyer; rarely the best choice for first-time importers due to hidden handling fees.
  • CIF includes freight and insurance to the destination port but not destination handling, customs clearance, or inland delivery.
  • DDP is the most inclusive term; seller bears all costs and risks until goods arrive at the buyer's named destination.
  • LIGHT BDB quotes FOB Shenzhen as standard and works with your nominated forwarder; T/T 30/70 and L/C at sight are accepted.
  • Add 8-15% of the FOB value for freight and insurance on a typical 20-foot container from Shenzhen to a US West Coast port.
  • Industry-typical MOQ tiers for wellness devices: Lite 100-499 units, Slim 500-999 units, Pro 1,000+ units for best unit cost.
  • LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty and 4-8 week lead time from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs.

What exactly do different Incoterms mean for my final unit cost?

Incoterms define who pays for and controls each leg of shipping, so the same factory price can differ by 10-25% in total cost depending on which term you choose. The term itself does not change the factory price; it changes how much of the logistics cost sits on your side of the ledger.

When a supplier quotes FOB Shenzhen at $12.00 per unit and another quotes CIF Los Angeles at $13.20, the second quote may actually be cheaper once you add the destination fees the first quote leaves out. The reverse can also be true if the CIF quote includes a high insurance premium or a less efficient shipping route.

Here is what each common term covers in practice:

  • EXW (Ex Works): Seller makes goods available at their factory. You arrange and pay for everything: export packing, trucking to port, customs clearance, ocean freight, insurance, destination clearance, and inland delivery.
  • FOB (Free On Board): Seller delivers goods onto your nominated vessel at the origin port. You pay ocean freight, insurance, and all destination-side costs. This is the most common term for Shenzhen factories.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): Seller pays for freight and insurance to the destination port. You pay for destination handling, customs clearance, duties, and inland transport.
  • DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): Seller bears all costs and risks until goods arrive at your named destination, including duties and taxes. Highest unit price but lowest buyer logistics burden.

For a first-time importer, FOB is usually the safest starting point because it gives you control over the freight forwarder and lets you see exactly what logistics costs you are paying.

How do I calculate a true apples-to-apples comparison between two Incoterms?

Convert every quote to a landed cost per unit by adding all logistics and duty costs to the quoted price, then compare the totals side by side. Landed cost is the only number that matters for margin decisions.

Use this formula for each quote:

  1. Start with the quoted unit price under the given Incoterm.
  2. Add the per-unit share of ocean or air freight from origin to your port.
  3. Add insurance (typically 0.3-0.5% of the cargo value for ocean freight).
  4. Add destination port handling fees, customs brokerage, and any duties or taxes.
  5. Add inland trucking from the destination port to your warehouse.
  6. Add any currency conversion or bank transfer fees if paying in a different currency.

For a 20-foot container from Shenzhen to a US West Coast port, industry-typical ocean freight runs $1,800-$3,500 depending on season and carrier. If you are shipping 5,000 units of a heated eye massager in that container, the freight adds $0.36-$0.70 per unit. Insurance adds roughly $0.04-$0.06 per unit at typical rates.

Destination-side costs—customs clearance, port handling, and inland trucking—typically add another $0.50-$1.50 per unit for wellness devices. A DDP quote that looks $1.00 higher per unit than an FOB quote may actually be $0.50 cheaper once you add these figures to the FOB side.

Which Incoterm is usually the best value for a first-time wellness device importer?

FOB Shenzhen is the most cost-transparent choice for first-time importers because it separates factory cost from logistics cost and lets you shop for freight independently. Most Shenzhen ODM factories, including LIGHT BDB, quote FOB as standard and work with your nominated forwarder.

Here is why FOB tends to win for first-time buyers:

  • You see the factory price clearly, without hidden logistics markups baked into the unit cost.
  • You can compare freight quotes from multiple forwarders and choose the best rate.
  • You control the shipping timeline and can expedite if needed without renegotiating with the factory.
  • Most factories are experienced with FOB and can provide accurate export documentation.

DDP is attractive when you want a single all-in price and do not have a freight forwarder relationship yet. The trade-off is that the seller's logistics markup is typically 10-20% above what you would pay by arranging freight yourself. For a first order, the convenience may be worth it; for repeat orders, moving to FOB usually saves money.

EXW is almost never the best choice for a first-time importer because you must handle export customs clearance in China, which requires a local agent and adds complexity without meaningful savings.

What hidden costs should I look for when comparing quotes with different Incoterms?

The hidden costs that distort Incoterm comparisons are destination handling fees, customs brokerage, duties, and inland transport—none of which appear in the quoted unit price under FOB or CIF. These fees can add $0.50-$2.00 per unit depending on your port and warehouse location.

Common hidden costs to ask about:

  • Destination port handling: Terminal handling charges, container fees, and documentation fees at your arrival port.
  • Customs brokerage: Fees charged by your broker to clear the shipment, typically $100-$300 per entry.
  • Duties and taxes: US import duties on wellness devices typically range from 0-5.4%, depending on the HS code classification.
  • Inland trucking: Cost to move the container from port to your warehouse, which varies by distance.
  • Demurrage and detention: Fees if the container sits at the port longer than the free time allowed by the carrier.
  • Currency conversion: If the quote is in USD but your bank charges a spread, factor that into the comparison.

When a supplier quotes CIF, ask them to itemize the freight and insurance components. Sometimes the CIF quote includes a higher insurance rate than you would buy yourself, or the freight is based on a more expensive carrier than necessary.

How do MOQ tiers and lead times affect the Incoterm comparison?

MOQ tiers change the per-unit freight cost because a smaller shipment uses less container space, so compare quotes at the same quantity level before factoring Incoterms. A quote at 500 units with FOB Shenzhen may have a higher per-unit freight cost than a quote at 1,000 units with CIF, simply because the freight is spread over fewer units.

LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers are typical for the wellness device category:

  • Lite (100-499 units): Best for sampling and limited drops; higher unit cost and higher per-unit freight.
  • Slim (500-999 units): The most popular tier; balances unit cost with manageable inventory risk.
  • Pro (1,000+ units): Best unit cost and full customization options; freight cost per unit drops significantly.

Lead time also interacts with Incoterms. LIGHT BDB's standard lead time is 4-8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen. If you choose DDP, add 2-4 weeks for ocean transit and customs clearance. If you need goods faster, air freight can cut transit to 5-7 days but adds $3-$6 per unit for a typical wellness device.

When comparing quotes, ask each supplier for their MOQ tier at the same quantity and confirm the lead time in writing. A quote with a faster lead time may justify a higher unit price if it lets you hit a seasonal sales window.

What should I ask each supplier before making a final decision?

Ask each supplier for a full cost breakdown including the Incoterm, the MOQ tier, the lead time, and the warranty terms—then compare those four data points side by side. The lowest unit price is rarely the best overall deal once you factor in lead time, warranty, and logistics complexity.

Here are the questions to send to each supplier:

  1. What is your exact Incoterm and what is included in that price?
  2. Can you provide an itemized breakdown of freight and insurance if quoting CIF or DDP?
  3. What is your MOQ at the quantity I am considering, and how does the unit price change at higher tiers?
  4. What is your confirmed lead time from deposit to shipment under this Incoterm?
  5. What certifications does your factory hold, and can you share the certificates?
  6. What is your warranty period and what does it cover?
  7. What payment terms do you accept, and are there any bank or transfer fees?
  8. Can you provide references from buyers in my region who have imported similar products?

LIGHT BDB's standard answers to these questions are: FOB Shenzhen, T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight, 4-8 week lead time, 500-day limited warranty, and ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI, UL registered factory, and Sony Green Partner certifications. Sample turnaround is 7-14 days with the sample fee refundable against a PO.

What are the honest trade-offs when choosing between Incoterms?

The honest trade-off is that FOB gives you cost transparency but requires you to manage logistics, while DDP gives you convenience but hides the logistics markup inside the unit price. Neither is objectively better; the right choice depends on your experience level and whether you have a trusted freight forwarder.

Here are the real risks to weigh:

  • FOB risk: You are responsible for shipping delays, customs issues, and unexpected destination fees. If you have no forwarder relationship, the learning curve is steep.
  • DDP risk: You pay a markup for the seller's logistics management, and you have less visibility into where your money goes. If the seller's forwarder underperforms, you have less recourse.
  • CIF risk: The seller controls the freight but you still handle destination clearance, so you get the worst of both worlds—less control and no all-in price.
  • EXW risk: You must handle Chinese export customs, which typically requires a local agent and adds cost and complexity for no savings.

For a first order, many importers choose DDP to reduce risk and learn the process, then switch to FOB for repeat orders once they have a forwarder relationship. This phased approach is practical and common in the wellness device category.
