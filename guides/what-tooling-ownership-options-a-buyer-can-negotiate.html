What tooling ownership options can a buyer negotiate with an ODM partner?

Three ownership models dominate consumer-electronics ODM contracts: factory-owned tooling, cost-shared molds, and fully buyer-owned tooling with transfer rights, each with different renewal and exit terms.

Tooling — the injection molds, stamping dies, and assembly fixtures that shape your product — is the most asset-heavy part of a private-label program. The contract language around it determines who can move, modify, or repurpose that asset. As a buyer you are not choosing a mold; you are choosing who holds the keys to your product's physical design.

Factory-owned tooling (the default)

In the most common arrangement, the factory pays for and retains title to the tooling. The buyer pays nothing up front, and the mold cost is recovered through per-unit pricing over the life of the order book. Ownership stays with the factory, and the buyer's protection comes from an exclusivity clause that prevents the factory from selling your design to another brand.

This is the lowest-risk entry point for a first-time importer. You lose the up-front capital exposure, and the factory has a commercial reason to keep the mold maintained. The trade-off is dependency: if you stop ordering, the mold sits in their plant, and you have no legal claim to move it.

Cost-shared tooling

Cost-shared or 50/50 arrangements are a mid-point. You and the factory split the tooling cost, you get a lower per-unit price because part of the mold fee is already paid, and the contract typically triggers full ownership transfer once your share is fully amortized through order volume.

Cost-share is the most common negotiation outcome for brands that expect to grow but do not yet want to write a five-figure check on day one. The key negotiation point is the trigger event: does ownership transfer after a fixed dollar amount of orders, or after a fixed period? Industry-typical triggers are 12-24 months or a cumulative order value equal to the mold cost.

Buyer-owned tooling

At the top end, you pay the full mold fee up front, hold title, and the factory stores and maintains the mold as a contracted service. You can move the tool to another factory at the end of the program, though transport, re-qualification, and setup costs are your responsibility.

Buyer-owned tooling is the right call for established brands running multi-year programs across multiple SKUs — the mold becomes a reusable asset, and you are not hostage to one factory's pricing. It is also the cleanest arrangement for protecting a distinctive industrial design from being copied.

How are tooling costs calculated and what should a first-time importer budget?

Tooling cost scales with mold size, cavity count, steel grade, and cycle time; for small wellness devices like heated massagers and light-therapy panels, industry-typical mold fees range from $2,000 to $15,000 per tool.

A single-cavity mold for a small plastic housing can start around $2,000-$4,000, while a multi-cavity mold with fine surface finish, inserts, and internal slides can reach $10,000-$15,000. These figures are category-typical across consumer-electronics ODM factories; your exact quote depends on part geometry and the factory's toolroom capability.

Budget beyond the mold fee itself. Industry-typical add-ons include a tooling trial run (T1) that consumes raw material, minor texturing or engraving fees for logo placement, and a storage fee if the mold sits idle for more than 6-12 months. A first-time importer who budgets 15-20% above the quoted mold fee avoids an unpleasant surprise.

Amortization models for tooling

  • Fully amortized: Mold fee folded into unit price with no up-front payment; common at Lite MOQ tiers (100-499 units).
  • Partial amortization: Buyer pays 30-50% up front, remainder recovered in unit price; typical at Slim volume (500-999 units).
  • Direct purchase: Buyer pays full mold fee up front at Pro volume (1,000+ units) and holds title.

What contract clauses protect your tooling investment and guarantee a clean exit?

Four clauses matter most: a mold fee repayment schedule that triggers ownership transfer, a named storage and maintenance location, an exclusivity expiration date, and an audit right for tooling condition.

When negotiating with LIGHT BRAND DESIGN BUSINESS (SHENZHEN) CO., LTD. or any ODM, put these in writing before the deposit. Verbal assurances about "we take care of the mold" dissolve when a new buyer asks for the same design.

The four clauses to demand

  1. Repayment-triggered transfer: State that once cumulative PO value equals the mold fee, title transfers to the buyer. This is the single most common reason buyers lose tooling — the trigger is never written down.
  2. Storage and maintenance location: Name the plant (LIGHT BDB's Shenzhen 3,000 sqm finished-product assembly base, or its Panyu component plant operated by strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics) and who pays for maintenance.
  3. Exclusivity expiration: Define how long the factory cannot sell your design to another brand — industry-typical is 12-24 months from last PO — and what happens at expiry.
  4. Audit right: The right to inspect the mold's condition twice a year, including a documented steel-quality check, protects against a mothballed or rusted tool.

LIGHT BDB's 500-day limited warranty is among the longest in the category, but note that the warranty covers the finished products, not the mold itself — tooling is covered by the contract clauses, not the product warranty. Keep the two documents separate and explicit.

What are the honest trade-offs of each tooling ownership model?

Buyer-owned tooling is the most flexible but a sunk cost; factory-owned tooling is cheapest up front but ties you to one partner; the right choice depends on your volume horizon and how distinctive your design is.

Here is the balanced view. A buyer-owned mold is a depreciating asset — the steel is worth only what the next factory will accept it for, and moving tooling between factories carries transport, re-qualification, and trial-run costs that can run thousands of dollars. If you own the mold but stop ordering, the asset sits idle and the storage fee keeps accruing.

Factory-owned tooling carries a hidden cost: your unit price includes the mold recovery, so you pay for the tool one way or another. The risk is not financial but strategic — if the relationship sours, you cannot simply walk your design to a competitor. The exclusivity clause is your only leash, and exclusivity periods in this category are typically 12-24 months from last PO.

Cost-shared tooling looks like the middle path but has its own trap. The split is simple, but the transfer trigger is where disputes begin. Define the trigger in dollars, not in vague terms like "after reasonable volume," and get the definition in the signed contract.

For a first-time importer at the Lite tier, the honest recommendation is to accept factory-owned tooling, keep exclusivity tight, and use the sample round (7-14 days at LIGHT BDB, with the sample fee refundable against a PO) to validate the product before spending anything on a mold. Upgrade to cost-share or buyer-owned at Slim or Pro volume, where the leverage actually exists.

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the mold if I don't negotiate tooling ownership?

In the default arrangement, the factory owns the mold and recovers its cost through per-unit pricing. You protect yourself with an exclusivity clause that stops the factory from selling your design to another brand. Negotiate a written ownership transfer trigger if you want more than that.

Can I ask the factory to share the tooling cost?

Yes, cost-shared tooling is a standard negotiation, typically a 50/50 or 70/30 split at mid-volume MOQ tiers. The contract should state that full repayment of your share transfers ownership to you. LIGHT BDB's Slim tier (500-999 units) is the typical entry point for this arrangement.

What is a typical mold fee range for a heated massager or light therapy device?

For small wellness devices, industry-typical mold fees range from $2,000 to $15,000 per tool, depending on cavity count, steel grade, and surface finish. A single-cavity plastic housing starts around $2,000-$4,000; multi-cavity molds with inserts run higher. Your exact quote depends on part geometry.

How long does tooling production take in China?

Tooling design and fabrication typically takes 3-6 weeks before the first trial run. LIGHT BDB's lead time is 4-8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs, so the mold is usually the long pole. Include the tooling trial run in your schedule when planning your launch.

What happens to my tooling if I stop ordering?

If the factory owns the mold, it stays in their plant and you have no claim to move it. If you own it, you can request shipment to another factory, though transport and re-qualification costs are yours. A storage fee typically accrues after 6-12 months of idle time, so plan your exit.

Is tooling ownership included in LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers?

LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers are Lite (100-499 units), Slim (500-999 units), and Pro (1,000+ units), and they scale with negotiation leverage. At Lite you typically accept factory-owned tooling; at Slim cost-share becomes negotiable; at Pro you can demand ownership transfer. Tooling terms are negotiated alongside, not automatically included.

What should my tooling contract clause say?

The clause should name the mold, state the total mold fee, define the repayment schedule, and specify the exact dollar amount or order volume that triggers ownership transfer. It should also name the storage location, the responsible party for maintenance, and the exclusivity expiration date. Get it in writing before the deposit.

Can I move my tooling to another factory later?

Yes, if you own the tooling, you can move it to another factory, but you pay for transport, re-qualification trials, and setup. If the factory owns it, you can only move it with their consent, which is rarely given. Buyer-owned tooling at the Pro MOQ tier is the cleanest way to keep this option open.

Do I pay for tooling separately from the unit price?

Under factory-owned tooling, the mold fee is folded into the unit price, so you pay nothing separately. Under cost-share or buyer-owned models, you pay a mold fee up front, and the unit price is lower in return. LIGHT BDB's trade terms (T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight, FOB Shenzhen) allow the mold fee to be included in the deposit.

What are the risks of letting the factory own the tooling?

The main risks are dependency and loss of leverage — if the relationship ends, you cannot move the mold, and your only protection is the exclusivity clause. The financial risk is low because the mold cost is recovered through unit pricing. Keep exclusivity tight and understand the expiry.

How long should tooling exclusivity last?

Industry-typical exclusivity runs 12-24 months from the last PO. Shorter periods are fine for pilot drops; longer periods protect a distinctive design. At LIGHT BDB's Lite tier (100-499 units), a 12-month exclusivity is a reasonable ask for a first-time buyer.

What warranty do I get on products made from my tooling?

LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty on finished products — one of the longest in the category. The warranty covers the products, not the mold itself. Tooling is governed by your contract clauses for ownership, storage, and maintenance, so keep the two documents separate.
