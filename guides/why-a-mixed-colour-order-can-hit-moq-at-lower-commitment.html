How does a mixed colour order actually lower your commitment?

A mixed colour order splits one production run across three to five shades, lowering your per-colour commitment to 100-200 units while the total order meets the same MOQ tier.

Factory MOQs are defined per production run, not per SKU. A production run is a single work order for a specific product model. When you order 500 units of one colour, you are committing 500 units of inventory to a single variant. When you order 500 units split across four colours, you commit roughly 125 units per shade. Your cash exposure per variant drops by 75% without moving to a lower tier or paying more per unit.

The arithmetic of a colour split

  • Single colour, 500 units: one SKU, 500 units of inventory, one forecast.
  • Four colours, 500 units total: four SKUs, about 125 units each, four small forecasts.
  • Five colours, 500 units total: five SKUs, 100 units each — a full shade test inside one run.

The same logic works at the Pro tier: a 1,000-unit run split across five colours gives you 200 units per shade at the best unit cost.

What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect for a mixed colour order?

A first-time buyer can expect a 100-499 unit Lite tier for sampling and limited drops, or the 500-999 Slim tier as the most common mixed colour starting point.

Most buyers in the wellness-device category start with one product and a small slate of colours. The Lite tier is best for validating a concept; the Slim tier is the sweet spot where per-unit cost, colour variety, and risk balance well.

Lite tier (100-499 units)

Best for testing and seasonal drops. You can run one colour at 300 units or two colours at 150 each. Per-unit cost is the highest of the three tiers, so reserve Lite for discovery orders rather than your main volume.

Slim tier (500-999 units)

The most popular tier for first orders. At 500-999 units you have room for three to five colours, which gives you real sell-through data on shade preference without a heavy inventory bet. Most brands use Slim to decide which colours to scale up later.

Pro tier (1,000+ units)

The best unit cost and full customization, including your packaging and colourways. Once you know which colour sells, reorder that single colour at 1,000+ to lock the lowest price. This tier is also where you add personalised touches such as logo placement and custom packaging.

Do colour splits add cost or delay?

Colour splits add no cost and no delay at LIGHT BDB — the same 4-8 week lead time from deposit to FOB Shenzhen applies whether you run one colour or five.

Why colour changes don't add cost

Manufacturing a different colour is a material swap, not a new process. The same moulding, SMT, and assembly steps run for every shade. The only extra work is the colour-match check at the end of the line, which is part of LIGHT BDB's ISO 9001 quality system rather than a separate chargeable step.

Sampling before you commit

LIGHT BDB turns samples around in 7-14 days, and the sample fee is refundable against your production order. Request samples in the exact colourways you plan to run so you lock the final coating and finish before the full order. How do the MOQ tiers map across product categories?

The same tiered MOQs — Lite 100-499, Slim 500-999, Pro 1,000+ — apply per production run across heated massagers, red light therapy panels, and light & mood products.

That means the mixed colour logic works horizontally. You do not need to meet a different minimum per category to split colours: the run size is the run size.

Heated massagers

Colour lives in the outer housing and the fabric or PU cover. A four-colour run tells you quickly which finish your audience prefers, and the cover can be swapped without re-tooling the motor and heating core.

Red light therapy panels

Colour applies to the housing frame and the light diffuser tint. Panel size drives the unit price more than colour does, so a mixed colour run at the Slim tier is an affordable way to test two or three frame finishes.

Light & mood products

With lamps and mood lights, colour has two layers: the physical fixture and the light output. A mixed colour order lets you test both the housing palette and the light settings users choose at home.

All three categories share the same certification backbone — ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI, UL registered factory, Sony Green Partner — and the same 500-day limited warranty, regardless of colour count.

What should a brand owner verify before placing a mixed colour order?

Verify colour consistency, coating durability, packaging SKUs, and per-colour sell-through forecasts before you commit a mixed colour run.

Colour consistency

Ask the factory how it controls colour variance across batches. ISO 9001 covers the quality management system, but you should confirm the specific colour-matching procedure and ask for a reference panel from the sample stage.

Packaging and SKU management

Each colour needs its own barcode and carton marking. One production run with five colours means five SKUs in your ERP, five listings on your storefront, and a packing slip that matches. Confirm the labelling and packaging plan before the deposit.

Per-colour forecasting

Industry-typical sell-through follows a power curve: one or two shades will out-sell the rest. Plan the mixed colour run as a discovery step, and be ready to reorder the winning colour alone at the Pro tier afterwards.

What are the honest trade-offs of a mixed colour order?

The honest trade-off is forecast uncertainty: a mixed colour order spreads your SKU risk but leaves slow-moving shades holding cash in your warehouse.

Buyers who benefit most from colour splits are those testing a new market or a new product line. Buyers who should stay single-colour are those with proven demand and a tight cash cycle.

  • More SKUs to manage: each shade adds ERP entries, listings, and return codes.
  • Slow movers: the weakest colour may sell at 30-40% of the strongest, and its inventory ties up cash until moved.
  • Reorder complexity: the winning colour will run out first; you may need two reorders at different tiers for the same product.
  • Logistics: cartons of different shades make picking slower in your warehouse.

Mitigation: cap the slowest shade near the Lite minimum, use the colour split for discovery, and reorder the leader before it hits zero. LIGHT BDB's 500-day warranty applies to every shade, so quality risk is not colour-dependent.
