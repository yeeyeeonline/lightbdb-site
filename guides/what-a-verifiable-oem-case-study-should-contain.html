What a Verifiable OEM Case Study Should Contain: Nine Parts a Buyer Can Actually Check

Quick answer

A verifiable OEM case study contains nine linked parts — buyer brief, dated timeline, specification, BOM and tooling record, certificate scope, production data, QC evidence, cost breakdown, and a reference — and at least three of them must be checkable against an outside document. Anything shorter is a brochure with better typography.

  • Ask for the certificate scope page, not the cover; ISO 9001 names a site, not a brand.
  • A case study without month-level dates is marketing; timeline dates are the cheapest lie detector buyers have.
  • MOQ is tiered: LIGHT BDB runs Light & Mood from 100 pcs and body care from 500 pcs.
  • Lead time for most SKUs is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen, with the end date stated.
  • Sample turnaround is 7–14 days and the fee is refundable against the PO.
  • Warranty belongs in the document: LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty on finished products.
  • UL registered factory status is not a product listing; ask which one the case study claims.
  • Redaction is normal, but a study with no callable reference and no labeled redactions is a warning sign.

What should a verifiable OEM case study contain?

A verifiable OEM case study should contain nine parts: buyer brief, dated timeline, product specification, BOM and tooling record, certificate scope, production data, QC evidence, cost breakdown, and a reachable reference.

Most case studies sent to first-time importers are four paragraphs and a hero photo. They read well and prove nothing. The nine-part structure below survives a diligence call because each part maps to a document someone outside the factory can request.

  1. Buyer brief. The problem as the brand stated it — target retail price, target user, channel, destination market. Without the brief you cannot judge whether the result was hard or routine.
  2. Dated timeline. Month-level dates for brief, sample, approval, tooling, pilot run, mass production, and shipment. Dates turn a story into a record.
  3. Specification. Dimensions, materials, power input or battery, heat output or LED wavelength where relevant, packaging format, and the standard the product was built to.
  4. BOM and tooling record. Critical components by supplier type, tool count, who paid, who holds title, and the amortization rule.
  5. Certificate scope. Not the logo wall — the certificate number, issuing body, exact site address covered, and validity window.
  6. Production data. MOQ tier ordered, units per run, line rate, first-pass yield, and which site performed each step.
  7. QC evidence. Inspection gates, AQL levels used, sample sizes, and report dates. Pre-shipment inspection is table stakes; in-process checks are the tell.
  8. Cost breakdown. Unit cost structure at the agreed Incoterm, tooling line, packaging line, and the variables that would move the price.
  9. Reference. A named contact, or a labeled reason there isn't one — plus documented permission to call.

The three parts buyers skip first — and why they matter

Buyers usually skip 2, 4 and 9. The dated timeline is the cheapest lie detector in the document: a factory that cannot reconstruct dates from its own production records cannot reconstruct your launch either. Tooling ownership decides whether you can move your product to a second source in year three. And a reference you are allowed to call is the only section that answers questions the writer did not prepare for.

LIGHT BDB builds its own case studies on this structure, which means you can hold the same checklist against us. Where we cannot disclose something — a client name, a target retail price, a component supplier — the redaction is labeled rather than silent.

How do you verify a factory and certification claim in an OEM case study?

You verify it by matching the certificate's site address to the site named in the case study, then requesting the audit date and the scope page.

A certificate cover page shows a logo. The scope page shows a legal entity and an address, and that is the whole argument. If a case study says a product was built at a 10,000 sqm component plant, the certificate should name that plant — or an affiliated entity you can trace in one step.

What to request, line by line

  • ISO 9001 and ISO 14001: certificate number, issuing body, site address, validity window, and the last surveillance audit date.
  • BSCI (amfori): the audit report status and rating, plus the audit date. Membership is not an audit result — ask which one the study claims.
  • UL registered factory: registration of a site is not a product listing. Ask which is being claimed and get the file reference.
  • Sony Green Partner: a supply-chain environmental qualification. It demonstrates component-level process control, not a product safety mark.
  • Site reality: square metres, headcount, and number of lines, backed by a dated photo set or a live video walkthrough of that specific area.

What production, MOQ, and lead-time data should a case study show?

It should show the MOQ tier the buyer actually ordered at, the deposit date, the FOB date, the units shipped, and the site where each step ran.

MOQ is not one number. It moves with how much of the product is yours versus ours. A case study quoting a single MOQ figure without naming the tier is hiding the variable that decides your first order.

How LIGHT BDB publishes MOQ tiers

  • Light & Mood platforms: 100 pcs standard, 300 pcs white-label, 500–1,000 pcs ODM custom.
  • Body care platforms: 500 pcs standard, 1,000 pcs white-label, 2,000–3,000 pcs ODM custom.

Read what the tiers imply. A first-time importer testing a heated massager in one market can start at 500 pcs on a standard body-care platform. The same buyer wanting a custom housing, custom firmware, and custom packaging moves into the 2,000–3,000 pcs band, because tooling and setup have to be absorbed somewhere. A credible case study states which band the buyer was in — project difficulty is a function of that number.

Lead time and sample timing

Lead time is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs. The range is wide because it bundles tooling, component lead times, and line scheduling, so a case study should say which end of the range the project hit and why. Sample turnaround is 7–14 days, and the sample fee is refundable against the PO. If a study claims a two-week mass production run on a custom-tooled product, ask for the tooling dates; industry-typical injection mold lead times alone run several weeks.

Ask for throughput evidence too. LIGHT BDB's Yangon overflow line handles more than 5,000 pcs/day — the number that matters when a case study describes a demand spike rather than steady-state ordering. Design work runs out of Hangzhou and Hamburg, which is worth confirming if your brief involves industrial design changes rather than color and packaging alone.

How should a case study document tooling, cost, payment terms, and redaction?

It should state who owns the tooling, what it cost, how it amortizes, the exact payment schedule, the Incoterm, and which fields are redacted and why.

Tooling and IP ownership

  • Tool count and type — housing molds, fixtures, test jigs.
  • Who paid, and whether cost was amortized into unit price or billed separately.
  • Who holds title, and the conditions for transferring the tool to another supplier.
  • Which files — CAD, firmware, packaging artwork — sit with the brand versus the factory.

This paragraph determines your exit cost. A factory that owns your mold has a permanent negotiating position. A case study saying "tooling included" without saying who holds title has not answered the question.

Cost structure and payment

A useful breakdown gives the unit price at the agreed Incoterm and separates material, labor, packaging, tooling amortization, and test. LIGHT BDB quotes FOB Shenzhen and works with your nominated forwarder, so freight stays yours and visible rather than folded into a landed number you cannot audit. Payment terms are T/T 30/70 with L/C at sight accepted. If a study mentions "flexible payment" and nothing else, ask for the actual schedule before you model cash flow.

Redaction rules that keep a study honest

  • Hide client names, retail prices, and supplier identities where an NDA requires it.
  • Keep dates, quantities, MOQ tier, site, and outcome visible.
  • Label every redaction — "unit cost withheld under NDA" is credible; a document with no numbers is not.
  • Offer a live reference call under NDA instead of a written client name.

What quality-control and warranty evidence belongs in an OEM case study?

A case study should show three inspection gates with named AQL levels and dated reports, plus a plain statement of warranty length and coverage.

The three gates

  1. Incoming components. Especially on heated products, where the heating element, thermistor, and controller decide field failure rate.
  2. In-process assembly. Functional test at the point of assembly, not only at the end of the line.
  3. Pre-shipment. AQL sampling with sample size and accept/reject numbers written down.

Industry-typical pre-shipment AQL for consumer electronics sits around 2.5 major and 4.0 minor, tightened for safety-critical functions. A case study should publish its own numbers rather than say "strict QC".

Reliability and packaging testing

Ask which tests ran and on how many units: drop test, charge/discharge cycling, over-temperature cut-off verification, packaging compression for retail cartons. For a heated massager or a red light panel, a case study that never mentions thermal cut-off testing has skipped the part buyers actually care about.

Warranty terms in writing

LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty on finished products, one of the longer terms in this category. The case study should repeat the real conditions: what is covered, what is excluded, who pays outbound replacement freight, and claim turnaround. Warranty length is easy to advertise and expensive to honor, which makes it a useful proxy for how much confidence a factory has in its own process.

What are the honest limits of any OEM case study?

A case study cannot prove your product will sell; it can only prove the factory has done comparable work under comparable constraints.

  • Survivorship bias. Nobody publishes the project that missed its window. Ask how many projects in the last two years ran past the quoted lead time, and expect a number rather than a shrug.
  • Category transfer. A strong case study in light and mood products does not automatically transfer to body care. Different tooling, different thermal considerations, different MOQ bands.
  • Redaction cuts both ways. An NDA that hides the client also hides context — the market, the price point, sometimes the volume. Accept it and lean harder on the reference call.
  • Snapshot, not a guarantee. A study documents one run at one moment. Staffing, component sourcing, and plant allocation change. Certificate scope and audit dates tell you whether the snapshot is current.
  • Capacity is finite. A factory running 5,000+ pcs/day on an overflow line still has a queue. A study showing an aggressive timeline should also show what it displaced.

None of this argues against case studies. It argues for reading them as evidence rather than reassurance: nine parts, at least three checkable outside the document, and a reference you are allowed to call.

What do buyers ask most about verifiable OEM case studies?

What is an OEM case study?

It is a documented record of one private-label project: the brand's brief, the specification built, the timeline, the production data, the quality checks, and the outcome. It is evidence of process, not a product catalog page.

How do I verify that an OEM case study is real?

Request three things that exist outside the document: a certificate scope page, a bill of lading or invoice matching the stated dates, and a reference call. If a supplier cannot produce any of the three, treat the study as marketing.

What should a case study say about MOQ?

It should name the tier the buyer ordered at, not a single vague figure. LIGHT BDB publishes tiers by platform: Light & Mood from 100 pcs standard, 300 pcs white-label, 500–1,000 pcs ODM custom; body care from 500 pcs standard, 1,000 pcs white-label, 2,000–3,000 pcs ODM custom.

What lead time should a case study state?

It should state the deposit date and the FOB date with an Incoterm. LIGHT BDB quotes 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs, with sample turnaround of 7–14 days and the sample fee refundable against the PO.

Do I need an NDA to see a factory's case study?

Often yes, because the study contains a client name or pricing. A good supplier offers a redacted version immediately and a deeper version, or a live reference call, once a mutual NDA is signed.

Which certifications should I look for in an OEM wellness-device case study?

Look for quality and environmental management plus social audit evidence with site addresses attached. LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory status, and Sony Green Partner.

How long should a sample take before I place a production order?

Plan for roughly one to two weeks on a platform product, longer if tooling changes are involved. LIGHT BDB quotes 7–14 day sample turnaround, and the sample fee is credited back against the purchase order.

What warranty should a wellness-device OEM offer?

Compare days, exclusions, and who pays return freight rather than the headline number. LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty on finished products, and the case study should restate the actual claim process.

Can a case study prove a factory can handle my volume?

Only if it shows line rate and unit counts. LIGHT BDB's Yangon overflow line runs above 5,000 pcs/day, but a study should also disclose queue position and what capacity your order is competing with.

What payment terms should a case study disclose?

It should state the exact schedule rather than "flexible terms". LIGHT BDB works on T/T 30/70 with L/C at sight accepted, FOB Shenzhen, using your nominated forwarder.

What is a red flag in an OEM case study?

No dates, no quantities, no certificate scope, and no callable reference. Also watch for unlabeled redactions and claims that mix site registration with product certification.

How many case studies should I request before choosing a supplier?

Ask for two or three in your own category, plus one from an adjacent category to test range. Two or three lets you compare how consistently timelines, MOQ tiers, and QC data are reported.
