How a Design Patent Becomes a Checkable Sourcing Signal

What does a design patent actually protect on a sourcing checklist?

A design patent protects how a product looks — its shape, surface pattern and ornamentation — and nothing about how it works.

That limitation is the whole point. In most jurisdictions a design right has no claims paragraph at all: the scope is set by the drawings or photographs filed with the application. Two products can perform identically and sit on opposite sides of a design right; equally, a competitor can keep the engineering and change the silhouette and stay clear.

So on your sourcing checklist, a design patent belongs in the ownership column, not the performance column. It is a dated, public record that a specific appearance was examined and registered to a named party. That is a different kind of evidence from "we designed this ourselves" or "this is our own mold," both of which are unverifiable claims a salesperson can make about any product in the catalogue.

The reason it is genuinely checkable is structural. A registration number is issued by a government office, indexed in a public database, and updated when the right lapses, transfers or expires. You do not need the supplier's permission to look it up, and the supplier cannot edit what you find.

What a design patent does not tell you

  • Whether the product is safe or performs. A design right is not a safety mark and is not issued after any product testing.
  • Whether the factory can hold tolerance. Registration says nothing about tooling, QC or process capability.
  • Whether the design is valid. Registration is not a guarantee of novelty; validity is only tested when someone challenges it.
  • Whether you have freedom to sell. A third party may hold a broader right that covers your product in your market.
  • Anything outside its territory. A Chinese registration does nothing for sales in the US, EU or Japan.

Which design-patent documents should a brand owner ask a factory to produce?

Ask for four items: the granted certificate, the registration number with its jurisdiction, the drawings as filed, and a written statement of who owns the right.

Most first-time importers ask one question — "do you have a patent?" — and accept one word in reply. That exchange produces nothing you can verify. Replace it with a fixed document request and the conversation changes within a day.

The four-document request list

  1. The granted certificate, as a PDF. It should carry the registration number, the owner's full legal name and the grant date. A filing receipt is not a certificate.
  2. The jurisdiction, stated in writing. "We have a patent" is not a location. You need the office and the register — a US design patent number, an EU registered Community design number and a Chinese ZL number are three different rights, verified in three different places.
  3. The drawings or photographs exactly as filed. Not marketing renders, not a CAD screenshot from this week. The filed drawings are the legal boundary of the right.
  4. A signed ownership statement. Who filed it, whether it has been assigned, licensed or shared, and whether any other client buys the same shell.

Red flags in the paperwork

  • Only a filing receipt is available, with no granted certificate.
  • The applicant name differs from the company issuing your proforma invoice, and no assignment document bridges the gap.
  • The number returns a completely different product when you look it up.
  • The drawings match a well-known brand's product rather than the factory's own catalogue.
  • Someone claims the patent "covers the whole category" — design rights are narrow by construction.
  • Numbers that do not fit the issuing office's format, which usually means a transcription error or an invention.

How do you verify a design patent number in about ten minutes?

Look the number up in the issuing jurisdiction's public register, then read four fields: applicant, filing date, legal status and drawings.

That is the entire method. Everything else is navigation. Budget 10–20 minutes per number as an industry-typical figure; register interfaces change often and a first lookup is always the slowest.

The verification sequence

  1. Match the number format to an office. US design patents are quoted with a leading D; EU registered Community designs use a long numeric identifier, often shown with a three-digit suffix; Chinese design patents are usually quoted as ZL numbers.
  2. Open the right register. USPTO Patent Public Search for US design patents, EUIPO's design search tools for EU registrations, CNIPA for China, J-PlatPat for Japan, and WIPO's Global Design Database for Hague-system filings that cover multiple territories at once.
  3. Read the legal status. Granted, pending, lapsed and expired are four very different answers, and the register states which one applies.
  4. Read the owner field. This is the field most buyers skip. It tells you whether the right belongs to the factory you are talking to, an affiliate, a trading company, or your competitor.
  5. Open the drawings. Compare them with what you intend to buy, not with what the brochure shows.
  6. Check term and renewal dates. A design that expires inside your product's planned life cycle is a weak signal for a multi-year programme.

How do you check the patent actually covers the product you are buying?

Put the filed drawings next to your 3D file or golden sample and compare silhouette, parting lines, button placement and surface pattern line by line.

This is where most verification stops too early. A real number that covers a different product is worse than no number, because it creates false confidence. Design rights are defined visually, so the comparison is visual too — and small differences genuinely matter, in both directions.

Three questions that settle scope

  • Does the drawing match what you would be shipping? If the register shows a rounded shell and your quote is for a squared one, the patent does not cover your SKU.
  • Is the named owner the entity signing your purchase order? If not, ask for the assignment or licence that connects them. Otherwise the right sits with someone who has no obligations to you.
  • Is the registration in a territory where you actually sell? A design registered only in China gives you no leverage in the US, the EU or Japan — the three markets where most private-label wellness brands earn their revenue.

One more distinction worth making early: a factory that holds a design registration on its own generic platform shell has not given you exclusivity. Ask directly whether other clients buy the same housing. The honest answer is usually yes, and it is better to hear it in week one than to discover it at a trade show.

What does a design patent tell you about MOQ, tooling and lead time?

It tells you the design is tooled and repeatable, so expect a defined MOQ tier rather than a one-off build.

A registered design is a design someone expected to sell more than once. That expectation shows up in the commercial terms, which is why the patent question and the MOQ question are really the same question asked twice.

Reading the filing date as a tooling date

The filing date on the certificate is a rough proxy for where the project sits. A design filed two or three years ago that is still in the catalogue almost certainly has working tooling, fixtures and test jigs behind it — industry-typical behaviour in this category. A design filed three months ago may still be in tooling, so treat any quoted lead time as provisional and ask what has actually been moulded.

Where does a design patent sit next to ISO 9001, UL and BSCI?

It sits in a different category: a design patent is evidence of ownership, while ISO 9001, UL and BSCI are evidence of process and compliance.

Buyers often bundle all of these into one vague idea of "certified," then feel reassured without being informed. Each document answers a specific question and is verifiable in a specific place. The table below is the version worth keeping next to your sourcing file.

EU registered Community design number A design right valid across EU member states, renewable in 5-year terms EUIPO design registers That the design is novel worldwide, or that the factory is the owner
Chinese design patent (ZL number) A Chinese design right with a 15-year term CNIPA patent search Any protection for your sales in the US, EU or Japan

What are the honest limits of a design patent as a sourcing signal?

A design patent proves ownership of an appearance — not manufacturing quality, safety, or your freedom to sell the product.

It is a genuinely useful signal, and it is a narrow one. Buyers who over-weight it tend to make two mistakes: treating a registration as a capability certificate, and treating it as a moat. Both are wrong.

The trade-offs worth naming

  • Territorial by nature. A registration only bites in the jurisdictions where it is filed. If your factory's design patent is Chinese and your market is Germany, you are holding a document about a market you do not sell in.
  • Design-arounds are cheap. Moving a seam, changing a radius or splitting a housing can clear a design right entirely. Design protection raises the cost of copying slightly; it does not stop it.
  • Registration is not validity. Examined offices do refuse applications, but a granted design can still be invalidated later. Treat the certificate as a starting position, not a verdict.
  • Filing is inexpensive, enforcement is not. Because registering a design is cheap, the existence of a registration proves very little about the owner's seriousness. What it cost them to obtain is not a quality signal.
  • It cuts both ways. A factory showing you a design patent is not automatically reassuring. Ask whose design it was, and whether the drawings were made for a client who paid for them.
  • Verification takes time you may not have. Ten to twenty minutes per number is realistic for a first lookup, and a portfolio of five SKUs across three jurisdictions becomes an afternoon.

None of this makes the check optional. It makes it proportionate: verify the number, verify the owner, verify the drawings, then move on and spend your remaining diligence budget on tooling, QC and the factory's actual production record.

What do brand owners ask most about design patents and sourcing?

These are the questions that come up in nearly every first sourcing call.

How do I check if a design patent is real?

Take the number and the jurisdiction, open that office's public register, and read the applicant, filing date, legal status and drawings. The supplier's permission is not required and the record cannot be edited by them. If the number returns nothing, ask for the certificate again — a real registration always resolves.

Does a design patent prove a factory actually manufactures the product?

No. It proves someone registered an appearance. Manufacturing evidence is different: tooling photos, an audit, a sample you can hold, and a factory listing you can look up. Use the design patent for ownership, and the ISO 9001 certificate plus a sample for capability.

Is a Chinese design patent enforceable in the US or EU?

No. Design rights are territorial, so a Chinese registration gives you no leverage in the US, the EU or Japan. If your market is outside China, ask specifically which registrations the supplier holds in your selling territory.

What is the difference between a pending design application and a granted design patent?

A pending application is not an enforceable right; it is an intention. A granted registration is a right you can point to, with a date and a scope set by the filed drawings. Always ask for the granted certificate.

How long does a design patent last?

Terms vary by jurisdiction. US design patents run 15 years from grant for applications filed under current rules, EU registered Community designs run 5 years and are renewable up to 25, and Chinese design patents run 15 years. Check the specific registration rather than assuming.

What MOQ should I expect for a patented ODM design?

Expect a defined tier rather than a one-off build. At LIGHT BDB, Light & Mood platforms start at 100 pcs standard, 300 pcs white-label and 500–1,000 pcs ODM custom; body care platforms start at 500 pcs, 1,000 pcs and 2,000–3,000 pcs respectively.

Can a factory file a patent on my design and sell it to competitors?

It can happen if ownership was never written down. Whoever files first is presumed to hold the right in many systems, so the development agreement needs to state that designs created for you are assigned to you, signed before tooling begins.

Does a design patent replace a freedom-to-operate search?

No, and it is the opposite of one. A design patent shows what someone else has claimed, not what is clear for you to sell. Freedom-to-operate work looks for rights that might block your product, including rights held by parties you have never spoken to.

Which certifications should I verify alongside a design patent?

Verify ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 through the issuing certification body's client directory, BSCI through amfori's audit platform, and UL factory status through UL's public directories. LIGHT BDB holds all of these plus Sony Green Partner.

Samples turn in 7–14 days, and the sample fee is refundable against the purchase order. Production lead time is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs, under a 500-day limited warranty, with T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight accepted.

What should I do if a supplier refuses to share the patent number?

Treat the refusal as the answer. A registration number is a public record, so there is no confidentiality reason to withhold it. If nothing arrives within a reasonable follow-up, price the product as though no design right exists and plan accordingly.
