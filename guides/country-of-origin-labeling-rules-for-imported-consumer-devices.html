# Country of Origin Labeling Rules for Imported Consumer Devices When you private-label a heated massager, red light therapy panel, or light-and-mood device from an ODM such as LIGHT BDB, the country of origin determines what goes on the label, what appears on customs paperwork, and how smoothly your shipment clears port. The rule itself is simple; the practical execution is where first-time importers slip. This guide walks through the US, EU, and Japanese rules, how to apply them to an ODM-built product, and exactly what to ask your factory before you pay the deposit. ## What exactly does "country of origin" mean for a consumer device I import? Country of origin is where your product underwent its last substantial transformation, which for assembled devices is almost always the final assembly line. Substantial transformation means the product gained its essential character at that location. A massage gun that goes from loose PCB, motor, shell, and battery into a finished working unit on a Shenzhen line has its substantial transformation in Shenzhen — regardless of where the brand, design, or R&D originated. This distinction matters because every label, invoice, certificate of origin, and customs declaration must name the same place. For LIGHT BDB's typical build, the chain runs from a component plant in Panyu (auto-grade inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils, SMT work) to the 3,000 sqm finished-product assembly plant in Shenzhen. Since final assembly is the substantial transformation, the standard country of origin for those SKUs is China. ## How do I determine the correct country of origin for my private-label massager or wellness device? Ask your ODM in writing where final assembly occurs and which assembly steps happen there — that location becomes your country of origin. You do not invent the origin; you verify it. The same substantial-transformation principle applies across the United States, the European Union, and Japan, so one answer works for all three markets. ### What a first-time buyer should verify before signing a PO - Confirm the final assembly plant address in your ODM contract, not just the factory's headquarters. - Ask whether any significant production step happens in a second country (for example, an overflow line abroad). - Request the intended certificate of origin template with your purchase order. - Have the ODM state, in writing, that no post-assembly step in another country changes the product's character. A relevant case: LIGHT BDB runs a Yangon overflow line capable of 5,000+ pieces per day. If your order is built there, origin becomes Myanmar, not China, and your labels and customs documents change accordingly. Confirm the build line before you approve marking. ## What are the US Customs rules for country of origin marking? US 19 CFR Part 134 requires a legible, permanent "Made in China" mark in English on every imported consumer article. The marking must be conspicuous, positioned so a retail buyer sees it, and durable enough to survive handling. For a wellness device, that usually means the product base or back panel, or a permanently attached label that cannot be easily peeled off. Practical US requirements: - English only is sufficient; bilingual marks are allowed but not required. - The mark must be permanent — laser etching, in-mold marking, or a label that resists removal. - If the device is sold in a blister card or box, the importer's name and address must also appear on the outer packaging; the origin mark stays on the article itself. - Goods that reach the retail buyer without the mark face CBP refusal, re-export, or fines. LIGHT BDB can implement the origin mark at different permanence levels. On the Pro tier (1,000+ units), you can request in-mold or laser-etched marks that become part of the shell. At lower tiers, durable adhesive labels or hang tags are the practical route. The factory also maintains UL registered factory status, which supports the electrical safety compliance US retailers expect alongside proper origin labeling. ## What do the EU and Japan require for country of origin labeling? The EU requires origin marking only where omission could mislead consumers; Japan's Customs Tariff Law applies the same "misleading" test. Neither market forces a blanket "Made in China" stamp on every product, but both penalize packaging or marking that suggests a false origin. ### What the EU asks for Regulation (EU) No 952/2013, the Union Customs Code, does not demand origin marking on most consumer goods. If your packaging, brand name, or design implies European manufacture when the product is assembled in China, you risk market withdrawal for misleading the consumer. The practical move is low-cost insurance: mark "Made in China" on the product or packaging anyway. It answers customs questions instantly and protects you from national enforcement. ### What Japan asks for Japan's Customs Tariff Law requires origin marking when its absence could mislead buyers about the true origin of an import. For electronics and wellness devices, the misleading test is applied case by case. Many importers add the Japanese phrase 中国製 alongside "Made in China" to remove any ambiguity at customs and on shop shelves. Your MOQ tier decides which labeling methods you can afford, but origin marking itself is mandatory at every volume. Even a 100-unit limited drop must carry the correct origin on each product. What changes with volume is the permanence and cost of the mark. LIGHT BDB's MOQ structure is designed around exactly this trade-off: - Lite: 100–499 units — best for sampling and limited drops; durable adhesive labels or hang tags are the sensible marking route. - Slim: 500–999 units — the most popular tier for first orders, balancing cost with enough volume for tooling and label flexibility. - Pro: 1,000+ units — the best unit cost and full customization, including in-mold or laser-etched origin marks built into the shell design. The practical timeline for origin-labeled product is straightforward. Most SKUs ship 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen. Samples turn around in 7 days, and the sample fee is refunded against your production order. Trade terms are T/T 30/70, and L/C at sight is accepted. You ship FOB Shenzhen and can use your nominated forwarder — so the origin declaration on the bill of lading stays in your control. ## What practical steps should a first-time importer take to stay compliant? Confirm origin in writing, put marking in your product spec, and make sure your customs documents match the label. Compliance is a paperwork discipline, not a mystery. Follow these steps and your first container will clear far more smoothly. - Get a written statement from LIGHT BDB naming the assembly location (Shenzhen or Yangon) and the line that will build your SKU. - Specify label placement, font, and permanence method in your spec sheet before tooling starts. - Review the origin mark on the 7-day sample and approve it in writing. - Keep the commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin consistent with the label on the product. - Pass the correct origin declaration to your nominated forwarder for the bill of lading and destination-market entry. - If you sell on Amazon or marketplaces, keep the origin data in the product listing consistent with the label. A small but useful habit: keep copies of the origin statement from your ODM with every order. If a customs broker questions the mark, you can produce the factory documentation within minutes. ## What honest trade-offs and risks should you accept? Origin marking is low-cost, but it locks you to your ODM's assembly location — moving assembly later means relabeling every unit. That is the central trade-off. If you shift production to a second country for cost reasons, or use an overflow line such as LIGHT BDB's Yangon operation, your origin changes and your labels, certificates, and listings must change with it. Transshipment through a third country does not change origin — origin follows assembly, not the shipping route. A few honest boundaries to keep in view: - LIGHT BDB is a wellness-device ODM, not a medical manufacturer; no product is approved for medical use, and label claims must stay within relaxation, relief, and comfort. - The company holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), and UL registered factory status — strong quality and social-compliance signals, but none of these turn a wellness device into a regulated medical product. - Permanent origin marks like in-mold or laser etching require Pro tier volume (1,000+ units); below that, adhesive labels are the honest, low-cost choice. - Mislabeling risk is real: incorrect origin marking in the US can trigger CBP refusal or fines, while misleading origin in the EU or Japan can cause import refusal or market withdrawal. Buyers who treat origin labeling as a spec item — confirmed at sampling, documented at PO, matched at customs — rarely get into trouble. Those who skip the confirmation step tend to learn the rule the expensive way.
