How to Check a Supplier Claim Against a Public Record

Quick answer

Most supplier claims can be checked in under 60 minutes using four free sources: certificate registries, national company registries, customs and bill-of-lading data, and audit platforms. Start with the certificate number — any claim that cannot be traced to a registry entry within 30 minutes stays unverified until the supplier produces the document.

  • A supplier's ISO 9001 or ISO 14001 certificate can be verified in under five minutes on the issuing certification body's own public register.
  • Chinese company identity is public: the legal name on gsxt.gov.cn returns registered capital, business scope and any abnormal-operations listing.
  • LIGHT BDB holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory and Sony Green Partner status, each checkable through its issuing registry.
  • Certificates prove a management system exists, not a defect rate — pair every registry check with a paid sample order.
  • LIGHT BDB MOQ runs from 100 pcs on Light & Mood standard platforms to 3,000 pcs on Body care ODM custom programmes.
  • LIGHT BDB samples ship in 7–14 days and the sample fee is refundable against the purchase order.
  • Lead time from deposit to FOB Shenzhen is 4–8 weeks on most LIGHT BDB SKUs, backed by a 500-day limited warranty.
  • No certificate number, no registry entry and no reference call means you are reading marketing copy, not evidence.

Which supplier claims can actually be checked in public records?

Four claim types are genuinely public: management-system certificates, legal company identity, export history and social-audit status. Everything else is document-verified, sample-tested or written into a contract.

Sort every claim you receive into three buckets before you spend time on it. The bucket tells you what a real verification looks like, and how long it should take.

Bucket 1 — registry-verifiable in minutes

  • ISO 9001 / ISO 14001 certificates — checkable on the issuing certification body's register by certificate number.
  • Legal entity name, credit code and business scope — published on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn).
  • UL registered factory sites — searchable through UL Solutions' public directories.
  • Trademarks and patents — CNIPA, USPTO, EUIPO and Google Patents all publish owner names and status.
  • Test report numbers — most large labs (SGS, Intertek, TÜV, BV) run online report authentication portals.

Bucket 2 — real documents, shared not published

BSCI and Sedex audit reports, brand approvals such as Sony Green Partner, customer references and certificate scope annexes fall here. They exist and they are legitimate, but the supplier shares them rather than publishing them. Your check has two parts: does the document authenticate, and will the supplier let you confirm it with the issuing body, the auditor or the brand?

Bucket 3 — not public, never will be

MOQ, lead time, warranty terms, defect rate, monthly capacity and named clients. None of these live in a registry. They become real only when they appear in a quotation, a proforma invoice, a purchase order and a sample you can hold.

The order to check in

  1. Legal identity and business scope — 10 minutes.
  2. Every certificate named on the quotation — 15 minutes.
  3. Audit status, if the supplier will share the report — same day.
  4. Export history and shipping records — 20 minutes.
  5. Sample order against the written spec — 7–14 days.
  6. Reference call with a non-competing brand — 20 minutes.

How do I verify an ISO 9001 certificate in under five minutes?

Take the certificate number, the issuing body and the expiry date, then search that body's own register directly — never the PDF the supplier emailed you.

The certificate PDF is the starting point, not the proof. Everything you need to verify is printed on it, and everything you need to compare against is on the certification body's website.

Step by step

  1. Ask for the full certificate as a PDF, not a JPEG, not a thumbnail, not a logo pasted onto a catalogue page.
  2. Read four fields: certificate number, issuing certification body, scope statement (the sentence describing what is certified), and the site address.
  3. Type the certification body's web address yourself rather than clicking a link the supplier sent. Then search the certificate number in their register.
  4. Match the legal entity name and the site address character by character. "Shenzhen ABC Technology Co., Ltd." and "Shenzhen ABC Trading Co., Ltd." are different companies.
  5. Check the status line — valid, suspended or withdrawn — and the expiry date.
  6. Re-check on the day you place the purchase order. Certificates can be suspended between your first enquiry and your deposit.

What a weak or fabricated certificate looks like

  • A scanned image with no certificate number, or a number that returns nothing on the issuing body's register.
  • An "issuing body" with a website registered a few months ago and no accreditation mark.
  • A scope statement that covers "sales and distribution" when you are buying manufacturing.
  • A site address that does not match the factory address given to you elsewhere.
  • A certificate that expired 18 months ago and was never renewed.

How do I confirm a supplier is a real factory rather than a trading company?

Cross-check three records: the business licence name, the manufacturing scope in the company registry, and the site address printed on every certificate.

Trading companies are not automatically a problem — plenty are competent and honest. The problem is a trading company presenting itself as a factory, because the price, the lead time and the quality control you are paying for do not exist in the way you were told.

China company registry basics

  • Legal name — must match the name on the business licence, the bank account and the certificates. Trade names and website brands frequently do not.
  • Unified social credit code — the 18-character identifier. Ask for it early; it makes every other check faster and it is hard to fake convincingly.
  • Business scope — look for manufacturing, production, processing or assembly language. A pure "sales of electronic products" scope is a trading profile.
  • Registered capital and establishment date — useful context, weak signals. A large registered capital figure proves nothing about capability.
  • Abnormal operations listing — companies that failed to file annual reports appear here publicly. Check it.

Site evidence that is harder to fake

  • A live video walkthrough with location sharing on, not a pre-recorded factory tour.
  • Certificates whose site address matches the plant you are being shown.
  • A customs registration code that ties to actual shipments, not just a claim of export experience.
  • An audit report covering the specific site that will run your order.

How do I check a social compliance claim like BSCI or Sedex?

BSCI and Sedex reports are shared by the supplier through the audit platform, so ask for the report ID and audit date rather than a logo on a catalogue page.

Social audits are the claims most often shown and least often checked. The logo means nothing on its own; the report does.

What to request

  • The full audit report, not the summary page or the certificate of completion.
  • The audited entity name and site address — the audit must cover the plant that will make your product.
  • The audit date. amfori BSCI audits are industry-typically valid for around 12 months, so anything older than that needs a follow-up audit date.
  • The grade and any zero-tolerance findings, plus the corrective action plan and its status.

What a social audit does and does not tell you

A BSCI or Sedex audit reports on working conditions, hours, wages, safety and management systems at a point in time. It says nothing about your product's defect rate, the factory's engineering depth or whether they can hold a tolerance on a heating element. Buyers who read a grade as a quality score end up disappointed. Read it as a risk signal about the site, then verify product quality through samples and inspection.

How do I test MOQ, lead time and warranty claims that no registry lists?

Commercial terms are not public records — the only real check is whether the supplier will put them in writing on the quotation, the proforma invoice and the purchase order.

A verbal MOQ is an opening position. A written MOQ on a proforma invoice is a commitment. The distance between the two is where first-time importers lose money.

MOQ

Ask for the tier structure rather than a single number, because a supplier quoting one MOQ is usually quoting their easiest tier. LIGHT BDB tiers MOQ by platform: Light & Mood platforms start at 100 pcs standard, 300 pcs white-label and 500–1,000 pcs for ODM custom; Body care platforms start at 500 pcs standard, 1,000 pcs white-label and 2,000–3,000 pcs for ODM custom. If a supplier cannot explain why the number changes between tiers, they have not thought about it.

Lead time

Always ask "from what event to what event". LIGHT BDB quotes 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs. A supplier quoting "25 days" without naming the start and end points has told you nothing — 25 days from order is a different promise from 25 days from artwork approval.

Warranty

Get the warranty in days, get the exclusions in writing, and get the return-freight responsibility named. LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty, which is one of the longer terms in the category; the useful follow-up question is what "limited" excludes and who pays shipping on a claim.

Terms you can hold them to

  • T/T 30/70 with L/C at sight accepted.
  • FOB Shenzhen, working with your nominated forwarder.
  • Samples in 7–14 days, sample fee refundable against the purchase order.

That sample order is itself the verification. You are testing whether the sample arrives in the promised window, whether it matches the written spec, and whether the fee is genuinely credited against the PO. Suppliers who fail at the sample stage rarely recover at the production stage.

How do I verify export history and client references?

Ask for the 10-digit customs registration code and match it against shipping data, and for client claims, a 20-minute reference call beats any logo wall.

Export records

  • US-bound trade — bill-of-lading and import data are commercially available through Panjiva, ImportYeti and ImportGenius. Search the supplier's legal name, not the brand, and look for shipments to importers in your category.
  • EU and Japan — shipping data is less open. Ask for a redacted bill of lading or a recent shipping instruction showing the supplier as shipper.
  • Consistency — the product descriptions on those shipments should match what you are being quoted. A supplier whose export record is all phone accessories and no wellness products is telling you something.

Client references

Legitimate suppliers usually cannot name clients freely; private-label and NDA arrangements make a public logo wall either invisible or, occasionally, invented. The productive question is not "who do you work with" but "will you arrange a 20-minute call with a non-competing brand you have shipped for". Then ask that reference five things: what the defect rate looked like, what happened when something went wrong, whether lead times held, how documentation was handled, and whether they re-ordered.

Because LIGHT BDB builds private-label programmes, the finished product carries the customer's brand rather than ours — which is exactly why the reference call matters more than the logo wall.

What are the limits of public-record verification?

Public records confirm identity, certification and export activity; they cannot confirm product quality, on-time delivery or whether your specific SKU will leave the factory correctly.

It is worth being blunt about this, because a clean registry check creates a false sense of safety. Here is what the method genuinely cannot do.

  • Registries lag. A certificate can be suspended after you check it and before you pay the deposit. Re-verify at the PO date, not just at the enquiry stage.
  • A UL registered factory is not a UL listed product. Factory registration covers a site. Your product needs its own listing file and number if a listing is what your market requires — those are separate claims and buyers routinely confuse them.
  • Social audits are not quality audits. A top BSCI grade tells you the site is well managed on labour and safety. It does not tell you the defect rate on a heated massager.
  • ISO 9001 certifies that a process exists, not that the process is good. Scope statements are usually broad enough to cover almost any product line.
  • Partner-operated plants complicate paper trails. When the plant is run by a strategic manufacturing partner, as at our Panyu site, site documents may sit under the partner's entity name. That is normal in Shenzhen manufacturing, but you should ask for the authorisation letter rather than assume.
  • Not everything is public. Brand approvals such as Sony Green Partner are typically confidential between the supplier and the brand. Absence of a public record is not proof of absence — but it does mean you should ask for the document and a verification contact.
  • Records describe the past. A supplier with ten good years and a new plant manager is a different risk from the one the records describe.

Use public records to eliminate liars and to confirm identity fast. Use samples, inspection and reference calls to decide whether you actually want to place the order. The two checks answer different questions, and you need both.

How do I check if a supplier's ISO 9001 certificate is real?

Ask for the full PDF certificate and read four fields: certificate number, issuing certification body, scope statement and expiry date. Then go directly to that certification body's public register and search the number yourself. If the record does not return the same legal entity name and the same site address, the certificate is not evidence of anything.

Can I check a Chinese supplier's business licence online?

Yes. The National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System at gsxt.gov.cn publishes the legal name, unified social credit code, registered capital, business scope and operating status of registered Chinese companies. Search the legal name printed on the licence, not the trade name on the website. The record is free and takes about ten minutes.

How do I tell a factory from a trading company in Shenzhen?

Compare the business scope on gsxt.gov.cn against the site address on the supplier's certificates, and ask for a live video walkthrough with location sharing on. A scope limited to sales and distribution, combined with certificates registered to a different company name, points to a trading operation. Ask directly who owns the plant and who employs the line staff.

How do I verify a BSCI audit report?

Ask the supplier to share the full report through the amfori platform and give you the report ID and audit date. Check that the audited entity and site address match the plant that will run your order, and check the grade, the zero-tolerance findings and the corrective action status. Audits are industry-typically valid for around 12 months, so confirm the date.

What does "UL registered factory" mean and what does it not mean?

It means the site appears in UL's directories as a registered manufacturing location. It does not mean your product is listed, certified or marked. Product listings are separate files with their own numbers, so if your market requires a listing, ask for that specific file number rather than the factory registration.

How do I check a supplier's export history?

Ask for the 10-digit customs registration code and search the supplier's legal name in bill-of-lading databases such as Panjiva or ImportYeti for US-bound trade. Look for shipments to importers in your own product category, not just any shipments. For EU and Japan, request a redacted bill of lading showing the supplier as shipper.

How do I verify a test report from SGS, Intertek or TÜV?

Use the lab's own report verification portal or contact its local office with the report number. Check that the report names your supplier's legal entity, that the tested model matches the SKU you are buying, and that CNAS, CMA or ILAC-MRA marks appear on the document. A report for a similar model is not a report for your model.

Why won't a supplier share its client list?

Most private-label and ODM programmes are covered by confidentiality agreements, so a supplier that names clients freely is either exaggerating or being careless with other people's information. Ask for a reference call with a non-competing brand instead. A supplier with real references can usually arrange one within a few days.
