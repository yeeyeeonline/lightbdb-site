A Three-Plant Manufacturing Chain, End to End: How Components, Assembly and Overflow Capacity Fit Together

Quick answer

LIGHT BDB runs one chain across three sites: a 10,000+ sqm component plant in Panyu, a 3,000 sqm finished-product assembly base in Shenzhen, and a 5,000+ pcs/day overflow line in Yangon. Components are built first, products assembled and tested second, overflow absorbed third — 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen.

  • Panyu component plant: 10,000+ sqm, 600+ staff, producing inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils and SMT boards.
  • The Panyu plant is operated by exclusive strategic manufacturing partner Fuli Electronics, not by a separate unrelated supplier.
  • Shenzhen assembly base: 3,000 sqm turning Panyu components into finished, tested, packed product.
  • Yangon overflow line: 5,000+ pcs/day, used to absorb demand spikes without pushing standard lead times out.
  • MOQ starts at 100 pcs on Light & Mood platforms and 500 pcs on Body care platforms.
  • Lead time is 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs.
  • The chain operates under ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory and Sony Green Partner status.
  • Finished product carries a 500-day limited warranty; samples ship in 7–14 days and the fee is refundable against your PO.

What does a three-plant manufacturing chain actually look like, plant by plant?

It is one chain with three distinct jobs: component manufacturing in Panyu, finished-product assembly in Shenzhen, and overflow volume in Yangon.

Most first-time importers picture a factory as one building with one gate. In practice, a wellness device like a heated neck massager or a red light panel is built in layers, and each layer has different equipment, different operators and different tolerances. Splitting those layers across sites is not a marketing device — it is how you get auto-grade component quality underneath a consumer-priced finished good.

Panyu: the component layer

The Panyu plant covers 10,000+ sqm with 600+ staff and is operated by LIGHT BDB's exclusive strategic manufacturing partner, Fuli Electronics. It produces auto-grade inductors, transformers, wireless charging coils and SMT assemblies. Those are the parts that decide whether a heated massager runs cool and quiet for 500 days or starts buzzing in month four.

Two things matter about this site for a brand owner. First, "auto-grade" is a component qualification standard, not a marketing word — it means parts screened to tighter tolerances and higher temperature ranges than typical consumer electronics sourcing. Second, because the plant is run by an exclusive partner rather than an open-market supplier, your coil and transformer supply is not being re-quoted against whoever bids lowest that week.

Shenzhen: the finished-product layer

The Shenzhen base is 3,000 sqm of finished-product assembly. This is where Panyu components, plus incoming mechanical parts, soft goods and packaging, become a sellable unit: sub-assembly, final assembly, functional test, aging checks, cosmetic inspection, retail packing and export documentation.

Keeping assembly in Shenzhen — close to the FOB port, close to the design and account teams — shortens the feedback loop when something needs fixing mid-run. A tolerance issue spotted on the line can be traced back to the Panyu component batch the same week.

Yangon: the overflow layer

The Yangon line handles 5,000+ pcs/day of overflow volume. It exists for one reason: demand spikes. Q4 gifting seasons, retail promo launches, a marketplace listing going viral — these are the moments when a single-site factory either pushes your lead time to 14 weeks or tells you no. A third site absorbs that pressure.

Which plant handles which part of my product?

Panyu builds the electromagnetic and electronic core, Shenzhen assembles and tests the finished unit, and Yangon absorbs overflow volume.

For a heated massager, the split looks like this: the wireless charging coil, the transformer and the control board SMT come out of Panyu; the motor mounts, heating element integration, shell assembly, firmware flashing and burn-in happen in Shenzhen. For a red light therapy panel, the driver board and coil work sit in Panyu while LED array assembly and optical test sit in Shenzhen. For light and mood products, the SMT driver and any wireless charging sit in Panyu, and the diffuser, housing and packing sit in Shenzhen.

Design happens before any plant touches metal

Industrial design, mechanical engineering and electronics engineering run out of two design centres — Hangzhou and Hamburg. Hamburg matters to EU brands because it puts a design contact in your time zone and your regulatory context; Hangzhou matters because it sits close enough to the Panyu and Shenzhen plants that a design change can be walked onto the line rather than emailed into a void.

Why the split is not arbitrary

  • Equipment mismatch. SMT lines and coil winding machines are capital-intensive and want steady utilisation; assembly benches are labour-intensive and want flexibility. Mixing them in one hall wastes both.
  • Yield control. Component defects get caught at source rather than discovered after the product is sealed.
  • Volume flexibility. A third site gives you a release valve that a single-building supplier cannot offer.

How does my order actually move through the chain, from deposit to FOB Shenzhen?

Your order moves through five stages — component build, sub-assembly, final assembly and test, packaging, then FOB export — inside 4–8 weeks.

  1. Sample and sign-off (7–14 days). Sample turnaround is 7–14 days, and the sample fee is refundable against your purchase order. This is where cosmetics, tolerances and packaging get locked.
  2. Deposit and component build. Standard terms are T/T 30/70, with L/C at sight accepted. Once the deposit lands, Panyu schedules the coils, transformers and SMT boards against your build.
  3. Sub-assembly and final assembly. Components move to Shenzhen and join incoming mechanical and soft-good parts on the assembly benches.
  4. Functional test and QA. Units run through functional checks, cosmetic inspection and packing-line QA before cartoning.
  5. Packaging and export. Retail packing, carton marking and export documentation are completed, then goods move FOB Shenzhen to your nominated forwarder.

The 4–8 week window is deposit-to-FOB for most SKUs. The wide band is honest rather than vague: a standard Light & Mood SKU in a repeat colourway sits near the short end, while an ODM custom build with new tooling, new packaging and first-article approval sits near the long end. Plan the peak season backwards from the long end, not the short one.

What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect at each stage of the chain?

MOQ is set by product platform, not by plant: Light & Mood starts at 100 pcs and Body care starts at 500 pcs.

Three plants do not mean three minimums. You place one order with one contract, and the tier is determined by how much customisation sits on top of the platform.

Light & Mood platforms

  • 100 pcs — standard platform, existing tooling, your logo applied within the existing design language.
  • 300 pcs — white-label, with your brand assets, packaging and documentation.
  • 500–1,000 pcs — ODM custom, where the housing, optics, electronics or feature set changes to your specification.

Body care platforms

  • 500 pcs — standard platform.
  • 1,000 pcs — white-label.
  • 2,000–3,000 pcs — ODM custom.

Body care tiers run higher for a physical reason. Heated massagers and comparable body-care devices carry more mechanical assembly steps, more thermal and safety-related test points, and more incoming-part variables than a light or mood product. Each of those steps costs setup time, and setup time is what MOQ actually pays for.

Practical advice for a first order: pick the standard tier on one platform, prove sell-through, then move to white-label on the reorder. Jumping straight to an ODM custom build at 2,000+ pcs before you have a single sell-through data point is the most common way first-time importers tie up cash.

How do certifications and audits work across three sites?

Certification is held at group level, so ask for the certificate scope in writing rather than assuming every site carries every mark.

The chain operates under ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI (amfori), UL registered factory and Sony Green Partner status. Those cover quality management, environmental management, social compliance and factory registration respectively — a combination that satisfies most EU and US retail vendor onboarding packs.

What to request before you sign

  • Current certificates with scope statements and expiry dates, not just logos on a website.
  • Written confirmation of which legal entity and which site appear on your purchase contract.
  • Your own audit or a third-party audit, scheduled through your account manager, with the site list agreed in advance.
  • For UL-related questions, the factory registration reference rather than a product claim.

What we do not hold — stated plainly

LIGHT BDB does not hold ISO 13485, and no product is FDA approved or FDA cleared. Our products are wellness devices, not medical devices. If your channel requires a medical-device classification, a registered quality management system or a regulatory clearance pathway, we are the wrong partner for that SKU and we will say so early rather than late. For red light panels, heated massagers and light and mood products sold as wellness goods, the certification set above is the relevant one.

Where does the money go in a three-plant chain?

Most of your unit cost sits in components and assembly labour; a three-plant chain adds coordination cost, not a second profit layer.

Industry-typical BOM structures in this category put components and materials at roughly 40–60% of FOB unit cost, assembly labour at roughly 15–25%, packaging at roughly 5–10%, with testing, overhead and margin making up the balance. Treat those as category ranges to benchmark against, not as a quoted breakdown — ask for a line-item cost sheet for your specific SKU and compare it to those bands.

Three plants, one invoice

You are not buying from three suppliers. You contract with LIGHT BRAND DESIGN BUSINESS (SHENZHEN) CO., LTD., receive one set of terms, one invoice and one shipment. The internal transfer between Panyu, Shenzhen and Yangon is our coordination problem, not a line item you reconcile.

Where a three-plant chain genuinely reduces cost

  • Component margin. Coils, transformers and SMT built in-house rather than bought from a broker removes a markup layer.
  • Rework cost. Defects caught at the component stage are cheaper than defects caught after final assembly.
  • Peak-season premium. Overflow capacity you already have beats expedited production you have to beg for in October.

Where it costs more

Coordination is real work: engineering change control across sites, cross-site quality documentation, extra inbound logistics between Panyu and Shenzhen, and a heavier project-management load. That cost exists in the price. A supplier claiming three sites at single-site pricing is either not running three sites or is hiding the difference somewhere in the BOM.

What are the honest trade-offs and risks of a three-plant chain?

The main trade-offs are coordination overhead, a longer change-control loop, and capacity you have to book early rather than assume.

A three-plant chain is the right structure for a brand planning repeat orders and peak seasons. It is the wrong structure for someone who wants a 200-piece test run in three weeks with a design change halfway through. Here is the honest ledger.

Where a three-plant chain is the better fit

  • You are building a product line, not testing a one-off idea — repeat orders are the point.
  • Your volumes land in the hundreds to low thousands per SKU per cycle.
  • You need peak-season capacity without a 14-week lead time.
  • You want component quality controlled at source rather than assembled from broker parts.
  • You value a 500-day limited warranty, which is one of the longer coverage windows in this category.

Where it is the weaker choice

  • Very small first runs. Below the standard tier on your platform, the setup economics stop working and a broker-led supplier will beat us on price.
  • Fast iteration. A design change touching both a Panyu component and a Shenzhen assembly step has to be validated at both ends. That loop is longer than a single-building factory's.
  • Cross-border complexity. The Yangon line adds transit and documentation steps to any order routed through it. It is a capacity tool, not a default routing.
  • Capacity is shared. Panyu is run by our exclusive strategic partner, which means capacity is committed across multiple programmes. Q4 volume needs to be booked in Q2, not requested in September.
  • Warranty is priced in. A 500-day limited warranty is a genuine differentiator and it costs money to stand behind. Do not expect the absolute lowest FOB quote in the category alongside it.

None of these are disqualifying. They are the terms of the deal, and you should hear them before the first purchase order rather than after.
