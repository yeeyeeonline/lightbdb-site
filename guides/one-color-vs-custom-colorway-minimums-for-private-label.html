What is the difference between a one-color order and a custom colorway order?

A one-color order uses a factory-standard color already in production, while a custom colorway requires Pantone matching, new tooling or mold adjustments, and a higher minimum quantity to justify the setup costs. The core difference is cost per unit versus setup cost: standard colors spread no extra tooling expense, while custom colors require amortizing mold changes and color formulation across a larger batch.

For a private label brand, the choice affects your cash outlay, lead time, and brand differentiation. A one-color order gets you to market faster with lower risk. A custom colorway gives you shelf distinction but demands a larger commitment. Most first-time importers start with a standard color to validate demand, then move to custom colors once sales data supports a bigger order.

What counts as a "standard" color?

Standard colors are the shades a factory already runs regularly, such as white, black, grey, or a signature product color. These require no new tooling, no color formulation, and no additional lead time. At LIGHT BDB, standard colors are available at the Lite tier (100–499 units), which is designed for sampling and limited product drops.

What counts as a "custom" colorway?

A custom colorway is any shade that is not already in the factory's production palette. This includes Pantone-matched colors, dual-tone combinations, soft-touch matte finishes, or metallic effects. Custom colorways require color formulation, test panels, and often mold surface adjustments, which is why they demand a higher MOQ.

What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect for a standard color order?

A first-time buyer should expect a standard color MOQ of 100–500 units for most wellness devices, with 500–999 units being the sweet spot for cost efficiency. LIGHT BDB's Lite tier starts at 100 units, which is unusually low for the industry, and the Slim tier at 500–999 units offers the best balance of cost and flexibility.

At 100 units, you are paying a higher per-unit cost because fixed costs like assembly setup and quality control are spread across fewer pieces. At 500 units, the per-unit cost drops noticeably. At 1,000+ units, you hit the Pro tier where unit costs are lowest and full customization becomes available.

For context, most factories in this category require 500–1,000 units even for standard colors. LIGHT BDB's 100-unit entry point is designed for brands that want to test a product before committing to a larger run.

What MOQ is required for a fully custom colorway?

A fully custom colorway at LIGHT BDB requires the Pro tier of 1,000+ units, which is the industry-typical threshold for Pantone-matched colors on injection-molded wellness devices. Below 1,000 units, the setup cost for color formulation and mold adjustments cannot be amortized without making the unit price prohibitive.

The 1,000-unit threshold covers several fixed costs:

  • Color formulation and Pantone matching (typically 3–5 iterations before approval).
  • Test panels and color chips sent for your approval before production.
  • Mold surface adjustments if the custom color requires a different texture or finish.
  • Additional QC checkpoints to ensure color consistency across the batch.
  • Potential re-tooling of the injection mold if the color requires a different gate position.

If you want a custom colorway at a lower volume, expect to pay a significant premium per unit, often 20–40% above standard color pricing, which is industry-typical. The alternative is to run a limited drop in a standard color first, then commit to a custom colorway once you have sales data.

While exact pricing depends on the product, industry-typical cost differences are: moving from Lite to Slim reduces per-unit cost by roughly 15–25%, and moving from Slim to Pro reduces it by another 10–20%. These are estimates based on common manufacturing economics, not LIGHT BDB's specific price list. Your actual pricing will depend on the product category, component costs, and assembly complexity.

What is the lead time for standard colors versus custom colorways?

Standard colors ship in 4–8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen, while custom colorways add approximately 1–2 weeks for color matching and approval. LIGHT BDB's standard lead time is 4–8 weeks for most SKUs, and the additional time for custom colors is industry-typical for injection-molded products.

The custom color process adds these steps:

  1. Color formulation and Pantone matching (3–5 days).
  2. Test panel production and shipping to you for approval (5–7 days).
  3. Your approval or revision feedback (2–5 days, depending on your response speed).
  4. Mold adjustments if required (3–7 days).
  5. Production ramp-up with new color parameters (2–3 days).

If you are on a tight deadline for a seasonal launch, a standard color is the safer choice. If brand differentiation is critical and you have lead time flexibility, a custom colorway is worth the extra weeks.

How does sampling work for color decisions?

LIGHT BDB offers a 7-day sample turnaround, and the sample fee is refundable against your purchase order, so you can evaluate a standard color before committing to a full run. For custom colors, the sample process is slightly different because the color does not exist yet.

For standard colors, request a production sample to verify the exact shade, finish, and texture. For custom colors, you will typically go through a two-step process:

  • Step 1: Color chips or small molded panels in your requested Pantone shade (3–5 days).
  • Step 2: A full sample unit in the custom color once the formulation is approved (additional 5–7 days).

Budget for at least two rounds of color approval for custom shades. The first round gets you close, and the second round fine-tunes the match. This is normal in the industry, not a sign of poor quality.

What are the honest trade-offs between one-color and custom colorway orders?

The honest trade-off is that custom colorways cost more, take longer, and require a larger commitment, but they deliver brand distinction that standard colors cannot. The risk is that you commit to 1,000+ units of a color that does not sell, leaving you with dead inventory in a shade you cannot easily offload.

Standard colors carry the opposite risk: your product looks like every other white or black device on the shelf, and you compete on price rather than brand identity. For most first-time importers, the smart move is to launch in a standard color, validate demand, and then invest in a custom colorway for your second or third production run.

Another trade-off to consider is flexibility. A standard color order at the Lite tier (100–499 units) lets you test multiple product variations with minimal cash outlay. A custom colorway locks you into a single shade across a larger volume. If you are unsure which color resonates with your audience, run two standard colors at lower volumes before committing to a custom shade.

Finally, consider your exit strategy. Standard colors are easier to sell through discount channels if a product underperforms. Custom colors are harder to liquidate because the market for a specific shade is narrower. This is a practical consideration that many brands overlook until they are stuck with inventory.

How do I decide which option is right for my brand?

Choose a standard color if you are launching your first product, testing a new category, or working with a budget under $10,000–$15,000 for your initial run. Choose a custom colorway if you have an established audience, validated demand, and a budget that supports 1,000+ units without straining your cash flow.

Ask yourself these questions before deciding:

  • Do I have sales data or pre-orders that justify a 1,000+ unit commitment?
  • Is my brand identity dependent on a specific color that competitors cannot easily copy?
  • Can I absorb the extra 1–2 weeks of lead time for custom color matching?
  • Do I have a plan to liquidate inventory if the color underperforms?
  • Am I confident enough in the product-market fit to commit to a larger run?

If you answered "no" to any of these, start with a standard color at the Lite or Slim tier. LIGHT BDB's 100-unit entry point exists specifically to let you test without overcommitting. Once you have real sales data, the Pro tier and custom colorways will still be available for your next run.
