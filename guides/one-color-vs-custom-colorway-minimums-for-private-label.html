One Color vs Custom Colorway Minimums for Private Label

Quick answer

A single standard color starts at 100-499 units (LIGHT BDB's Lite tier), while a fully custom colorway — your own Pantone match and finish — requires 1,000+ units (Pro tier) for the best unit cost. Most private-label buyers land in the Slim tier at 500-999 units.

  • LIGHT BDB MOQ tiers: Lite 100-499, Slim 500-999, Pro 1,000+ units.
  • Full customization with custom colorways starts at 1,000+ units in the Pro tier.
  • One standard color can be ordered from just 100 units for sampling or limited drops.
  • Lead time is 4-8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen for most SKUs.
  • Sample turnaround is 7 days; the sample fee is refundable against your PO.
  • Every LIGHT BDB product carries a 500-day limited warranty, among the longest in the category.
  • Trade terms: T/T 30/70 or L/C at sight; FOB Shenzhen with your nominated forwarder.

This guide is horizontal — it applies whether you are sourcing a heated massager, a red light panel, or a light & mood home product. The colorway decision is usually the first fork in your sourcing plan, and it determines your minimum order, your lead time, and your unit cost.

What MOQ should a first-time buyer expect for a single color?

For a single standard color, LIGHT BDB's Lite tier starts at 100-499 units — enough for sampling, a limited drop, or proving a new SKU.

If this is your first import, the Lite tier is your safest entry point. You are using existing tooling and a standard finish, which keeps risk low and time-to-market short. A 100-unit run lets you test retail appetite, validate carton dimensions, and confirm packaging design without betting the season's budget. Most first-time buyers in this product category find 200-300 units in one color feels right for a first order — you confirm the basics, then scale.

When a single color makes sense

One color is the right call when you are testing a new category, launching a seasonal drop, or working with a tight first budget. It also suits buyers selling through one channel — a marketplace listing or a small web shop — where color variety matters less than price and stock availability.

When to stretch to the Slim tier

Once you know the product sells, the Slim tier (500-999 units) — LIGHT BDB's most popular bracket — gives you room for a second color option or light differentiation while keeping unit costs reasonable. For a heated eye massager or a red light panel, 500-999 units is typically enough to cover two to three color variants in your first shelf-ready batch.

What MOQ applies when you want a fully custom colorway?

A fully custom colorway — your own Pantone match, brand-specific finish, or two-tone treatment — requires LIGHT BDB's Pro tier at 1,000+ units, which also unlocks the best unit cost.

"Custom colorway" is more than picking a nicer shade. It means the factory mixes your specific color, applies your finish (matte, gloss, soft-touch), and often adjusts tooling surface or texture so the color reads correctly on the final product. That setup work and batch consistency don't make economic sense at small volumes. LIGHT BDB positions custom colorway work in the Pro tier deliberately: 1,000+ units is where the per-unit economics work for both sides.

When you order a custom colorway, provide a Pantone reference or a physical sample chip. LIGHT BDB's design centers in Hangzhou and Hamburg can help you shortlist finishes that hold well on the product's material — useful before you commit to a color that may not survive a soft-touch surface or repeated heat exposure in a massager.

What lead time should you plan for custom colorway development?

LIGHT BDB's standard lead time is 4-8 weeks from deposit to FOB Shenzhen, with a custom colorway typically adding an extra 1-3 weeks for color matching and approval.

Your 4-8 week window covers production and assembly after the deposit clears. Custom colorwork adds steps you don't see with stock colors: color matching iterations and a first-article approval pass. Industry-typical is 1-3 additional weeks depending on finish — soft-touch and two-tone finishes usually sit at the longer end.

Two tools keep the timeline honest:

  • Order a sample first. LIGHT BDB's sample turnaround is 7 days, and the sample fee is refundable against your PO, so the color approval loop starts before production.
  • Build buffer. Plan 6-10 weeks total from deposit to dock when a custom colorway is involved.

What does a custom colorway cost beyond the unit price?

Expect a per-unit premium over stock color — industry-typical is 5-15% depending on finish complexity, volume, and the number of color stops in the design.

Three drivers sit behind that premium:

Material and paint sourcing

Custom colors mean the factory buys paint and masterbatch in your specific shade. Below a certain volume, that color batch cost spreads across too few units — which is exactly why most factories, LIGHT BDB included, move custom colorway work to the higher tier.

Tooling and finish adjustment

Texture, gloss level, or two-tone contrast can require tooling surface changes or an extra production pass. That adds setup labor and slows the line. Two-tone designs are the most expensive per unit because they need two molding or painting steps.

Quality and rework allowance

Custom color runs carry a slightly higher defect risk because the color recipe is new. Factories build a rework allowance into your price; a stock color that has run for years is far less likely to drift between batches.

What are the honest trade-offs between one-color and custom colorway?

One-color gives you speed, lower minimums, and lower risk; a custom colorway gives you shelf presence and brand identity but demands 1,000+ units and a per-unit premium.

Weigh these trade-offs before deciding:

  • Speed vs identity. Stock color ships in 4-8 weeks; custom color adds matching time and a first-article approval step.
  • Volume vs presence. 200 units in stock color lets you test a market; 1,000 units in custom color lets you own a shelf.
  • Cost per unit vs retail value. Custom color costs more, but a distinctive colorway can justify a higher retail price and better sell-through.
  • Risk vs control. Custom colorways add variables — color drift between batches, finish defects, longer rework. LIGHT BDB's 500-day warranty and sample-first workflow keep those manageable, but they are real.
