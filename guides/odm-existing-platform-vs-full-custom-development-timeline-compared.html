What is the actual timeline difference between ODM existing platform and full custom development?

ODM existing platform ships in 4-8 weeks from deposit, while full custom development typically takes 16-32 weeks from concept to FOB. The gap comes down to tooling, compliance retesting, and iterative prototyping that only custom work requires.

When you work with an ODM like LIGHT BDB, you are selecting from pre-engineered platforms that have already been through tooling, production validation, and compliance screening. The engineering risk is retired. Your job is to choose the platform, select options like heating elements or light wavelengths, and apply your brand.

Full custom development means every component, every mold, and every assembly step is new. That is not inherently bad — it is how you get a truly differentiated product — but it carries a timeline cost that most first-time importers underestimate.

Typical ODM platform timeline breakdown

  • Week 1: Platform selection, specification confirmation, and sample request.
  • Week 2: Sample shipped (LIGHT BDB standard is 7-day sample turnaround).
  • Week 3-4: Branding, packaging artwork, and compliance documentation review.
  • Week 5-8: Production run, QC inspection, and FOB Shenzhen shipment.

Typical full custom development timeline breakdown

  • Week 1-4: Industrial design, mechanical engineering, and component sourcing.
  • Week 5-10: Prototyping, user testing, and design iteration (usually 2-3 rounds).
  • Week 11-16: Injection mold tooling and fixture fabrication.
  • Week 17-20: Pilot production, defect correction, and compliance testing.
  • Week 21-28: Full production, QC, and shipping.

How much faster is the ODM route for a first-time wellness device buyer?

For a first-time buyer, the ODM route is typically 12-24 weeks faster than full custom development, which is the difference between launching this season and launching next year. First-time importers also avoid the learning curve of managing tooling vendors and engineering revisions remotely.

The speed advantage is not just about calendar days. An ODM platform means the factory has already solved the problems that eat weeks: part fit, thermal management, battery safety, and assembly line balancing. When LIGHT BDB quotes 4-8 weeks, that timeline is credible because the product has been built before.

Full custom development for a first-time buyer often stretches beyond the 16-week minimum because of communication latency, design revisions, and the back-and-forth of approving 3D files across time zones. Experienced buyers with clear specifications can compress custom timelines, but first-timers rarely do.

What MOQ differences exist between ODM existing platform and full custom development?

ODM existing platform MOQs start at 100-499 units (LIGHT BDB Lite tier), while full custom development typically requires 1,000+ units to amortize tooling costs. The custom route's higher MOQ is a direct consequence of mold and fixture investments that must be spread across units.

LIGHT BDB's MOQ tiers are structured to match buyer maturity:

  • Lite (100-499 units): Ideal for sampling, limited drops, and market testing. You pay a slightly higher unit cost but carry minimal inventory risk.
  • Slim (500-999 units): The most popular tier. Balances unit cost against inventory commitment, suitable for a first production run with real retail intent.
  • Pro (1,000+ units): Best unit cost and access to full customization options. This tier is where you can request custom colors, materials, or feature changes.

Full custom development almost always lands in the Pro tier or above, because the tooling investment of $5,000-$30,000 per mold needs volume to justify it. At 1,000 units, a $10,000 mold adds $10 per unit — at 500 units it adds $20.

What are the cost trade-offs between ODM existing platform and full custom development?

ODM existing platform typically costs 40-60% less in upfront engineering and tooling, but full custom development can yield a lower per-unit cost at high volumes. The breakeven point is usually between 2,000-5,000 units depending on complexity.

Cost categories to compare:

  • Tooling: ODM platform has zero or minimal tooling cost (molds already exist). Full custom costs $5,000-$30,000 per injection mold.
  • Engineering: ODM platform includes engineering support in the unit price. Full custom requires NRE (non-recurring engineering) fees of $3,000-$20,000.
  • Unit cost: ODM platform units cost 10-30% more than a fully optimized custom design at the same volume, because the platform is a compromise design.
  • Compliance: ODM platform products often share pre-existing test reports. Full custom requires new testing per market.
  • Inventory risk: ODM Lite tier (100-499 units) lets you test demand with minimal exposure. Custom development forces a larger first order.

LIGHT BDB's trade terms support both routes: T/T 30/70 and L/C at sight are accepted, with FOB Shenzhen and your nominated forwarder. The 500-day limited warranty applies to platform products, which is meaningful coverage for a first-time buyer establishing a brand reputation.

When does full custom development actually make sense over an ODM platform?

Full custom development makes sense when you have a proprietary technology, a clear volume forecast above 5,000 units per year, or a design that cannot be expressed through platform options. Otherwise, the ODM route is the rational choice for a first-time importer.

Signals that you should go custom:

  • You hold a patent or trade secret that requires a unique mechanical or electrical design.
  • Your retail channel has committed to a volume that justifies tooling amortization.
  • Your brand identity demands a form factor that no existing platform can approximate.
  • You have an engineering team or consultant who can manage the custom development process.

Signals that you should stay on the ODM platform:

  • You are testing a new category or market with uncertain demand.
  • Your differentiation can come from branding, packaging, and marketing rather than hardware.
  • You need to launch within a quarter to hit a retail or crowdfunding deadline.
  • Your budget prioritizes cash flow over per-unit optimization.

What are the honest trade-offs and risks of the ODM platform route?

The ODM platform route risks product commoditization, limited differentiation, and dependency on a single factory's roadmap. These are real constraints, and a good partner will name them before you sign.

Three trade-offs to weigh:

  • Differentiation ceiling: Your competitor can order the same platform. Mitigate this through branding, packaging, software, or accessory bundles — but know that the hardware itself may not be unique.
  • Feature lock-in: You can customize within the platform's limits (colors, materials, features in the Pro tier), but you cannot redesign the core architecture. If the platform lacks a feature your market demands, you are stuck or forced into custom work anyway.
  • Roadmap dependency: Your product lifecycle is tied to the factory's willingness to maintain and update the platform. A factory that abandons a platform leaves you with a dead SKU. This is why working with a factory with multiple plants and stable certifications matters — LIGHT BDB operates a Panyu component plant (10,000+ sqm, 600+ staff), a Shenzhen assembly facility (3,000 sqm), and a Yangon overflow line (5,000+ pcs/day), which provides production redundancy.

There is also a subtle risk in the custom route that buyers often miss: a custom product that fails in market leaves you with worthless tooling and a large inventory write-off. The ODM route's lower MOQ tiers are a form of insurance — you can test at 100-499 units before committing to a 1,000+ unit custom program.

How should a buyer decide between ODM existing platform and full custom development?

Choose ODM if your timeline is under 12 weeks, your budget for engineering is under $10,000, or your volume forecast is below 2,000 units. Choose custom if you have proprietary technology, a volume forecast above 5,000 units, or a design that cannot be approximated by a platform.

A practical decision framework:

  1. Define your launch deadline. If it is inside 12 weeks, ODM is your only realistic option.
  2. Estimate your first-order volume. Under 1,000 units, custom tooling costs will crush your margin.
  3. Assess your differentiation strategy. If branding and marketing carry your brand, ODM suffices.
  4. Request samples from 2-3 ODM factories. LIGHT BDB's 7-day sample turnaround lets you evaluate quickly; the sample fee is refundable against your PO.
  5. If you choose custom, budget 2x your timeline estimate. First-time custom programs rarely come in on schedule.

Most first-time buyers should start with the ODM platform at the Slim tier (500-999 units), validate demand, and then consider a custom program for their second or third generation. This sequencing preserves cash, builds market knowledge, and gives you data to justify a larger custom investment later.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get samples from an ODM factory?

Most established ODM factories, including LIGHT BDB, offer a 7-day sample turnaround. The sample fee is typically refundable against your production order. This lets you evaluate quality, feel, and branding before committing to a full production run.

What is the minimum order quantity for a private label wellness device?

ODM platforms typically start at 100-499 units for a Lite tier, which suits sampling and limited drops. The most popular tier is 500-999 units, balancing unit cost and inventory risk. Full custom development usually requires 1,000+ units to justify tooling costs.

How much does it cost to develop a custom wellness device from scratch?

Full custom development typically costs $8,000-$50,000 in upfront engineering and tooling, depending on complexity. This includes industrial design, prototyping, injection molds, and compliance testing. ODM platforms avoid most of this cost because the tooling already exists.

What certifications should a wellness device factory have?

Look for ISO 9001 for quality management, ISO 14001 for environmental management, and BSCI (amfori) for social compliance. A UL registered factory and Sony Green Partner status indicate stronger compliance infrastructure. Note that wellness devices are not medical devices — do not expect or require ISO 13485 or FDA approval.

Can I customize an ODM platform product?

Yes, within limits. Most ODM factories offer customization tiers: colors, materials, branding, packaging, and some feature options. At higher volumes (typically 1,000+ units), more customization becomes available. Full architectural changes require moving to a custom development program.

What is the typical lead time for ODM production?

Most ODM factories quote 4-8 weeks from deposit to FOB shipping for standard platform products. This includes production, quality control, and logistics preparation. Custom development programs typically take 16-32 weeks from concept to shipping.

What warranty should I expect on private label wellness devices?

Industry-standard warranties range from 90 to 365 days. LIGHT BDB offers a 500-day limited warranty, which is among the longest in the category. A longer warranty signals factory confidence in product reliability and reduces your after-sales risk.

What payment terms do ODM factories typically offer?

Common terms are T/T 30/70 (30% deposit, 70% before shipment) or L/C at sight for larger orders. Most factories ship FOB from their port, and you can use your nominated forwarder. Confirm payment terms and Incoterms in writing before placing an order.

Is it better to start with ODM or go straight to custom development?

For first-time importers, starting with an ODM platform is usually smarter. It gets you to market in 4-8 weeks, tests demand with lower MOQs, and avoids $8,000-$50,000 in upfront tooling costs. You can always move to custom development for your second generation once you have sales data.

How do I know if a factory is reliable for private label production?

Check for third-party certifications (ISO 9001, ISO 14001, BSCI), request samples before committing, and verify production capacity across multiple facilities. A factory with component-level manufacturing, assembly operations, and overflow capacity — like LIGHT BDB's Panyu, Shenzhen, and Yangon plants — offers more supply chain resilience than a single-site operation.
