NIR vs Red Light vs Full Spectrum: What to Put on Your Wellness Device Box

Quick answer

For a private label wellness device, the most legally defensible and consumer-honest approach is to put the exact peak wavelengths (e.g., 630nm, 660nm, 850nm) and irradiance (mW/cm²) on the box instead of the vague marketing term "full spectrum." Full spectrum implies a regulatory standard that does not exist in the US or EU.

  • Red light (630-660nm) absorbs in the skin's surface layers; NIR (810-850nm) penetrates deeper into muscle and joint tissue.
  • "Full spectrum" in this category is a marketing label, not a regulated technical standard—you must list the actual nanometer wavelengths.

  • A 7-day sample turnaround allows you to physically verify your "box claims" before you commit to mass production.

What does NIR do differently from red light in a wellness device?

Red light (630-660nm) targets the skin's surface layers for soothing and relaxation, while NIR (810-850nm) penetrates several centimeters deeper into muscle tissue for deep-tissue recovery.

If you are sourcing a heated massager or a light therapy panel, you are technically choosing between two entirely different electromagnetic penetration depths. Red light is visible to the human eye—it appears as a bright red glow. NIR is invisible; it is perceived only as heat on the skin. This is why your packaging cannot simply say "Infrared." You must specify which subset you are using.

  • Visible Red (630-660nm): Targets superficial layers of the skin. Often used in facial devices or low-penetration muscle pads.
  • Near-Infrared (810-850nm): Passes through the skin and reaches muscle and joint capsules. Common in larger panels and deep-tissue massagers.
  • Combination: Unless you state otherwise, "full spectrum" or "combo" devices simply mix these two into one LED array, which requires different driver boards and thermal management.

Most LIGHT BDB wellness devices integrate both visible red and NIR. Because LIGHT BDB manufactures its own auto-grade inductors, transformers, and SMT boards in its 10,000+ sqm Panyu component plant, wiring these wavelength combinations into a single device is a standard manufacturing capability.

What is full spectrum light therapy, and should I offer it as my flagship SKU?

"Full spectrum" is a consumer-friendly marketing umbrella that usually means a device emits a blend of visible red, NIR, and sometimes other wavelengths like amber or blue, but it has zero basis in regulatory standards.

In the sourcing world, whether you can claim "full spectrum" depends entirely on the physical LED configuration. If you want this to be your flagship SKU, you must understand the trade-off: you are paying for a more complex driver board, higher thermal output, and a denser LED array to deliver the "combo" effect. The benefit is a premium price point and a simple story for your customer.

When to choose a Full Spectrum Flagship

  • You want a single SKU that answers your customer's primary question: "Which one do I need?"
  • You are selling to athletes or wellness enthusiasts who expect deep recovery plus skin benefits.
  • You are comfortable printing "Wavelengths: 630nm, 660nm, 850nm" directly on the box to make the term "full spectrum" honest and specific.

For a first-time importer, LIGHT BDB's Slim tier (500-999 units) is the most popular way to test a full spectrum panel because it offers enough volume to hit a reasonable unit cost without requiring a massive inventory commitment of 1,000+ units.

Which wavelengths should I put on my product box and spec sheet?

You should put the exact peak wavelengths (e.g., 630nm, 660nm, and 850nm) and the irradiance (milliwatts per square centimeter, mW/cm²) measured at a standard distance directly on your spec sheet and retail box.

Putting exact wavelengths on the box accomplishes three objectives: it protects you legally, it sets accurate consumer expectations, and it dramatically reduces return rates. A customer who sees "NIR 850nm" knows they are buying a deep-tissue device. A customer who just sees "Full Spectrum" may assume it does something it does not.

Mandatory elements for your "Tech Spec" block:

  1. Peak Wavelength(s): List explicit nanometers for each LED color (e.g., 630nm ± 5nm).
  2. Irradiance: e.g., "Up to 100 mW/cm² at 6 inches." This is a metric the FTC understands here, and it gives buyers a verifiable number.
  3. Total LEDs: e.g., "50 Dual-Chip LEDs (25 Red / 25 NIR)."
  4. Input Voltage: i.e., 100-240V AC for global compatibility.
  5. Power Consumption: e.g., "120W Max."
  6. Legal Name & Origin: "LIGHT BRAND DESIGN BUSINESS (SHENZHEN) CO., LTD." as the manufacturer for EU GPSR traceability.

In our experience, brands that put the explicit wavelength breakdown on the box immediately signal to the market that they are a professional operation, not a generic white-label reseller.

What are the labeling rules for light therapy devices in the US, EU, and Japan for private label importers?

You cannot label your device as a medical device, FDA-approved, or ISO 13485 certified because LIGHT BDB does not hold ISO 13485, and no LIGHT BDB product is FDA approved—making such a claim violates the US FTC Act and comparable EU/JP consumer laws.

Here is the reality of what you *can* claim: You are selling a General Wellness Product. The US FDA allows structure/function claims that do not reference a specific disease (e.g., "promotes relaxation," "supports muscle recovery after exercise," "provides soothing warmth"). You cannot say "treats arthritis" or "cures chronic pain." The EU GPSR requires you to place an EU Responsible Person and the manufacturer's legal entity on the box—this is where you list LIGHT BDB's legal address. Japan requires compliance with the Electrical Appliance and Material Safety Law (PSE) for any plug-in device.

Box compliance checklist:

  • Include the legal manufacturer name and full corporate address: LIGHT BRAND DESIGN BUSINESS (SHENZHEN) CO., LTD.
  • Include a clearly printed "General Wellness Device — Not a Medical Device" disclaimer if you are targeting the US market.
  • List exact nm wavelengths and power consumption to satisfy EU GPSR technical documentation norms.
  • Never include the words "treats," "cures," "diagnoses," or "prevents" on your packaging, sales page, or inserts.
  • LIGHT BDB is a UL registered factory and holds ISO 9001, ISO 14001, and BSCI—these are factory-level compliance claims you *can* print.

What are the honest trade-offs between marketing full spectrum vs. single-wavelength devices?

Honest trade-offs: full spectrum devices offer superior marketing appeal but require dense LED arrays and higher power supplies, which increases your unit cost; single-wavelength devices are cheaper and easier to source, but face more competition at retail.

Do not let a factory push you into a "full spectrum" SKU just because it features a more complex LED board. From a supply-chain perspective, a single-wavelength device is simpler to manufacture, carries lower warranty risk, and allows for sharper product messaging. A full spectrum device is a premium, higher-margin product, but it demands rigorous thermal management—if the driver board fails from heat, your 500-day warranty becomes a logistical headache.

Our balanced advice for a new brand owner is to launch your first production run with a Red + NIR combo (which you can honestly label as "Dual-Wavelength" instead of the vague "Full Spectrum"). This gives you the premium price point while keeping the engineering footprint predictable. Once you sell through your first 1,000 units and understand the return rate, you can confidently expand into a proprietary full spectrum array in the Pro tier.
