MOQ 500 explained: where that number comes from

Almost every wellness-device supplier quotes a 500-piece minimum, and almost nobody explains it. Once you can see the fixed costs behind the number, you can plan a first order that does not over-buy — and you know which parts are genuinely negotiable.

Key Facts

  • Why 500: most of a small run's cost is fixed — line setup and changeover, component purchase minimums (heating film, PCB, batteries), tooling amortisation, test samples, packing labour.
  • Per model, not per order: MOQ normally applies per model and per colourway, because each variation triggers its own setup and materials.
  • Below MOQ: handled as sample or pilot orders at a higher unit price, typically with the buyer covering express freight.
  • Private tooling raises the bar: new moulds must be amortised, so MOQ rises — unless part of the tooling is paid up front or an existing ODM platform is used.
  • LightBDB: production MOQ 500 pcs per model; samples in about 7 days; proposal with 3D rendering and quotation within 24 hours.

The five costs that create the minimum

1 · Line setup and changeover

Clearing the previous job, loading programmes, calibrating fixtures and running first-article checks. Whether the run is 200 or 5,000 units, this cost is essentially the same.

2 · Component purchase minimums

Heating film, PCBs, battery cells and fabric all come with their own supplier minimums and lead times. Ordering below them means paying a premium or holding dead stock.

3 · Tooling amortisation

New moulds, jigs and test fixtures are spread across the run. Shorter runs mean each unit carries more tooling cost — this is why private-mould projects need higher volumes.

4 · Test and certification samples

Units pulled for safety testing, cycle testing and certification are consumed, not sold. On a small run that allocation is proportionally expensive.

5 · Packing and documentation labour

Manuals, labels, barcodes and market-specific inserts are per-model work, largely independent of quantity on small runs.

How to plan a first order

Start on an existing platform

Use an ODM model for the first run. You learn the real market response without carrying tooling risk, then move to a private mould on run two or three.

Test with samples before committing

Sampling takes about a week. Run the samples the way your customers will — full battery cycles, repeated heating cycles, and a drop test on the packaging.

Keep the first assortment narrow

One model, one or two colours, standard packaging. Depth beats breadth on a first order; breadth comes once the hero SKU is proven.

Plan the second order before the first ships

Component lead times, not factory capacity, are usually what delay a re-order. Agree the re-order window while the first run is still in production.
